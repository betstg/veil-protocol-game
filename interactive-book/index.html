The Campaign in One Paragraph

Obon week in Tokyo. Rei Uedera, 19, a suppressed Arbiter-class medium, wakes at 3:47 AM to find his older brother Gin — a CVD field investigator — gone. Not dead. Gone in a stranger way. Searching for Gin pulls Rei across the threshold into the hidden world: a war between two demons, a thinning membrane over Kita district, thirteen psychic domains in varying states of collapse, and the slow revelation that Gin is not missing but possessed — by Yushiro, the Duty demon, who is himself hunting the thing that got there first.

Every road Rei can take — through the Directorate, through Itsuki and the Kuroda house, through the unregistered Grid, through his own forbidden sight, or on foot through the city — leads to the same final night. What changes is what he carries into it, and what it costs.

Five Roads

Every road branches from the same 3:47 AM kitchen and converges back into Book Two. Each takes a different approach to the same problem. All 8 beats per road are authored with full prose and three choices.

The Five Endings

Three variables. Five outcomes. The combinations are not symmetrical — some endings are harder to reach, some require specific road choices to set up their preconditions.

The Three Variables

Variable One
Gin's Window
Whether Rei reaches Gin before the lucid window closes. Set by road choices in Books One through Three, especially the convergence beat on each road.
Variable Two
Domain IX Stability
Whether Rei's interventions across side missions and road beats stabilize the Kita membrane. Affected by the Watanabe Jun vignette (Domain VI) and the Thirteen-holder sub-spine.
Variable Three
The Domain XIII Choice
Chapter Ten, Book Four. Accept or refuse the succession to the vacant Duty seat. This choice is only available after the two-demon trap resolves and Yushiro has discharged his interrupted duty.
ENDING ONE
The Continuation
Window reached Domain IX stabilized Accept Domain XIII

Rei reaches Gin. The exorcism succeeds. Domain IX holds through Rei's intervention. The Axis offers Domain XIII and Rei accepts. He becomes the holder for Duty — living an ordinary life in Tokyo while channeling something vast. The archive gets a third author. Gin files retirement papers. Tanizaki makes tea and says nothing about any of it.

The fullest resolution. Requires the highest investment across all three tracks — the road convergence beat, the domain sub-spine, and the Book Four choice. Nothing is lost. Something irreversible is chosen willingly.
ENDING TWO
The Refusal
Window reached Domain IX stabilized Refuse Domain XIII

Rei reaches Gin. The exorcism succeeds. Domain IX stabilizes. The Axis offers Domain XIII and Rei refuses. The domain remains vacant. The organized entity pressure does not resolve — it pauses. The district is stable. Gin recovered. A future bought rather than saved.

The same preconditions as Ending One, different answer to the final question. Stability without continuation. The campaign ends with the world held in a particular balance that won't hold forever.
ENDING THREE
The Long Game
Window missed Domain IX partial Rei survives

Rei does not reach Gin in time. The window closes. The demon's institutional campaign succeeds partially. Domain IX destabilizes but not catastrophically. Rei documents everything, closes what can be closed, accepts the loss without becoming defined by it. The archive continues under a different investigation.

The ending for players who followed the evidence carefully but ran out of time. A practitioner's ending. The work goes on. The window was real and it was missed and that is a true thing that happened.
ENDING FOUR
The Cost
Window reached Cost paid Warden judgment

Rei reaches Gin. The exorcism succeeds but at significant cost — either Rei's stability, a relationship, or Domain IX itself. Something that cannot be recovered is lost in the process of recovering Gin. The campaign ends with Gin back and the world different in a way he has to live with.

The ending where the exorcism works but the reckoning is exact. Something was traded that wasn't Rei's alone to trade. Gin is back and knows the price. The world doesn't return to what it was.
ENDING FIVE
The Quiet
Mixed outcomes Books Three and Four

The campaign resolves without dramatic conclusion. The organized entity campaign loses momentum. Gin's window closes without Rei reaching it, but the demon moves on rather than consolidating. The district stabilizes on its own timeline. Rei learns something true about the work: it is ongoing, incomplete, and the practitioners who do it keep doing it anyway.

The ending for players who played cautiously, gathered information without forcing action, or who recognized the limits of individual intervention. Not a failure. Not a victory. An honest account.

Core Truths (GM Use Only)

Core Truth One — What Gin Got Wrong

Gin felt watched for three years and identified the source as Architect attention. He was wrong. What he was feeling was a high-grade demon conducting long-term targeting preparation: slowly and methodically thinning the membrane in his direction. The demon specifically mimics the quality of structural attention. Gin had the framework for one and not the other.

Core Truth Two — Why Rei Masked the Warning

Gin's clairsentience could not isolate the demon's preparatory signal because Rei's unmanaged Arbiter-rank aperture was flooding the apartment with chaotic energy continuously. The demon's frequency — subtle, directional, patient — was buried under Rei's omnidirectional noise. Rei's uncontrolled gift protected the demon's approach by accident for three years.

Schema Inconsistencies

Romance character field null across all 13 entries

Romance entries use a who field (populated, correct) but also have a character field that shows null for every entry. The character registry uses slugs as IDs. Before wiring the romance stages to the character system, either populate character with the matching slug or rename who to character for consistency.

Non-sequential chapter numbering across books

Chapters numbered 1-10 are distributed across Books One through Four as early content. Chapters 11-17 sit alongside them as later-authored content in Books Two through Four. There is no chapter 18 or 19. This appears intentional (waves authoring model) but could confuse the build pipeline. Chapters currently have no number or id field — only title. Consider adding slugs if the build tool needs to address chapters by reference.

Road beats have no title field

Road beats use scene as the scene-setter text, with no title field. The summary above uses scene text as titles. If the build renders beat headers separately from scene prose, a title field will be needed per beat.
