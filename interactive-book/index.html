LORE ARCHIVE
KUROSAWA CITY

A mid-sized Japanese coastal city where the veil between the physical world and something older has grown thin over three years. The city's infrastructure looks normal. Its shadows do not behave normally.

Three years ago: unexplained disappearances, equipment failures in the old ward, and a documented uptick in what the Directorate calls "anomalous social behavior" near the waterbank district.

THE VEIL

What practitioners call the membrane between ordinary space and the layer beneath it where entities move. The Veil is not a place. It is a property of space. Some locations have almost no veil left. Others are thicker than they should be.

Gin's research suggests the Veil thins through collective belief, repeated ritual, or the sustained presence of certain entities. It does not self-repair quickly.

THE CULT

No confirmed name. Rei calls them the cult. They work in small autonomous cells with no apparent central coordination, yet their ritual methodology is identical across incidents. They are accelerating the thinning. No one knows why with certainty.

THE WATERBANK

Old industrial district along the eastern river. The city redeveloped most of it. One block remained sealed after a collapse in the 1980s. The cult is active here. Dead readers cluster here. The Directorate has a sealed floor in the local CVD office with files on the district going back to 1971.

THE OJI OVERPASS

Elevated crossing on the city's north edge. Known locally as a place people stop. Three Veil-sensitive individuals have reported the same entity presence there in the last year. The entity does not interact. It observes from a fixed point above the traffic lane.

The Thirteen are the oldest named entities in Gin's research. They are not gods. They are not demons. They predate both frameworks. They have names because humans gave them names, and they have kept those names because names are a form of agreement.

The Keeper of the Fold
Maintains the structural integrity of the Veil in a given area. Has been absent from the waterbank district for at least three years.
The Witness
Observes. Does not intervene. Has no known location but is documented in reports across six wards. May or may not be a single entity.
The One Who Speaks First
Initiates contact with practitioners it considers capable. Offers. Does not coerce. Every documented refusal was honored. Every documented acceptance changed the person significantly.
The Unbound
Not documented in Gin's notes. Referenced in the Directorate's sealed floor files. The description is redacted. The file classification is the highest the CVD uses.
Nine More
Gin's research identified thirteen entities meeting the criteria. Four are named above. The others are known only by the symptoms of their presence.
STABILITY

Rei's capacity to interpret what she sees within normal psychological frameworks. Starts at 100. Drops with entity exposure, traumatic revelation, and certain choices. At 0, Rei cannot distinguish the city from what is beneath it. Some abilities only unlock at low stability. Some bad endings only trigger there.

MADNESS

Distinct from Stability. Madness measures how much the other side has marked Rei, not how shaken she is. Madness can open doors Stability cannot. High Madness with high Stability is possible. It is an unstable condition.

CLAIRSENTIENCE

Rei's latent ability to read psychic residue on objects and locations. Five levels. Each level unlocks scene fragments and choice options not otherwise visible. The ability is not always reliable. At level 3 and above, it begins operating without Rei's intention.

THE FIVE ROADS

The investigation has five primary paths: following Itsuki Kuroda (Buddy Road), working with the Directorate's off-books team (CVD Road), tracing The Grid's anomalous signal network (Grid Road), pursuing Rei's clairsentient ability directly (Power Road), and walking the city until the city shows her something (Look Road).

Roads overlap. Choices on one road affect what is visible on others. The endings depend on combinations, not individual paths.

READERS AND DEAD READERS

A reader is a Veil-sensitive individual who can perceive entities and residue. A dead reader is a reader whose sensitivity overloaded and who is now locked in a permanent perceptual state overlapping the physical with the veil-layer. They are not dead. They are unreachable by normal means.

NOTE 1 — FIELD OBSERVATION

"The entity at the overpass has been there for eleven days. It does not move. I do not think it is waiting. I think it is watching the spot where something happened and the spot has not finished happening yet. Time is different for them. Maybe all time is the same to it and what we call 'waiting' is just where we are in a sequence it already sees complete."

NOTE 2 — ON THE CULT

"They don't want power. I was wrong about that. They want contact. They think accelerating the thinning will force contact before the entities choose to make it. That is the most dangerous possible goal. You don't rush a conversation with something that old by removing the walls between you."

NOTE 3 — ON CLAIRSENTIENCE

"Rei can do what I can do but she doesn't know she can do it. She's been filtering it as coincidence for years. The key is the cold sensation — the drop in apparent temperature when she touches something recently touched by the veil. She calls it a drafty room thing. It is not a drafty room thing."

NOTE 4 — FINAL ENTRY

"Someone from the Thirteen made contact tonight. Not one I've documented. It said one thing and then it was gone. I don't know what it meant but I know I have to go look at the waterbank tonight. Rei, if you're reading this: the cold key is not a metaphor. The lock it fits is real. Don't use it unless you understand what you're opening."
