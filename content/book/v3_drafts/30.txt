#title The Five
#part Book Three. The Thirteen

The first rank exam of the year was on the eighth of February, in the sub-basement under Reintegration, in a room the trainees called the Box.

It was a square concrete room with no windows and one heavy door. The walls were covered in white paper seals, layer over layer, so many that they looked like snow. In the middle of the floor there was a circle of iron set into the concrete. Inside the circle, every exam day, the Division put something.

"Class D," said Shimizu, outside the door, on the morning of the exam. "Tame. Old. It's been in the Division's care for twelve years. It's done this a hundred times. It will try to frighten you. It's not allowed to touch you." She looked at the line of six trainees. "You go in one at a time. You have five minutes. Read it. Calm it. Capture it. Come out. Instructors watch through the glass." She looked at Rei last, and the small line between her eyebrows went a little deeper. "Show me what you can do. Not more."

He went in fourth.

[break The Box]

The thing in the iron circle was a woman. Or it had been. It sat on the floor with its knees drawn up, very thin, very pale, in a long white nightdress, with black hair hanging down over its face so that only the chin showed. It was rocking, very slowly, back and forth. It was humming a lullaby, three notes, over and over.

When the door closed behind Rei, it stopped humming. It lifted its head. The hair fell back.

It had no eyes. Only smooth pale skin where the eyes should be.

"You," it said, in a voice like a rusty swing. "You. You have so much room in you. Just like..."

"Just like the other one," said Rei. "I know. Everybody says that."

The thing stopped.

He did not open the door in his chest any further than a finger's width. The latch on his wrist held it there. He could have opened it more. He could feel, behind the latch, how much more there was. He could have read this thing to the bottom in ten seconds. Where it died. Who it was waiting for. Why it hummed. It would have been easy.

*Show me what you can do. Not more.*

So he knelt down on the cold concrete outside the iron circle, at the same height as the thing, and he did what Tanizaki had taught him. He sat. He did not ask for anything. He let it look at him with the eyes it did not have. After two minutes, it began to hum again. Softer. After three, it put its head down on its knees.

He pointed the Capturer and pressed. Five amber lights. *SEALED.* Yellow light. Not green, not red. Confused. Old. Stuck.

He walked out at four minutes ten seconds.

On the other side of the glass, the instructors were writing. One of them, a grey-haired man Rei did not know, was frowning. Shimizu was looking at the wall. Okada, in the corner with her notebooks, did not look up at all.

His result was posted that afternoon on the board outside the trainee room. *Uedera Rei. Adept. Clairsentience 4.*

Adept. One rank up from Postulate. Two below what he was.

Kato stood beside him and looked at the board for a long time.

"They're going to notice eventually," he said quietly. "You know that, right? You sat on the floor and that thing went to sleep. The guy before you got thrown into the wall."

"I know."

"Okay." Kato turned his king over in his pocket. "Just checking."

[break The five]

The next morning Shimizu called them into her office. Rei and Kato. And two others.

One was Nakamura Aoi, with her bead bracelet, turning it round and round on her wrist. She smiled at Rei when he came in, the quick real smile, and moved her chair to make room.

The other was a man Rei had seen in the corridors but never spoken to.

He was about twenty-eight. Stocky, solid, with neatly parted hair and a beautiful dark suit, much better than anyone else's on the second floor. He had a lanyard, but it was tucked inside his jacket so you could not read it. He had an easy, open, handsome face, and he was sitting with one ankle on his knee and his chair tipped back on two legs, as if he owned the office. When Rei came in, he looked up. And his eyes stayed on Rei, steady and curious and amused, for a long moment.

"So you're the famous Uedera," he said.

"Famous?"

"In my head, at least." He let his chair down onto four legs and held out his hand. "Tendo Akira. Field. Psychokinesis." His handshake was warm and firm and a little too long. "I've been wanting to meet you for weeks. You read wrong on every instrument in the building. So do I." He grinned. It was a very good grin. "I thought we should be friends. Anomalies should stick together."

"Tendo," said Shimizu from behind her desk.

"Sorry." He sat back. He did not look sorry.

Shimizu put her hands flat on the desk.

"This is a team," she said. "Five. Me. Tendo. Nakamura. Kato. Uedera. From today we work together. Field operations. Real ones. Not cats and phone boxes." She looked at each of them in turn. "Tendo burns things. Nakamura moves things and does seals better than anyone on this floor, though her file says she can't. Kato hears things. Uedera reads things. I tell you all where it's going to go wrong." She paused. "This team was not my idea. The Director asked for it. Personally. I don't know why. I don't like not knowing why."

Aoi stopped turning her bracelet. Kato went still. Tendo raised his eyebrows.

"Personally?" said Tendo. "Well. My father will be pleased. He likes it when the Director takes an interest."

"Your father isn't on this team," said Shimizu flatly.

"My father's on every team," said Tendo pleasantly. "That's sort of his whole thing."

Rei looked at him. At the beautiful suit and the easy grin and the hidden lanyard. He thought of Haruki. *Tendo Akira. A family name with reach above the bureau.*

Beside him, very quietly, Kato said nothing at all. His hand was in his pocket, holding the king. He did not look at Tendo once.

[break The temple]

"First case," said Shimizu. She pushed a thin file across the desk.

Rei opened it. A photograph of a small old temple, with a tiled roof and a wooden bell tower, in a narrow street of old houses. *Jōkō-ji. Takinogawa, Kita. Resident priest Rev. Egami Jakū.* A list of dates. A list of names.

"Something has been in that temple since October," said Shimizu. "It started with the bell. Ringing by itself at dusk. Then the candles going out. Then the parishioners. Old people who came to pray for their dead. Three of them have gone home from the temple and not woken up. Heart failure. Nobody linked it until last month."

"The bell," said Rei slowly. "I know that temple. The Grid had it on a list. They sent someone in."

Shimizu looked at him for a moment. She did not ask how he knew.

"The Grid sent someone in December," she said. "She came out and wouldn't go back. A Division team went in in January. Two officers. One came out. The other is in Room Twelve in Reintegration, and he hasn't said a word since." She closed the file. "Reverend Egami has refused to leave. He sleeps in the temple. He says it's his temple and his parishioners and he is not going to be frightened out of it by something that rings his bell." She stood up. "He has asked the Division for help three times. We are the help. Tomorrow night. Seven o'clock. Bring everything."

They went out. In the corridor, Tendo stretched and yawned.

"A haunted temple," he said happily. "Finally. I've been bored out of my mind." He clapped Rei on the shoulder, warmly, and his hand stayed there a little too long, like his handshake. "See you tomorrow, anomaly." He walked off down the corridor, whistling.

Kato waited until he was gone. Then he turned to Rei.

"I can't hear him," he said.

Rei looked at him.

"Tendo," said Kato. "I can hear everyone on this floor. Aoi's thinking about whether she fed her plants. Shimizu's thinking about five ways tomorrow goes wrong. You're thinking about the bell." He was turning the king very fast now. "Tendo, nothing. Just a hum. Like a radio between stations. All the time. Since the first day I met him."

"Maybe he's good at hiding."

"Nobody's that good at hiding," said Kato. "Not from me." He put the king in his pocket. "Either he's had training I've never heard of. Or there's something in the way."

"Something like what?"

Kato looked down the empty corridor where Tendo had gone.

"I don't know," he said. "That's what scares me."
