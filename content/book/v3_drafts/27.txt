#title Partners
#part Book Two. The Families

In January, Shimizu put Rei and Kato together.

"You're partners," she said, on the first Monday of the year, standing in front of their two desks with her arms folded. "Field pairs. Every trainee has one. You'll go out together, you'll write reports together, and if one of you does something stupid, the other one gets the blame." She looked at Kato. "Kato hears what people are thinking." She looked at Rei. "Uedera reads what happened in a room. Between you, you're almost one useful officer."

"Almost," said Kato.

"Almost," said Shimizu. "Don't let it go to your heads."

She went back to her office. Kato turned his chair round and looked at Rei.

"So," he said. "Partners."

"Partners."

"You realise most people ask to be moved away from me. Within a week. Usually by Wednesday." Kato turned his chess king over in his fingers. "My last partner put in a request on the Tuesday. She said I made her feel like she was talking in a room with no curtains."

"What did you say?"

"I said, that's a very good description, can I use it?" Kato put the king down on the desk. "She did not find that funny."

"I find it funny."

"I know you do. I can hear it." Kato looked at him for a moment longer, and something in his face did not quite know where to go. Then he picked up a file from his tower of photocopies and threw it across the gap between their desks. "Okay, partner. Our first case. You're going to hate it. It's in Itabashi and it smells of cat."

[break Kuma]

Shimizu came with them on the first one. She brought her dog.

[need ch27_kuma | A broad tan-and-white Akita dog with a braided prayer-cord round its neck standing very still and stiff-legged at the bottom of a narrow apartment staircase, ears flat, staring up, a lean woman in a dark suit beside it, two young men behind]

Rei had not known she had a dog. It was waiting for them in the car park of the Division building, sitting by the back door of a grey Division car with the patience of a statue. It was an Akita, a big Japanese dog, broad and thick-furred, tan on top and white underneath, with a curled tail and small dark triangle ears and a wise heavy face like a bear. Round its neck, instead of a collar, it wore a thick braided cord of red and white silk, the kind that hangs on temple bells.

"This is Kuma," said Shimizu. "He belonged to an exorcist in Saitama who was hurt last year and had to stop working. Nobody wanted him. He's too serious for most people." She opened the car door and the dog got in without being told and sat on the back seat, very upright, looking straight ahead. "He's the best alarm in the Division. He won't step on thin ground. And when something's about to come at you, he knows half a second before you do." She got in the front. "Half a second is a long time."

Kato got in the back next to the dog. Kuma turned his big head and looked at Kato for a long moment. Then he looked at Rei. Then he put his head down on his paws and closed his eyes, as if he had seen everything he needed to see.

"He likes you," said Shimizu, starting the engine.

"How can you tell?" said Kato.

"He didn't growl."

[break The man in 302]

The case was a flat on the third floor of an old apartment building in Itabashi, near the Shakujii river. A man of about forty lived there with eleven cats. The neighbours had called the Division because they heard the man talking at night, for hours, in two different voices. Arguing with himself. Sometimes laughing. Sometimes screaming. The landlord had been in to check. He said the man was polite and tidy and the cats were well fed and there was nothing wrong. He said the man had made him tea.

"Class D, probably," said Shimizu, on the stairs. "A spirit sharing the flat. A dead relative. A previous tenant. Nothing violent. Three ways it goes wrong." She stopped on the landing. "The cats. A man who talks in two voices. And the landlord, who said everything was fine." She looked at Rei. "Why would a landlord go into a flat full of cats and voices and come out saying everything was fine?"

Before Rei could answer, Kuma stopped.

He stopped at the bottom of the last flight of stairs, the one that went up to the third floor, and he would not go on. He did not bark. He did not whine. He went stiff. All four legs straight. His tail uncurled and hung down. His ears went flat against his head. And he stared up the stairs at the door of flat 302 with his whole body, the way a dog stares at something it has decided it is going to fight.

Shimizu looked at the dog. The small line between her eyebrows went deeper.

"Not Class D," she said quietly.

The door of flat 302 opened.

[break The tea]

The man who opened it was ordinary. Forty, a little heavy, with thinning hair and round glasses and a cardigan with a cat's hair all over it. He was smiling. A nice, shy, slightly embarrassed smile.

"Oh," he said. "Oh, the Division! I wondered if you'd come. The neighbours. I'm so sorry about the noise." He stood back from the door. "Please. Come in. I'll make tea."

Behind him, in the dim flat, Rei could see cats. Many cats. On the sofa, on the shelves, on top of the fridge. They were all sitting very still. They were all facing the door. None of them was moving at all.

On his wrist, the latch went cold.

"Thank you," said Shimizu pleasantly. "We'd love some tea."

She went in. Kuma did not move from the bottom of the stairs. Rei went to follow her, and Kato caught his sleeve.

Rei looked at him.

Kato's face had gone pale. His eyes were fixed on the man in the cardigan, who was in the little kitchen now, filling a kettle, humming. Kato's lips moved, very slightly, without sound, as if he were listening to two radios at once and trying to follow both.

"Two," he whispered. "There's two of them in there. Two layers. The top one is him. He's nervous. He's thinking about the tea. He's thinking, *I hope they like me, I hope they go away soon.*" Kato swallowed. "And underneath."

"What's underneath?"

"Underneath it's thinking about the kitchen knife," said Kato. "The big one in the drawer by the sink. It's been thinking about it since we came up the stairs. It's thinking about which of us to do first." His hand was tight on Rei's sleeve. "It wants Shimizu first. Because she's the one who'll notice. It's going to do it when he brings the tray."

Rei looked at him.

He did not ask how Kato knew. He did not ask if he was sure. He did not stop to wonder if Kato might be wrong, or exaggerating, or making a joke. He looked at Kato's white face and his tight hand and he believed him. All the way. In one second.

He walked into the flat.

[break Half a second]

"Shimizu-san," he said, very calmly, as he went in. "The landlord said the cats were well fed."

Shimizu was standing by the sofa. She looked at him. She looked at Kato in the doorway behind him. And Rei saw it happen, in her face. He saw her see it. All the ways. Three, four, five. And choose.

"Yes," she said. "He did. I wonder why the cats look so thin."

In the kitchen, the humming stopped.

At the bottom of the stairs, Kuma barked.

It was one bark. Deep and loud and sharp, like a gunshot in the narrow stairwell. And in the half second after it, the half second that Shimizu had said was a long time, the man in the cardigan came out of the kitchen.

He did not have the tea tray. He had the knife. The big one. And his face was not shy any more. His face had stopped. Every muscle in it had gone still at once, the way a pond goes still when the wind drops. The round glasses had slipped down his nose and he did not push them up. His eyes behind them were open very wide and very wet and did not blink, and the pupils had gone so big and black that they swallowed the whole eye, and they were taking in all of them at once, every movement, every breath, and his mouth hung a little open, and a sound was coming out of it. A low, thin, continuous sound, like a kettle just before it boils.

He went for Shimizu.

He was fast. Faster than a heavy man in a cardigan should be. But Kuma's bark had given them half a second, and Shimizu had already chosen, and she was already moving. Not away. Sideways. Her left hand caught the man's wrist and turned it, smoothly, like turning a door handle, and the knife went past her ribs and into the back of the sofa. A cat screamed and fled. Shimizu's right hand came up flat against the man's chest, the way Grandmother Mitsu's had on Itsuki's.

"Uedera," she said, quite calmly. "Iron. Now."

Rei already had the jar open. He threw the filings in a black spray across the man's face and shoulders.

The man screamed in a voice that was not his. High and thin and shrieking, like metal on metal. He let go of the knife. He reeled back across the room and fell against the shelves, and the cats scattered in every direction, and something came out of him. Rei saw it with the door in his chest open. A grey shape, thin as a shadow on a wall, with too many long fingers, pulling itself out of the man's mouth and nose and ears like smoke out of a bottle.

"Kato," said Shimizu. "Where's it going?"

"The kitchen," said Kato from the doorway, instantly. "It's thinking about the window over the sink. It wants to get out."

"Uedera. Capturer."

Rei pulled out the little black box and pointed it at the kitchen window and pressed CAPTURE before he could think.

The five amber lights went across the top. One. Two. Three. Four.

On the fourth one, the grey shape at the kitchen window stopped. It turned. It looked at him, with a face that was all long grey fingers. And it laughed, a small dry laugh like paper tearing, and went through the glass of the window as if the glass were not there, and was gone.

The fifth light did not come on. The amber lights went out. The screen said *EMPTY*.

The big light under it, for one second, had glowed. Not green. Red. Then nothing.

[break After]

The man in the cardigan was lying on the floor among the scattered cats, crying. He did not know where he was. He did not know who they were. He had been living with something for eight months, Shimizu said later. It had come in through one of the cats, a stray he had taken in from the street in the spring. It had lived in him quietly, using him to feed the other cats less and less, using the cats to feed itself. The neighbours had heard him arguing. It was the man, arguing with the thing inside him, every night, for eight months, and losing.

He would live. He would go to Reintegration for a while. The cats would go to a shelter.

The landlord, when they called him, did not remember coming into the flat. He did not remember the tea.

In the car on the way back, Shimizu drove with her eyes on the road and did not say anything for a long time. Kuma sat on the back seat between Rei and Kato, very upright, looking straight ahead. After a while he put his big heavy head down on Rei's knee and left it there.

"You didn't check," said Kato.

Rei looked at him. Kato was looking out of the window at the grey January streets going past.

"On the stairs," said Kato. "When I told you about the knife. You didn't check. You didn't ask me how I knew, or if I was sure, or if I was making it up. You didn't even think it. I'd have heard." He was turning the king over and over in his fingers, very fast. "You just went in."

"You said it was going to happen. So it was going to happen."

"People don't do that," said Kato. "Not with me. Ever. They always check. They always wonder. Even the ones who like me. Even Okada. I can hear them wondering. *Is he right? Is he reading too much? Is he just trying to be clever?*" He stopped turning the king. "You didn't wonder."

Rei did not know what to say. So he did not say anything.

Kato was quiet for a long time. The car stopped at a red light. Kuma breathed slowly and heavily on Rei's knee.

"I was going to make a joke," said Kato. "Just now. I had a really good one ready. About cats." He looked down at the king in his hand. "I don't have one. I don't have a joke. This is the first time in about ten years I don't have a joke."

In the front seat, Shimizu's eyes went to the mirror for one second. Then back to the road.

"Good," she said. "Hold on to that. It doesn't happen often."

The light turned green. The car moved on.

"I'm putting in for a transfer," said Kato, after a while.

Rei looked at him, startled.

"Not from you, idiot." Kato almost smiled. "For the dog. I want to be the one who walks him."

Kuma lifted his head from Rei's knee and looked at Kato. Then he put his head down again, on Rei's knee, and closed his eyes.

"He's chosen," said Shimizu from the front. "Bad luck, Kato."

"Story of my life," said Kato. But he was smiling now. A small, real smile, on both sides of his face. And he did not have a joke for that either.
