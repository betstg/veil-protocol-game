#title The Last Favour
#part Book Three. The Thirteen

The delivery rider had been standing at the same crossing in Higashi-Jūjō for six months.

Rei found him by accident, on a wet night in late May, walking home from the station the long way. His wrist went cold at the corner by the big road, and he stopped, and opened the door in his chest a finger's width, and there he was. On the kerb. A young man of about twenty, thin, in the uniform of a food delivery company, with a big square insulated box on his back. He was standing at the edge of the road looking at the traffic, waiting for the light. When it turned green, he did not cross. When it turned red, he waited again.

There was a small scar through one of his eyebrows. And from the hip down, on his left side, his uniform trousers were dark and wet and shining, and the darkness never dried.

Rei stood on the other side of the crossing in the rain and watched him for a long time.

[break The name]

He took out the Capturer. He did not press CAPTURE. He pressed TALK, and pointed the top of the box across the road, and waited. The screen stayed dark for a long time. Then letters came, slowly, one at a time.

*order 4471*

*is it late*

*im sorry its late*

Rei typed back. *It's not your fault. What's your name?*

A long pause. The light went green and red and green.

*kōhei*

The little plastic flap over the HOST button clicked. The tape Shimizu had pressed back down split again.

*Kōhei,* typed Rei. *What happened?*

*truck*

*didnt stop*

*i was going to be late anyway. i always run late on thursdays. mum says ill be late to my own funeral*

A pause.

*was i*

Rei did not answer that.

*there was a man,* said the screen. *on the kerb. tall. long hair. umbrella. he watched*

*he didnt help*

*why didnt he help*

Rei stood very still in the rain. His wrist was burning cold. His heart was very loud.

Tall. Long hair. An umbrella. On a kerb. Watching.

He did not know. He could not know. There were ten thousand tall men with long hair in Tokyo. But his body knew before his head did. His body went cold from the top of his scalp to the soles of his feet, and stayed cold, and his hands on the little black box began to shake.

*I don't know,* he typed. *I'm sorry. I don't know why he didn't help.*

*ok*

*i have to finish the order*

*and i have to tell mum. i passed. the exam. i passed. i got the letter on the morning. i was going to tell her when i finished. i was going to surprise her*

*i cant get across the road*

[break The button]

Rei went home and did not sleep. He sat at the kitchen table with the Capturer in front of him and the green light glowing in the dark and the HOST flap standing open, its tape split.

*Your brother pressed that button exactly four times in six years. He said each time cost him a month.*

He read the archive. The page about it was short. Gin had written it in a hurry, the handwriting leaning.

*Hosting. Letting a spirit you know by name ride you, by consent, for a fixed purpose and a fixed time. The Capturer holds the terms. When the purpose is done, it leaves. Always. Spirits are not demons. They do not want to stay. They want to finish.*

*But you will not be the same afterwards. Something of them stays. A habit. A taste. A fear. It is like letting a stranger sleep in your bed. They leave. The sheets remember. Do it rarely. Do it only when there is no other way to give them what they need. And never, ever, do it alone.*

Under it, in pencil, in the margin, smaller:

*The last one cost me more than a month. I still can't eat melon. I don't know why. I used to love melon.*

Rei sat at the table until the sky went grey.

Then he picked up his phone and called Itsuki and Kato. Not Haruki, who would say no. Not Shimizu, who would say no and write it down. Itsuki and Kato. The trio.

"I'm going to do something stupid," he said. "I promised to tell you first."

There was a long silence on the line. Then Kato said, "I'll bring the car."

And Itsuki said, "I'll bring snacks. Stupid things go better with snacks."

[break Riding]

They stood at the crossing in Higashi-Jūjō at eleven the next night. Thursday. Kōhei's late night. Kato had the car parked by the kerb with the engine running. Itsuki stood beside Rei with his hand on his shoulder, very tight.

Rei held up the Capturer.

*Kōhei. I'm going to carry you across. You can use my legs. My mouth. Just for tonight. Just to get to your mum. Then you have to go. Okay?*

*okay*

*thank you*

*sorry about the trousers*

Rei almost laughed. He pressed HOST.

It was like stepping into a cold river with all his clothes on.

Something came into him. Not through the door in his chest. Through his hands, through the little black box, up his arms, into his shoulders, his neck, his face. Cold, and wet, and tired, and young. It filled him from the inside the way water fills a glass. And then he was standing a little to one side of himself, inside his own body, the way you stand at the side of a room at a party where you do not know anybody.

His body moved.

He did not move it. Kōhei moved it. His feet stepped off the kerb. His legs walked across the road, fast, the way someone walks who is always running late. His head turned left, then right, then left again, checking for trucks, over and over, much more than it needed to. His left hand went to his hip, to the place where the uniform trousers had been wet, and pressed, and pressed. On the other side of the road, his body stopped, and turned round, and looked back at the kerb where it had stood for six months, and something went through him that was not his. A huge, shaking relief. *Across. Across. Across.*

"Rei?" said Itsuki, beside him. His voice was very careful. "Rei, are you in there?"

Rei's mouth opened. A voice came out of it that was nearly his. His voice, but quicker, lighter, with a different rhythm, a little Kansai accent he had never had.

"He's here," said his mouth. "He's fine. He says hi. He says you've got paint on your neck." A pause. "Sorry. I'm Kōhei. I'm borrowing him. Thanks for the snacks."

Itsuki made a strange sound and took his hand off Rei's shoulder and then put it back again, harder.

[break The shop]

Kōhei's mother ran a small tobacco and stationery shop in Ōji, by the old tram line, ten minutes' walk. It was closed. There was a light on upstairs. Rei's body walked there with Itsuki on one side and Kato driving slowly along the kerb on the other, and the whole way, his body kept checking over its shoulder for trucks.

Rei could feel everything. The cold wet ache in the left hip that was not his. The tiredness of a boy who had been working nights and studying days for two years. A taste in his mouth of curry, very strong, though he had not eaten curry in weeks. And under all of it, like a bright warm coal, a letter. A letter with a hospital's name on the top. *We are pleased to inform you.*

He rang the bell. His finger rang it three times, quick, quick, quick, a pattern, a family pattern.

The light came on in the shop. The door opened.

A woman of about fifty stood in the doorway in a cardigan, with her grey hair down for the night and her reading glasses on her head. She looked at the young man on her doorstep. A stranger. A thin boy in a black hooded jacket with a key on a cord round his neck. She did not know him.

Then he rang the bell one more time, *quick quick quick*, without meaning to, and her face went white.

"Who," she said. "Who taught you that? That's... only my son..."

"Mum," said Rei's mouth.

He felt Kōhei push. Not to leave. To be closer. To get as far forward in Rei's body as he could, to the front of the eyes, to the edge of the tongue. Rei let him. He stepped back inside himself, as far as he could go, and let a dead boy wear his face.

"Mum," said Kōhei. "Mum, I passed. The nursing exam. I passed. The letter came that morning. I was going to tell you when I finished the shift. I was going to surprise you." His voice cracked. Rei felt the tears come into his own eyes and run down his own face, Kōhei's tears. "I'm sorry I was late. I'm always late. I'm sorry. I'm so sorry I didn't come home."

The woman stood in the doorway with both hands over her mouth.

"It's in my locker," said Kōhei. "At the depot. The letter. Locker 22. The code's your birthday." He laughed, wetly. "You always said I'd be late to my own funeral. I was. Sorry. I love you. I passed."

She reached out and touched Rei's face. His cheek. Very gently. Her hand was warm and shaking.

"Kōhei," she said.

"Yeah."

"Go on, then," she said. Her voice was steady. Rei did not know how it could be steady. "You're late. Go on. I'll get the letter. I'll put it on the altar. Go on, love."

And Kōhei went.

[break What stayed]

He went out of Rei the way a breath goes out. All at once, cold and wet and young, out through his chest and his mouth and his hands, and away, up, past the light in the shop window, into the dark over the old tram line. Rei felt him go. He felt the hip stop aching. He felt the taste of curry fade.

He felt, at the very last second, something brush against him. Like a hand on his arm in passing.

*thanks*

*the man on the kerb*

*he had your face*

Then he was gone.

Rei's legs folded. Itsuki caught him. Kato was out of the car and on his other side. They held him up between them on the pavement outside the closed shop, while Kōhei's mother stood in her doorway with her hands over her mouth and cried.

It cost more than a month.

It cost him the next three days, which he did not remember. Itsuki told him afterward that he had slept for most of them, on the sofa, and woken up twice and asked for curry, and once stood up in the middle of the night and walked to the front door and stood there, checking left and right, left and right, for trucks, until Kato took his arm and led him back.

It cost him a hum. The thin high note behind his eyes, the one that had been there since the floodgate, was louder now. Not much. Just enough that he noticed it in quiet rooms.

And it cost him something he did not understand until a week later, in the konbini, at the counter, with a can of hot corn soup in his hand. His phone buzzed. A message from Daiki. And before he could stop himself, before he even looked at the screen, his thumb went to it and his heart jumped and his mind said, very clearly, in a voice that was almost his own, *order 4471, is it late, am I late.*

It went away. It always went away. But it came back. For the rest of his life, every time his phone buzzed, there would be one half second when he thought he was late.

He did not mind. Not really. He thought it was a fair price.

What he minded was the other thing. The thing Kōhei said at the very end, brushing past him, going up into the dark.

*The man on the kerb. He had your face.*

He did not tell Itsuki. He did not tell Kato, though Kato could hear him not telling. He did not write it in the archive.

He sat at the kitchen table that night with two cups of tea, one for him and one for nobody, and thought about a tall man with long hair, standing on a kerb under an umbrella in the rain, watching a boy bleed to death in the road and doing nothing.

His brother's face. His brother's hands. His brother's body.

Not his brother.

He did not cry. He was past that. He sat with the two cups until both of them were cold. And then he did something he had never done before.

He was angry.
