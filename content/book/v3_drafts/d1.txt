#title The Umbrella
#part Book One. The Archive

*The Healer.*

It had been a very long time since the Healer had worn a body. It had forgotten how many small things a body had to do.

It stood in the doorway of a convenience store in the rain, in the borrowed body of Uedera Gin, and watched a man open an umbrella.

The man was young, in a cheap black suit, with a paper bag of food in one hand. He held the umbrella in the other hand with the point down. He pressed something with his thumb, a small catch on the handle, and the umbrella jumped open with a soft snap, like a black flower opening all at once. Then he lifted it over his head and walked out into the rain and did not get wet.

The Healer found this very interesting.

It had a body's memories to draw on. It knew, in the way you know a word in a language you speak badly, that this was called an umbrella, that you bought them in places like this for five hundred yen, that people lost them all the time and did not mind. Gin had owned perhaps forty umbrellas in his life and lost thirty-nine. But knowing a thing from inside someone else's memory is like knowing a city from a map. It is not the same as walking in it.

It bought an umbrella.

That was another interesting thing. Paying. The body's hand knew to take out a small plastic card and touch it to a small grey square by the till. The square made a bright little sound, and the clerk said *thank you very much*, and that was all. No coin changed hands. No promise was spoken. The Healer stood for a moment and looked at the little grey square and thought about what it meant. A debt, paid by touching. A whole city of people paying their debts all day long without speaking. It thought, perhaps, that it had misjudged the living. It had thought them careless. Perhaps they were only fast.

The clerk was a girl of about twenty with tired eyes. She was looking at the Healer with an expression it could not read.

"Are you alright, sir?" she said.

"Yes," said the Healer, in Gin's voice. "Thank you. I am very well."

The girl's face changed a little. It was the same expression people had made at it all week, in shops and stations and on the street. A small pulling back. As if they had seen something in its face that they did not have a word for. The Healer did not know what they saw. It had looked at the face in many mirrors. It seemed to it to be a perfectly good face. A tired, handsome face with dark eyes and long black hair. It smiled at the girl to reassure her.

The girl stepped back from the counter.

The Healer took its umbrella and went out.

It walked for a long time in the rain, holding the new umbrella. It practised the catch on the handle. Open. Close. Open. It found that if you held it slightly forward when walking into the wind, the rain did not reach your knees. It was pleased with this discovery.

On a crowded crossing near a station, a woman walking fast with her head down bumped into its shoulder.

"Ah, sorry, sorry," she said, bowing quickly, and was gone into the crowd.

The Healer stood still on the crossing while the lights changed and people flowed round it.

*Sorry.* She had said sorry. To a stranger. For a small touch on the shoulder that had cost nothing and harmed nothing. She had owed it nothing, and she had paid anyway, and walked on without waiting to see if her payment was accepted.

It turned this over and over, the way it had turned the umbrella. It did not understand it. In all its long existence it had understood debts. It had understood obligations, and duties, and what is owed by one to another. It had built itself out of them. It *was* them. A debt was a chain. You were bound by it until it was paid. That was the whole shape of the world.

But this woman had made herself a tiny debt, and paid it, in the same breath. As if the chain were nothing. As if it were only a way of saying *I see you are there*.

It thought that perhaps the living used debts the way they used umbrellas. Lightly. Many of them. Losing them all the time and not minding.

It did not know what to do with this thought. It put it away carefully, to look at later.

[break The finger]

Inside the body, very deep down, the man was asleep.

The Healer could feel him there, the way you feel the weight of a sleeping cat on your legs in the night. Uedera Gin. It held him down gently, like a hand on the shoulder of a fevered patient. It was not cruel to him. It did not believe in cruelty. It kept the body warm and fed and rested. It drank his tea and slept in his clothes and did not let anything harm him. He had carried too much for too long. Anyone could see it. It was only right that someone else should carry it now.

But twice this week he had woken.

Once in the kitchen, over the tea, when the phone buzzed. Once in the night, for one breath, when it walked past a train station and a train went by with a sky-blue stripe. Both times it had felt him surge up from the bottom of the body like a swimmer kicking toward the light. Both times it had been the younger one that woke him. A message. A train line the younger one rode.

And once his finger had moved.

The Healer thought about that finger often. One centimetre. Toward the phone. It had not expected that. In all its long experience, the ones it held did not move. They slept and grew quiet and, in time, they stopped. That was the way of things. It was a kindness. A long rest for a tired person.

This one had moved his finger.

It stood under its new umbrella on a quiet street in the rain, outside a small apartment building it did not know, and looked up at the lit windows. It was not here by accident. Somewhere across the river, very far away, something old and cold and hungry was moving through the city, laying its rats down one by one. Something the Healer knew. Something it had a very old debt with.

*Not yet,* it told itself. *Not yet. First, what is owed.*

And then, because the thought of the younger one would not leave it alone, it took out the phone and looked at the last message again. It read it through slowly, the way it had studied the catch on the umbrella.

*I helped someone today. In Jūjō. With salt and anpan. I think I did it right.*

The Healer did not understand why it read the message a second time. Or a third.

It did not reply. It put the phone away, and opened and closed the umbrella once more, just to feel the soft snap of it, and walked on into the rain.
