#title The Director
#part Book Two. The Families

Rei did not own a suit. Okada lent him the money, and he bought one at a big chain store by Akabane station for nineteen thousand eight hundred yen. Dark grey, a little too long in the arms. A white shirt. A black tie he tied four times in front of the mirror in the shop toilet before it looked like anything.

At nine o'clock on the sixteenth of December he walked into the Division building in Akabane through the front door.

[break Assessment]

The Assessment floor was the third. It was bright and quiet and smelled of new carpet. There were rows of small white rooms with glass walls, like the rooms in a hospital where they take blood. People in grey moved between them with tablets. On one wall hung a poster of the smiling white owl, with the words *Knowing yourself is the first step to keeping everyone safe.*

Okada met him at the lift. She did not say anything about the suit. She looked at the tie for one second, and Rei saw her decide not to say anything about the tie either.

"I'll be in the room," she said quietly. "I'm Assessment and Registry. It's my job. I measure people twice a month." She walked him down the corridor. "Remember what I said."

"Just tell them what I feel."

"Yes." She opened the glass door of a small white room. "Sit."

In the room there was a chair and a table and, on the table, a row of objects. A brass bowl. A child's red shoe. A folded handkerchief. An old wristwatch. A small white stone. And a machine Rei had never seen, a flat grey box with two pads on top like the pads for your hands at a passport gate, and a screen on the side covered in slowly moving green lines.

A young man in grey came in with a tablet and smiled at him with very white teeth. "Uedera Rei? Lovely. Just put your hands on the pads. It won't hurt. It just reads your general signature."

Rei put his hands on the pads.

The green lines on the screen jumped. Then jumped again. Then went into a sort of mad scribble, up and down, back and forth, like a heartbeat monitor in a film when the patient is dying. The young man frowned at it. He tapped the screen. He tapped it again.

"That's odd," he said. "That's very... Do you have any tattoos, Mr. Uedera?"

Rei held up his left wrist and turned back the cuff of his new shirt.

The young man looked at the latch and the little bridge and the dented key and made a small noise of pain, as if Rei had shown him a broken bone. "Oh, no. Oh, dear. That's going to make everything really hard to read." He wrote something on his tablet. "We'll do it the old way, then. Ms. Okada?"

Okada sat down opposite Rei. She took out her three notebooks and laid them on the table in a row.

"The objects," she said. "One at a time. Tell me what you feel. Don't try. Just tell me."

He did it the way Sōun had taught him. A finger's width. The latch on his wrist held it there. And underneath the latch, the old door he had been closing since he was eight, the one Gin had taught him at the kitchen table, closed itself the way it always did in a room full of strangers. He did not decide it. It was a habit older than he could remember.

The brass bowl was from a temple. Calm. Old. Nothing in it. The child's shoe had a small sad weight on it, faint, very old, like a dried flower in a book. The handkerchief had been held by someone crying, once, at a wedding, and they had been happy. The wristwatch was heavy and cold and he did not like it, and he put it down quickly. The white stone was nothing. Just a stone.

He said exactly that. No more. It was all he could feel through a finger's width and an old habit.

Okada wrote it all down in neat small handwriting. She looked at the green lines on the screen, still scribbling. She frowned at them for a moment. Then she wrote some more.

"Postulate," she said to the young man at the end. "Clairsentience, level three. Telepathy, faint, possibly latent. Signature unreadable. Configuration tattoo, plus a lot of noise I can't place." She closed her notebook. "Rank Postulate. Register him. Flag for re-assessment in six months."

The young man typed. "Postulate. Great. Lovely. That's a good solid rank, Mr. Uedera. Lots of very useful people are Postulates." He beamed. "Welcome to the list."

Rei did not know if it was right. Nobody in the room did. The machine said nothing useful, and he had only told them what he felt.

[break Moriya]

The second interview was on the fifth floor, in Internal Affairs.

It was the dullest room Rei had ever been in. A wall of grey filing cabinets. A desk. A chair. A plant on the windowsill, small and green and clearly well looked after. A cup of tea on the desk, going cold.

Rei knew him. It was the man from the basement. The one who checked Itsuki's drawings for doors every evening. The man behind the desk was in his thirties. Stocky. Tired eyes. Stubble. A dark suit that had been slept in, perhaps more than once. A lanyard with an ID card tucked inside his jacket so you could not read it. He did not look up when Rei came in. He was reading a file. Rei's file, probably. It was very thin.

"Sit down," he said. "Moriya. Internal Affairs." He went on reading.

Rei sat. He waited. Moriya read. The tea on the desk went on getting colder. Outside the window, a train went by on the Saikyō line, and then another. Rei understood, after about five minutes, that this was the interview. The waiting. The silence. He thought of Tanizaki's front room. He made himself sit still, with his hands on his knees, and not ask for anything.

After ten minutes Moriya closed the file and looked up. He did not look at Rei's face. He looked at the whole of him, slowly, the way you look at a room when you come into it. His hands. His shoulders. His feet. The too-long sleeves of the new suit.

"Your brother vouched for you," Moriya said. "Did you know that?"

"No."

"Three years ago. He came to this office and put a letter in your file. Before you had a file. He asked me to keep it until you came in." Moriya opened a drawer and took out a plain white envelope, sealed, with *Rei* written on the front in Gin's small, exact handwriting. He did not give it to Rei. He put it on the desk between them. "It says, in effect, that if you ever come in, you are to be trusted. That he will answer for you."

Rei looked at the envelope. He could not breathe properly.

"I've had it in this drawer for three years," said Moriya. "I've been waiting to see if you'd come. And I've been waiting to see whether, when you came, your brother would still be here to answer for you." He looked at Rei. "He isn't."

"No."

"So I don't know what this letter is worth now." Moriya leaned back. "I'll tell you what I do. I investigate the investigators. When somebody in this building does something they shouldn't, I'm the one who writes it down. I keep the accurate record. Not the family record. Not the public record. The accurate one." He nodded at the cabinets. "Your brother's name is in a lot of those files. Mostly as the person who reported someone else. Once as the person who was reported." He paused. "By the Office of Public Confidence. In July. For asking questions about Protocol."

The room was very quiet.

"I closed that file," said Moriya. "There was nothing in it." He picked up the white envelope and put it back in the drawer, and closed the drawer. "When your brother is here to answer for you, I'll give this to you. Until then, it stays with me." He picked up his cold tea, looked at it, and put it down. "Welcome to the Division, Uedera. Don't do anything I'll have to write down."

[break The Director]

The third interview was not on the schedule.

At four o'clock, when he had filled in nineteen forms and had a photograph taken for a lanyard and been told where the canteen was, a young woman with a bead bracelet came to find him in the corridor.

"Uedera Rei?" She was about twenty, small and quick, with a calm face and a ceramic bead bracelet on her left wrist that she turned round and round with her right hand while she talked. "I'm Nakamura. Trainee. Second year. The Director would like to see you." She smiled. It was a real smile, quick and warm. "I'm not supposed to say this, but I was really looking forward to meeting you. Your brother talked about you."

"Everybody says that."

"Because he did. A lot." She led him to the lift. "He was the only senior officer who ever asked how I was and waited for the answer." She pressed a button. Eight. "Well. Him and the Director."

Rei remembered a page in the archive. *Nakamura Aoi. She asks how I am doing and actually waits for the answer, which I did not ask for but have started depending on.*

He wondered if she knew that Gin had written that. He decided she probably did not. He decided not to tell her yet.

The Director's office was at the end of the eighth floor. The last floor on the directory. At the far end of the same corridor, Rei saw, there was a plain grey door with no handle on the outside and a small black card reader beside it. Nobody was near it. Nakamura did not look at it as they passed.

The office was plain and old-fashioned. A wooden desk. Bookshelves. A window over the railway lines. A man stood at the window with his back to the room.

He was in his fifties. Tall and grey and very upright. Grey suit. Grey hair. He turned round when they came in, and Rei saw a long, quiet, tired face, the face of a man who has not slept properly for twenty years and has stopped minding. In his right hand he was holding a transit card, an old one, the kind you touch to the gates. Its corners were worn soft and round from being held. He was turning it slowly in his fingers, like Kato with his chess king.

"Thank you, Nakamura," he said. His voice was low and even.

Aoi bowed and went out. The door closed.

"Adachi," said the man. "Sit down, Uedera."

Rei sat. Adachi did not. He stood by the window with the soft-cornered card in his fingers and looked at Rei. It was not like Moriya's look, slow and complete. It was not like Okada's, or Choi Hana's, or Sōun's. It was quicker than any of them and it seemed to finish before it started. Rei had the strangest feeling that the Director had read everything he was going to say before he came through the door, and was now simply waiting to see if he would say it.

He remembered the last line on the Director's page in the archive.

*Do not lie to him. You get one attempt and he will let you make it and he will not say anything when you finish, and that silence is the last time he trusts you.*

"I will tell you three things," said Adachi. "Then you will go downstairs and begin your training, and we will not speak like this again for some time. First. I do not know where your brother is. That is true. Second. I did not ask the council to approve what it approved in November. That is also true." He paused. "Third. There are parts of this building that do not answer to me. You will want to go into them. You must not. Not yet. Not until you are much more than a trainee, and much more careful than your brother was."

He went to the desk and opened a drawer and took something out and put it on the desk in front of Rei.

It was a small black plastic box, about the size of a deck of cards. It looked like an old pager, the kind doctors used to carry before phones. Rounded corners. A clip on the back. On the front, a narrow screen, dark green and empty, like the screen of an old calculator. Above the screen, a row of five small round lights, dark. Below it, a single larger light, also dark. And three small rubber buttons. *CAPTURE. TALK. HOST.* The last one was covered with a little plastic flap, taped shut.

"Every trainee is issued one," said Adachi. "It's called a Spirit Capturer. Your training officer will show you how it works. In short, it holds a small entity. A spirit, a fragment. You can carry it. You can speak with it, through the screen. The light shows you what it is. Green is good. Red is not." He looked at the taped flap. "That button you will not use until you know the name of what you are holding. Never before. Do you understand?"

"Yes, sir."

"Your brother carried one for six years." Adachi turned and looked out of the window again. "He said it was the most useful thing the Division ever gave him. He said it taught him that most of what comes through the veil only wants to be asked its name."

Rei picked up the little black box. It was light. It was cold. On his wrist, the latch went cold too, very faintly, and then was still.

"Thank you, sir," he said.

"Don't thank me," said the Director. "Downstairs. Nakamura will show you where. And Uedera."

Rei stopped at the door.

"Your brother never lied to me," said Adachi, without turning round. "Not once, in six years. It is a rare quality in this building." The worn card turned once more in his fingers. "Try to have it."
