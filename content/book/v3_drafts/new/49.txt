#title Tendo

They set the trap on a Monday.

Shimizu planned it. She sat in her office with the blinds down and Rei and Kato on the other side of her desk and Riki asleep on her feet, and she did not say anything for nearly ten minutes. The small line between her eyebrows came and went. Then she took out four blank cards and wrote on each one.

"Four operations," she said. "None of them real. Four different places. Four different times." She laid the cards on the desk. "Each one goes to one person. Only one. Moriya gets this one. The two officers from the other team get these. And Tendo gets this."

She pushed the last card a little apart from the others.

"If someone from the white house turns up at one of these places," she said, "we'll know which card they read."

"And if nobody turns up?" said Kato.

"Then I'm wrong," said Shimizu. "And I've insulted four colleagues for nothing. And I'll apologise to all of them." She looked at the cards. "I'm not wrong."

[break Thursday]

On Thursday evening, at the place on Tendo's card, a quiet park in Asagaya with a pond and a broken fountain, the woman in the grey coat sat down on a bench at exactly the time written on it.

Rei had told Gorō where to go that morning, standing on the balcony, saying it out loud as he had said the river in January. *Asagaya. The park with the broken fountain. The big tree by the pond. Sit there and look.* He had not expected it to work. It had never worked. Gorō went where Gorō liked.

At dusk he sat on the floor of the surveillance van two streets away with the black-backed mirror on his knees, and opened the door in his chest, and looked.

A pond. A broken fountain. A bench. From a branch of the big tree across the water.

Gorō was there.

Rei watched her through Gorō's eyes, with the mirror cold in his fingers and blood starting to run from his nose. She sat for twenty minutes. She looked round the park, slowly, carefully. She looked at the bushes and the paths and the empty fountain. She looked, once, straight up at the tree where Gorō was sitting, and smiled at him, a soft kind teacher's smile.

Then she stood up, and left a white chrysanthemum on the bench, and walked away.

Gorō screamed and flew.

At the other three places, nobody came.

[break The room on the sixth floor]

They did it in Sena's office on Friday morning, with the door shut, because Internal Affairs was the only floor in the building with locks on the inside of the doors.

Sena sat behind his empty desk. Shimizu stood by the window. Moriya sat in the corner on a hard chair with his crutches across his knees and his braced leg out in front of him. Kato stood by the wall with his hand in his pocket on the knight. Riki lay across the doorway, so that nobody could leave without stepping over him. Rei stood beside Kato.

Tendo Akira sat in the chair in front of the desk with one ankle on his knee, in his good suit, with his tie pulled loose, and listened to the whole thing without interrupting. The cards. The park. The chrysanthemum on the bench. The empty flat in Nakano. He listened with polite interest, as if it were a story about someone he had met once at a party.

When Sena finished, Tendo smiled.

It was not his smile. Rei saw that at once. It was close. It used the same face. But it went up only on one side, and stayed there, and the eyes did not come with it.

"*Four cards,*" said Tendo's mouth. The voice was Tendo's voice, but slower, and flatter, and very interested. "*That was clever. I didn't hear that one coming. She planned it in her head with the blinds down and I wasn't in the room.*" The eyes went to Shimizu. "*You think very quietly, for a woman with so many ways for things to go wrong.*"

Nobody moved.

"Who are you?" said Sena. Quite calmly. His pen was ready over his notebook.

"*Nobody you'd know.*" The eyes moved round the room, one person at a time, and Rei felt each look land like a hand going through a pocket. "*Something from the far side that was invited in. He came to see us, you know. In April. On his own. Without telling any of you. He was curious about the white house. He wanted to be the one who found it.*" The half-smile widened. "*And there I was. And I asked him if he'd like to be stronger. Much stronger. Strong enough that nobody on this team would ever have to save him. And he said yes before I'd finished asking.*"

"There's no contract," said Moriya quietly, from the corner. "Is there."

"*No.*" The eyes went to him. "*Why would I sign a contract? Contracts are for things that want to share. I don't want to share. I want the coat.*" It looked down at Tendo's big hands, turning them over, as if admiring a new pair of gloves. "*Another few months and he'll be very quiet indeed. And then he won't be anywhere at all.*"

Kato made a sound. A small, sharp, sick sound.

"*And you,*" said the thing, turning to him. "*The other one who hears. You've been so useful. Every time anything went wrong they looked at you. I didn't have to do a thing.*"

Then something happened to Tendo's face.

It was like watching somebody surface in deep water. The half-smile broke. Both sides of the mouth went slack. The eyes, for one second, were Tendo's eyes, brown and wet and terrified, and they found Rei, and held on.

"I didn't know," said Tendo. In his own voice. It came out cracked and very fast, as if he were shouting it up a well. "I didn't know it could hear. I didn't know it was telling them. I just wanted, I just went to look, I wanted to be the one, I'm sorry, Rei, tell Kato I'm sorry, tell him I didn't..."

And the smile came back over his face like a lid.

[break Fire]

"*That,*" said the thing pleasantly, "*was rude of him.*"

It snapped Tendo's fingers.

Every file in the room caught fire at once.

Not with a roar. With a soft, eager *whump*, like a gas ring lighting. Every grey cabinet along the wall burst into tall white flame from the inside. The heat hit Rei like a slap. The window cracked.

Riki was already moving. He came off the floor in the doorway and went straight for Tendo, low and fast and silent, as he had been trained. The thing looked at him, and the dog stopped in mid-leap, and hung in the air, and then was thrown sideways across the room into the burning cabinets with a yelp of pain that Rei would hear for a long time.

Shimizu threw up both hands. Rei felt the push go across the room, all her strength, and the thing in Tendo's body slid backward in its chair and the chair went over. It laughed, on the floor, and began to get up.

It did not get up.

Moriya had dropped his crutches. He had come across the room on his one good leg, hopping, falling the last metre, and he had his hand flat on the side of Tendo's neck, where the big vein is. He did not hit him. He did not grab him. He simply held his hand there, and closed his eyes, and his face went grey with concentration.

Rei felt it through the door in his chest. Tendo's heart, slowing. Not stopping. Slowing. Beat. Long pause. Beat. Longer pause. Like a clock running down.

"*What are you*..." said the thing, and its voice was slurring.

"Keeping him alive," said Moriya, through his teeth. "Very slowly. Too slowly for you to drive." He did not open his eyes. "Shimizu. Containment. Now."

The thing tried to burn him. Rei saw it try. A little white flame stood up on the cuff of Moriya's jacket and went out, because the hand under it was too slow and too cold and too far away from anything that wanted to burn.

Tendo's eyes closed.

[break After]

They took him down to Containment on a stretcher, with four seals across his chest and Moriya hopping beside it with his hand still on Tendo's neck, all the way down in the lift. The fire alarm was screaming. People were running with extinguishers. Sena stood in the doorway of his burned office with his black notebook in his hand, writing, very calmly, while the files that had been in it turned to ash behind him.

Tendo Akira went into a sealed room two doors down from where Itsuki had spent ninety days. Three weeks later, when the thing in him had been starved and sealed and argued with until it was small enough to hold, the Director signed a paper, and Tendo went down to the sub-level under the sub-level, where there are no windows in the doors at all.

*He went to them alone,* the paper said. *He let a hostile entity into his body and into this building. There is no evidence that he meant harm. There is no way to know what remains of the entity once it is sealed.* Rei read it over Shimizu's shoulder.

"It isn't fair," said Rei.

"No," said Shimizu. "It isn't." She put the paper in a drawer. "He'll live. That's more than the thing in him wanted."

Riki lived. His back leg was broken in two places and he would limp for the rest of his life. When he came out of the animal hospital, he did not go back to the dog room on the ground floor. The Director went down to collect him himself, in his grey suit, and knelt on the floor of the car park in front of him, and Rei, who happened to be coming in, heard the Director of the Civil Veil Division say very quietly to a dog, "I am sorry. That should not have happened to you. It was my building." Then Adachi carried him to the lift.

After that Riki lived on a cushion beside the Director's desk on the fourth floor. Sometimes, when Rei went in, Adachi was brushing him, very slowly, and said "just a moment," and went on brushing, and then sat down behind his desk and was the Director again.

[break What the Director gave him]

On the first Monday of June, Adachi sent for Rei.

He did not say anything at first. He was standing at the window with the worn train pass in his fingers, and Riki was asleep on his cushion with his cast stuck out sideways, snoring.

"Tendo told you he was sorry," said Adachi at last. "Kato wrote it in his report. In his own words. In the one second he had."

"Yes."

"You believe it."

"Yes."

Adachi nodded slowly. He went to his desk, and opened a drawer, and took something out, and put it on the desk in front of Rei.

It was a small black plastic box, about the size of a deck of cards, with rounded corners and a clip on the back. It looked like an old pager, the kind doctors used to carry before phones. On the front, a narrow green screen, dark and empty. Above it, a row of five small round lights. Below it, one larger light. And three small rubber buttons, and a fourth under a little plastic flap that had been taped shut.

"A Spirit Capturer," said Adachi. "Every field officer carries one, after a time. After we trust them." He looked at the box. "It holds one small thing at a time. A spirit. A fragment. You can carry it. You can talk to it, through the screen. The light tells you what it is." He touched the taped flap with one finger. "That button you will not use until you know the name of what you are holding. Never before."

Rei looked at it. He did not pick it up.

"Why now?"

"Because nobody in this building checked Kato," said Adachi. "Except you. And you did not check him. You simply knew." He sat down. "That is either a very good quality in an officer, or a very dangerous one. I have decided it is a good one. For now." He looked at Rei with his pale resting eyes. "There is a limit to what it can hold. It is made for small things. Do not point it at anything large and expect it to help you."

Rei picked it up. It was light, and cold. On his wrist, the latch went cold too, very faintly, and then was still.

"Thank you, sir."

"Don't thank me," said the Director. "Your brother carried one for six years. He said it taught him that most of what comes through the veil only wants to be asked its name." He looked down at the dog. "Go on. And tell Shimizu her team is short one. I am working on it."
