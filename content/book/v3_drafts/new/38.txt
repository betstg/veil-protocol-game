#title The Team

The first rank exam of the year was in the middle of March, in the sub-basement under Containment, in a room the trainees called the Box.

It was a square concrete room with a wall of thick glass on one side and an iron circle set into the floor in the middle. Inside the circle sat something the Division had looked after for twelve years. Outside it, there was nothing but a stool and the cold.

"It's old," said Shimizu, outside the door, on the morning of the exam. "Tame. It's done this a hundred times. It will try to frighten you. It can't leave the circle and it can't touch you." She looked at the line of six trainees in the corridor. "You go in one at a time. You have five minutes. Read it. Calm it. Come out. The examiners watch through the glass." She looked at Rei last. "And nobody does anything brave. Brave is for people who have finished training."

He went in fourth.

[break The Box]

The thing in the iron circle was a woman. Or it had been. It sat on the floor with its knees drawn up, very thin, very pale, in a long white nightdress, with black hair hanging down over its face so that only the chin showed. It was rocking, very slowly, back and forth. It was humming a lullaby, three notes, over and over.

When the door closed behind Rei, it stopped humming. It lifted its head. The hair fell back.

It had no eyes. Only smooth pale skin where the eyes should be.

"You," it said, in a voice like a rusty swing. "You. You have so much room in you."

The latch on his wrist went cold. He did not open the door in his chest any further than a finger's width. He did not know how to do more in a room like this, with examiners behind glass, and he did not try.

So he knelt down on the cold concrete outside the iron circle, at the same height as the thing, and he did what Tanizaki had taught him. He sat. He did not ask for anything. He let it look at him with the eyes it did not have.

Through the finger's width he could feel it. Not much. A woman in a house by a river, a long time ago, with a baby that would not stop crying, and the lullaby she sang over and over until her voice went, and then went on singing in her head. Tired. That was all it was, under the white face and the missing eyes. Tired, and still singing, because nobody had ever told her she could stop.

"You can stop," Rei said quietly. "The baby's asleep."

The thing went still. After a minute it began to hum again. Softer. After three, it put its head down on its knees, and the humming went on, very low, and then it was only breathing.

He walked out at four minutes ten seconds.

On the other side of the glass, the examiners were writing. One of them, a grey-haired man Rei did not know, was frowning at him with a pencil in his mouth. Shimizu was writing too. She did not look up.

His result was posted that afternoon on the board outside the trainee room. *Uedera Rei. Adept.*

One rank up from Postulate.

He stood and looked at it for a long time. He did not know what it meant. He did not know if Gin would be pleased, or if Gin would be frightened, or both. He thought of a small room full of paper cranes, and a woman saying *don't rush*.

[break Five]

The next morning Shimizu called him into her office. It was a small room off the Operations floor with a window onto a light well and a cork board covered in maps. Kato was already there, standing by the window with his hands in his pockets.

So were two people Rei had seen in the corridors and never spoken to.

One was a big man of about twenty-eight, very tall, well over one eighty, with broad shoulders and a big easy grin and his hair sticking up at the back where he had slept on it. He wore a good dark suit with the tie already pulled loose at ten in the morning, and he was sitting with his chair tipped back on two legs and a paper bakery bag on his knee, telling a story to the other one about a pigeon that had got into the canteen. When Rei came in he let the chair fall forward with a bang and stuck out his hand.

"Tendo," he said. "Akira. Field. You're the crow kid." He shook Rei's hand hard. "Cream bun? I bought eight. I never remember how many people there are."

Rei took a cream bun. It seemed rude not to.

The other one was a stocky man in his thirties with tired eyes and stubble and a suit that had been slept in, more than once. Moriya. The one who had checked Itsuki's drawings for doors every night for ninety days, and never once said anything about it. He nodded at Rei and did not get up.

Shimizu put her hands flat on the desk.

"This is a team," she said. "Five. Me. Tendo. Moriya. Kato. Uedera. From today we work together. Field operations. Real ones." She looked at each of them in turn. "Tendo moves things that are heavy and burns things that are bad. Moriya keeps us alive. Kato hears. Uedera reads. I'm in charge." She paused. "The Director asked for this team himself."

Kato went still. Tendo raised his eyebrows.

"Himself?" said Tendo. "That's either very good or very bad."

"Yes," said Shimizu.

Moriya said nothing. He looked at Rei for a moment, the slow complete look that took in the whole of a person, and Rei had the feeling of being weighed, and not found too light.

"There's a sixth," said Rei.

Shimizu looked at him.

"Kuroda Itsuki," said Rei. "He's not Division. He's not registered. He'll never be on a list. But he goes where I go. You can't stop him. I've tried." He swallowed. "And he sees things in rooms that none of us do. With a pencil."

Shimizu looked at him for a long time. Then at Kato. Kato shrugged.

"He's right," said Kato. "About all of it. Especially the part about not being able to stop him."

"The boy from Room Nine," said Moriya. It was the first thing he had said. "The one who draws." He picked up a pen. "He's not on any list I have to sign."

"He's not on any list," said Shimizu. "He doesn't come into the building past the lobby. He doesn't touch Division equipment. If something happens to him, it didn't happen on my team." She paused. "But if he's standing outside a building we go into, I'm not going to tell him to go home." She looked at Rei. "Six, then. Five and a painter."

Tendo laughed out loud. "Five and a painter," he said. "I love that. That's our name now. I'm going to put it on a cake."

[break First case]

"First case," said Shimizu. She pushed a thin file across the desk.

Rei opened it. A photograph of a small old temple, with a tiled roof and a wooden bell tower, in a narrow street of old houses. *Jōkō-ji. Takinogawa, Kita. Resident priest Rev. Egami Jakū.* A list of dates. A list of names.

"Something has been in that temple since the autumn," said Shimizu. "It started with the bell. Ringing by itself at dusk. Then the candles going out. Then the parishioners. Old people who came to pray for their dead. Three of them have gone home from the temple and not woken up. Nobody linked it until last month."

"A Grid team went in in January," said Kato quietly. "Two people. They came out and wouldn't go back. I heard it at the counter."

Shimizu looked at him. She did not ask how he heard.

"A Division team went in in February," she said. "Two officers. One came out. The other is in Room Twelve in Containment, and he hasn't said a word since." She closed the file. "Reverend Egami has refused to leave. He sleeps in the temple. He says it's his temple and his parishioners and he is not going to be frightened out of it by something that rings his bell." She stood up. "He has asked the Division for help three times. We are the help. Tomorrow night. Seven o'clock. Bring everything."

They went out. In the corridor, Tendo put an arm round Kato's shoulders and one round Rei's and walked them both toward the lifts, talking about the pigeon again, and how it had got into the kitchen and stolen a whole fried chicken cutlet off a plate and flown away with it and been chased by three cooks with ladles. Kato did not shrug the arm off. Rei noticed that. Kato shrugged off everybody.

At the lifts Tendo let them go and went off down the corridor whistling, with his bakery bag swinging.

"He's nice," said Rei.

"He's very nice." Kato took the ivory knight out of his pocket and turned it over. "He thinks about food more than anybody I've ever met. Mostly food. Some pigeons." A strand of hair had come loose over his forehead. "Nobody buys the telepath a bun."

"He bought you a bun."

"He did." Kato looked at the knight in his hand. "I'm going to have to think about that."

That night Rei sent Itsuki a message.

*we've got a team. five and a painter. you're the painter.*

The answer came in nine seconds.

*obviously i'm the painter. who else would be the painter. when do we start.*

*tomorrow. a temple. seven pm.*

*i'll bring pencils. and snacks.*

Then, a minute later, *five and a painter. that's the worst name i've ever heard. i love it.*

Rei put the phone face down on the table and sat in the dark kitchen for a while, with two cups of tea, one on each side, and did not feel quite so much like the only person at it.
