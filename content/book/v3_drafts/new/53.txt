#title Hinata and Ren

The bakery was in Kōenji, on a narrow shopping street under the railway, between a second-hand record shop and a shop that sold only socks with cats on them. It had a striped awning and a window full of melon bread and cream buns, and a hand-painted sign on the door that said *Thank you for coming today!* with a drawing of a smiling rabbit.

Rei went because the young man behind the counter had asked him to.

He had come into the Division on a Tuesday in early June, in an oversized knitted jumper and a tote bag with a book in it, and sat down on the bench outside the trainee room, and waited three hours for Rei to come back from the field. When Rei came up the stairs, the young man stood up at once, with a warm, shy, slightly apologetic smile, as if he were sorry for taking up the space on the bench.

"Uedera Rei?" he said. "Sorry. Sorry to bother you. I'm Usami. From the Vessel Program. Could you come to the bakery on Thursday? After closing?" He hesitated. "We'd like to ask you something."

*We.*

Kato, coming up the stairs behind Rei, stopped dead. Rei felt him go still.

"Two," Kato said, very quietly, after the young man had gone. "Two layers. Like the cat man. Like Egami." He frowned. "No. Not like them. They're not fighting. They're..." He shook his head slowly. "I've never heard anything like it. It's like listening to two people finish each other's sentences inside one head. They're *nice*, Rei. Both of them."

[need ch53_bakery | A tiny bakery in Kōenji after closing, striped awning, window of melon bread, a slight soft-featured young man in an oversized knit jumper wiping the counter, a white mug with a blue stripe beside the till, Rei sitting on a stool with a cup of tea]

The bakery closed at seven. Rei came at ten past. The young man let him in and locked the door behind him and turned the sign round, and then stood there for a moment in the warm empty shop, smelling of butter and sugar, with his hands in the pockets of his jumper.

He was twenty-four. Slight and soft-featured, with dark brown hair a little too long and warm brown eyes. He had the kind of face people liked before they decided to. When he looked at you, his eyes turned first, and his head followed half a second later, as if two people were looking out of the same face and one of them was always a little quicker.

"Tea?" he said. "I make it in this." He held up a plain white ceramic mug with a blue stripe round it. "It was his favourite. I don't like coffee much, but I use it for tea every morning. I don't know why." He smiled. "I do know why. I just don't like to say."

Rei opened the door in his chest. A finger's width. Then two.

And saw them.

Not one person. Two. Sitting in the same body, side by side, like two people sharing a bench. One of them was warm and bright and curious, reaching out toward Rei the way a plant reaches for light, wanting to know him, wanting to be near him, wanting. It was not grey like Kishin, or hungry like the thing on the river. It was soft and pink and golden, like the inside of a shell. A demon. Rei had no doubt of it. But a demon that felt like a hand on your arm.

The other was very faint. Far back. Very still. Like a person asleep in the back room of a house, behind a closed door, while someone else answers the phone.

"You can see us," said the young man softly. "Both. Can't you."

"Yes."

"Good." He put down the mug. "Then I'll tell you properly. I'm Ren. I'm the demon. I live in Usami Hinata's body." He said it simply, like a man telling you his job. "And he's Hinata. He's in the back room. He doesn't come out much any more." He paused. "That's what we wanted to ask you about."

[break What happened]

They sat at the little table by the window with tea. Ren told it slowly, turning the white mug in his hands.

"Hinata was a graduate student," he said. "Sociology. He studied how people find each other in big cities. How strangers become friends. He was very good at it." He smiled. "He was very good at people. Everyone loved him. Everyone at the university, everyone at this bakery, he worked here part-time, everyone on this street." The smile went. "And when he was twenty-two, they found something in his blood. A cancer. Fast. They said a year. Maybe less."

Rei did not say anything.

"He didn't want to die," said Ren. "He was so frightened. He was twenty-two and he had so many things he wanted to do and so many people he loved. And somebody at the hospital, a very kind doctor, told him quietly about a programme. The Vessel Program. For people who can carry something from the other side. A partnership, they said. Cohabitation. A demon lives in you. In return, by contract, it keeps your body alive."

"And you were the demon."

"I was the demon." Ren looked out of the window at the dark street. "I was very small. A Class C. Love, which is the register I'm made of. I'd been on the far side a long time, wanting. Love is wanting, mostly, at the bottom. Wanting to be near. Wanting to be known. Wanting to be part of something warm. I wanted to know what it was like from the inside. The way you all have it." He turned the mug. "So I signed."

"What did the contract say?"

Ren's eyes came to Rei first. Then his face.

"*Ren will keep the body of Usami Hinata alive,*" he said. "*Ren will keep Usami Hinata safe.*" He said it the way you say something you have said ten thousand times. "That's all. Two lines. Hinata signed them. I signed them. The Program witnessed them."

Rei thought of Shimizu on the station steps. *Read every single word. Twice. And then read what it didn't write.*

He thought of Fujimoto Kenji in his cold flat full of paper. *You will never be alone again.*

"Safe," he said slowly.

"Yes." Ren looked down at the mug. "Do you see? I keep my word. I can't not. It's what I am. *Alive* was easy. I hold the body together. The cancer is still there, it's always there, but I hold it still, so it can't do anything. Every day. Every second. The Program's records say Usami Hinata is clinically dead. They're right. Without me, he would be. If I left, he'd die within the hour." He swallowed. "But *safe*..."

He stopped. His hand was tight on the mug.

"Safe from what?" said Rei gently.

"From everything," said Ren. "That's what I thought it meant. Safe from fear. Safe from pain. Safe from dying. So I put him somewhere safe. In the back room. Where nothing can reach him. Where he doesn't have to feel the cancer, or the fear, or anything." His voice cracked. "It's been two years. He's been in the back room for two years. Safe. And I've been out here wearing his jumpers and making his tea in his mug and working his shifts and loving his friends, and they love me back, and I don't know..."

He stopped again.

"I don't know if it's real," he said. "What I feel. I don't know if I love them, or if I'm just a love-shaped hunger wearing a nice person. And I can't ask him. Because I put him somewhere safe. And I promised. And I can't take him out of the safe place without breaking the contract." He looked at Rei. His warm brown eyes were wet. "Mori told us you could see people's rivers. Underneath. Right to the bottom. And that you found your brother, locked in a room, through a key." He put the mug down. "Can you find Hinata? Can you ask him? Please?"

[break The back room]

Rei had never done it before. He had only seen Mio do it, with Gin's key, by the river in Kyoto.

He held out his hand across the table. "The mug," he said. "Give me the mug."

Ren gave it to him. The white ceramic, warm from the tea. The blue stripe.

Rei lifted the latch.

He went in slowly. Not into Ren. Past Ren. Through the warm golden pink of the demon, which let him pass, the way a crowd lets you through when you say *excuse me*. Down into the body, down past the place where Ren sat, into the back. Holding the mug. Following it. The mug was Hinata's. It had his memories in it. Choosing it in a shop in Kichijōji. Drinking from it at three in the morning writing his thesis. Holding it in both hands the morning the doctor called.

At the back of the house, there was a door. Closed. Not locked. Just closed. As if someone had pulled it to very gently, so as not to wake the person inside.

Rei knocked.

"*Hinata?*"

For a long time, nothing.

Then a voice. Faint. Far away. Sleepy. A young man's voice, warm and rough, like someone woken from a long nap on a sofa on a Sunday afternoon.

"*...who's there?*"

"*My name's Rei. I'm a friend. Ren asked me to come.*"

"*Ren.*" The voice was a little clearer. "*Is Ren all right?*"

Rei felt something go through him that was not his. He did not know if it was Hinata's or his own.

"*Ren's fine,*" he said. "*Ren's working your shifts. Ren's using your mug. Ren's very worried about you.*"

A long pause. Then something that might have been a laugh.

"*Of course he is,*" said Hinata. "*He's always worried. He's the most worried demon in the world.*" The voice was waking up now. "*How long have I been asleep?*"

"*Two years.*"

Silence. A long, long silence.

"*Oh,*" said Hinata quietly. "*That's a long nap.*"

[break What Hinata said]

They talked through the closed door for a long time. Rei did not know how long. Somewhere very far away, in a bakery in Kōenji, his body sat at a table holding a white mug, and Ren sat across from it, watching, and did not move.

Hinata was not angry. Rei had been afraid he would be. He was not. He was sleepy, and sad, and surprised, and very gentle.

"*I asked him to keep me safe,*" he said. "*I was so scared. I was so scared of dying. I didn't know what I was asking. Neither did he.*" A pause. "*He did exactly what I asked. Of course he did. He's a demon. They do exactly what you ask.*"

"*He wants to know if it's real,*" said Rei. "*What he feels. For your friends. For the bakery. For you. He thinks he might just be hunger wearing a nice person.*"

The silence behind the door was different this time.

"*Can you open the door?*" said Hinata. "*Just a bit. I want to see him.*"

Rei put his hand on the door, very gently, and pushed it open a crack.

And through the crack, Hinata looked out. Through his own eyes. For the first time in two years. At his own bakery. At the window full of melon bread. At his own mug, in a stranger's hands, across the table. And at the demon wearing his body, sitting very still, with its hands clenched together on the table and its face wet.

Rei felt him see it. Felt him see everything at once. The two years. The shifts. The friends. The tea in the mug every morning. The jumpers worn gently, the way he wore them. The way Ren had not corrected the soft apologetic way he sat, the way he turned his eyes before his head. The way Ren had kept everything of his. Exactly as it was. Like someone keeping a room for a person they love, who is away.

"*Oh, Ren,*" said Hinata softly. "*You idiot. Of course it's real.*"

Across the table, Ren made a sound. A small, broken, astonished sound.

He had heard. It was his body. Of course he had heard.

[break The promise]

Afterward, they sat in the bakery with the lights low and the tea cold. Rei's head was pounding. His nose was bleeding, a little, onto a paper napkin. He did not care.

"He doesn't want to come out," said Ren. His voice was hoarse. "Not all the way. Not yet. He says he's not ready. He says he wants to sleep a bit more, but with the door open now. So he can hear. So he can look out sometimes." He wiped his face with the sleeve of the jumper. "He says when he's ready, he'll tell me. And then I'll let go. And he'll go. Properly. Not frightened. The way he wanted to, if he hadn't been so scared."

"And you?"

Ren looked at the mug for a long time.

"I'm going to make a promise," he said.

Rei looked at him sharply. Ren saw the look and almost smiled.

"I know," he said. "I know what it means. I know we don't. I've never made one in my life except the contract. Promises are chains. We keep them to the letter, forever, even if they tear us apart." He turned the white mug round in his hands, slowly, once. "That's why I want to."

He closed his eyes.

"*I, Ren, promise Usami Hinata,*" he said. His voice was quiet and clear and very steady. "*That I will keep the door open. That I will let him look out whenever he wants. And that on the day he asks me to let go, I will let go. On that day. Not one day later. Even if I don't want to.*" He opened his eyes. "*Even if it's the worst thing I ever do.*"

The air in the bakery changed. Rei felt it. A weight settling. A chain closing. Something in the veil, very small and very heavy, clicking into place like the latch on his wrist.

Ren breathed out. He looked tired. He looked, Rei thought, more like a person than Rei had ever seen a demon look.

"There," he said. "Now I have to."

"Why?" said Rei. "Why did you want to?"

Ren picked up the mug and looked into it. At the cold tea.

"Because that's what you do," he said. "Isn't it? When it's real. You promise. Even when it's going to hurt." He looked up at Rei, eyes first, then head. "Hinata taught me that. He did it all the time. To his friends. To his mum. To strangers in the street. Small ones. Big ones. He broke half of them and made them again the next day." He smiled, crookedly. "I can't do that. I only get one. So I made it a good one."

Rei did not trust his voice. He drank his cold tea.

At the door, when he was leaving, Ren stopped him with a hand on his arm. Warm. Gentle. Ren touched people a lot. Hinata had, too.

"Your brother," said Ren. "The thing that's in him. Mori says it's Duty. Old. Polite. That it hasn't hurt him." He hesitated. "Things like us, Rei. Demons. We're not good. We do what we want, the second we want it. But we're not all the same." He let go of Rei's arm. "If it's been careful with him for nine months, without a contract, without a promise... then something in it wants to be careful. And that's something you can talk to."

He turned the sign on the door back round. *Thank you for coming today!* The smiling rabbit.

"Come back," said Ren. "Hinata would like to meet you properly. When he's awake. He's very good at people."
