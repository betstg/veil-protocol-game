#title Proposal

He woke at 3:47 because the Capturer was beeping.

Not loudly. A small dry electronic chirp, once every few seconds, like a smoke alarm with a dying battery in a flat two floors down. He lay on the sofa with his foot hanging off the end and listened to it for a long time before he understood what it was.

The Capturer was on the kitchen table, where he had dropped it with his keys and his wallet and the bloody tissue from his nose when Kato brought him home. It was lying face up. The narrow green screen was lit. Under it, the single round goodness light was glowing, soft and steady, the way it had glowed the first time, at the green phone box by the bus stop, with Shimizu standing beside him in the cold.

And the small rubber button that said TALK was lit too. Nobody had pressed it.

Gorō was standing on the table beside it, very still, with his feathers flat and his head low and his beak an inch from the screen. He was not making a sound. Rei had never seen him so quiet.

Rei got up. He did not turn on the light. He went to the table in the dark in his socks and looked down at the screen.

There were letters on it. Slow. One at a time. The way the screen always wrote, as if someone very small were pressing very hard on a very old typewriter. But these were not the clumsy, broken letters of a frightened spirit. They were neat. Spaced. Careful. Like the handwriting of someone who had learned to write a long time ago, from a strict teacher, with a brush.

*good evening.*

Rei stood very still.

*please do not turn on the light. please do not come to the window. i am on the roof of the building across the road. if you come to the window, i will go. i would rather not go. i have come a long way to knock.*

[break Knock]

He sat down at the kitchen table in Gin's chair because his legs did it without asking him.

The latch on his wrist was cold, but not burning. Cold the way the walkway was cold at night in winter. He put his hand over it. He did not lift it.

Gorō did not move. His blue eye, the one that saw things, was fixed on the window. On the dark beyond it. On the flat black roof of the building opposite, where the water tanks stood and the air-conditioning units hummed and nobody ever went.

Rei picked up the Capturer. His hands were not steady.

"I know who you are," he said. Very quietly. Not loud enough for anyone across the road to hear. He did not need it to be. "I know what you are."

The screen was blank for a long moment. Then the letters came again, slowly.

*you know what i am. you do not know who. that is all right. not many do. fewer every century.*

*i have been watching you since the walkway. you know that also. you are not stupid. your brother told me you were not stupid, many times, in many ways, usually when i was doing something stupid myself.*

Rei's throat closed.

He looked at the black window without going to it. "Is he there?"

*yes.*

Rei pressed the box against his chest for a second, then held it out again where he could read it. "Can he hear me?"

*yes. he is listening very hard. he is always listening when it is you.* A pause. The goodness light flickered. *he says you have not been eating. he says the rice cooker is full and you are thin. he says you have been sleeping on the sofa with your foot off the end, which he told you to stop doing when you were twelve. he says stop it.*

Rei put his free hand over his mouth.

He sat there in the dark with the Capturer in one hand and his other hand over his mouth, and Gorō stood beside him on the table, and across the road on a roof in the dark something old and polite wearing his brother's body waited for him to be able to breathe again. It did not hurry him. It did not write anything at all.

When he could, he took his hand away.

"Tell him," he said. His voice was wrecked. "Tell him I'll eat when he comes home and cooks it."

There was a very long pause.

*he is laughing,* said the screen. *i do not know why that is funny. he says it is funny because it is exactly what you would say. he says you are an idiot.* Another pause. *he says it the way he says it to you. i have heard the difference. it took me some time.*

[break What it wanted]

Rei wiped his face with the back of his wrist, latch and all. "Why are you here?"

*to ask.*

The word sat on the green screen by itself for a long time.

*i have never asked anyone for anything. i want you to understand that before i go on. i do not say it to be pitied. i say it so that you understand what this is. where i come from, everything is a debt. everything is owed and everything is paid. i take what i am owed. i pay what i owe. i do not ask. asking is a thing that small creatures do, creatures with no claim. i have had a claim on everything i ever touched.*

*your brother says that is the saddest thing he has ever heard. i did not understand him for a long time.*

*i am here to ask.*

Rei held the Capturer and waited.

*you went into her house today. you went into her house and you sat on her floor and she put her hand on yours and offered you tea, and you did not drink it. i was on the roof of the bath house at the end of her street. i felt her open the far shore under you. i felt you shut it.* A pause. *you should not have been able to shut it. not at your rank. not with her hand on yours. your brother could not have done it at your age. i am not sure he could do it now.*

*i felt you do it, and i decided to knock.*

Rei looked at the window. At the dark. He did not go to it.

"She offered me Gin," he said. "She said you owe her. She said she could make you give him back."

The screen was blank for a long time. The goodness light dimmed and brightened, dimmed and brightened, like slow breathing.

*that is true. and it is a lie.*

*it is true that i owe her. a very old debt. older than this city. i will not tell you what it is tonight. it is not a good story and you are tired.*

*it is true that she could call it in. and if she called it in, i would have to pay. that is what i am. i cannot be otherwise. if she said, give me the brother, i would have to give him.*

*it is a lie that she would give him to you.*

Rei's hand tightened on the box.

*she does not give. she has never given anything. she takes, the way i take, but she does not pay. she has eaten everything that was ever put in front of her, every grief, every lonely child, the woman whose face she is wearing, and she has never once paid for any of it. she would take your brother from me and she would put him on her wall with the others. and she would take you, in the chair, and she would hold your debt, and through you she would hold the thirteenth seat. and the current would run the wrong way.*

*that is what she wants. not you. not him. the chair. she has wanted the chairs for a long time. she has six of them in her mind already, the ones whose holders are weak or tired or dying. she is taking the grey one now. you know which.*

Watanabe. The thread running west. The grey up to his wrists.

Rei nodded at the screen, as if it could see him. "Yes."

*with yours she would have seven. with seven, on the night when the veil is thinnest, she can open the far shore and leave it open. and everything that is on the other side will come across. not a few. all of it.*

*your brother saw this. last july. in her white room. he sat on her floor for an hour and listened to her and he saw it. and when he came out of her door, i was watching, because i had felt it too, and he was the only one who had ever seen it, and i needed him. three weeks later, on the night before the dead came home, i was waiting for him on your walkway.*

*he did not agree. i want you to know that. i want you to know that he said no. i did not ask. i did not know how to ask then. i took him. it was not a good thing. he has told me so every day for eleven months.*

[break Please]

Rei sat in the dark. The fridge hummed. A motorbike went past at the bottom of the hill, far away, and was gone.

He set the Capturer down on the table in front of him, so he would not have to hold it. "What do you want from me."

The letters came very slowly this time. Slower than they had all night. As if each one cost something.

*i want to end her. i cannot do it alone. not in her house. not with her herd round her, the hollow ones, the ones who have drunk the tea. not before the night the veil is thin. she is stronger there than i am. she has been eating for a very long time.*

*i need someone who can stand in her house and not drink. i need someone who can shut the far shore when she opens it. i need someone the six chairs will listen to. i need someone the light at the centre has looked at.*

*i felt it look at you. on the night you lost a day. it has not looked at anyone in four hundred years. it looked at you.*

*so i am asking.*

The screen went blank. Then, one letter at a time, so slowly that Rei counted the seconds between them, a word he had never seen on that screen before. Perhaps nothing had ever written it there, in all the years Gin had carried the box.

*please.*

Gorō made a sound. Very small. Not a caw. The sound a crow makes in its throat when it is alone and thinks no one is listening.

[break The terms]

Rei did not answer for a long time.

He looked round the dark kitchen. At the hook by the door. *Come home.* At the window frame, with its layers of grey. *Not here. Not this one. Not him.* At the rice cooker. *Eat.* At Gin's chair, which he was sitting in. At the sofa where he slept with his foot off the end.

"Not to kill," he said at last. "I'm not helping you kill anybody. She's wearing a woman. The woman was a person once. I'll help you stop her. That's all."

*the woman is gone. she put the lights out one by one. there is nobody left to save in that body.*

Rei shook his head at the screen. "Then we find that out when we get there. Not before."

The pause was very long.

*your brother is laughing again. he says this is what it is like. he says get used to it.* Another pause. *agreed. stop her.*

Rei held up one finger, in the dark, to nobody. "And Yua comes out first. Before anything. Before you do anything to that house. She comes out."

*the girl.*

Rei leaned closer to the green light. "Yes."

*she is a null. did you know. there is nothing in her for the far shore to open. she cannot be eaten the way the others are eaten. that is why the thing in the soft dress is so interested in her. it is trying to teach her to want to be eaten. it is very patient. it has been very kind to her. nobody has ever been kind to her.*

*she is holding on. i felt her today too. she is counting.*

Rei's eyes stung. "I know."

*agreed. the girl first.*

"And when it's done," said Rei. His voice was very steady now. Steadier than it had been all night. "When she's gone. When there's no debt left. You give him back."

The goodness light went dim. So dim that for a moment Rei thought the Capturer had died. Then it came back, very faintly.

*i cannot promise that.*

Rei's thumb found the edge of the button. "Then I can't..."

The letters came faster than they had all night, cutting across him.

*listen. please. i am not refusing. i am telling you the truth because he is listening and he will know if i lie. i cannot promise that because i do not know what i will be when it is done. i do not know if there will be a door to open. i do not know if he will want to come through it. i do not know what it does to a man to be worn for a year, from the inside. no one has ever come back from it to tell me.*

*i can promise this. when it is done, if i can open the door, i will open it. and if i cannot, i will tell you why, and i will stay and try for as long as it takes. that is a debt. i am telling you that i am taking it on. freely. i have never done that either.*

Rei read it twice. Three times. The green letters swam.

He put his thumb on the edge of the screen, very lightly, the way you touch someone's sleeve. "Okay."

*okay.*

Rei picked the box up again. "What do I call you?"

The screen stayed blank for a long time.

*not yet. names are debts, where i come from. a name given is a claim. i will give you mine when i have paid for it. not before.*

*your brother calls me many things. most of them i will not write on this screen. sometimes, when he is tired, he calls me the old man. i do not mind that one.*

[break Gone]

The goodness light faded slowly, the way a coal fades. The TALK button went dark.

Then, very faintly, one more line.

*eat something. he means it. so do i, i think. i am not sure. it is a strange thing to mean something that is not owed.*

The screen went blank. The Capturer went cold in his hand.

Gorō leaped off the table, across the kitchen, onto the windowsill, and pressed his whole body against the glass with his wings half spread, staring at the roof across the road.

Rei did not go to the window. He had said he would not.

He sat in Gin's chair until the sky over the water tanks went grey, and then pink, and then a hard flat white, and the first train went past at the bottom of the hill, and somewhere in the building a radio came on with the weather.

Then he got up and opened the rice cooker. There was rice in it, two days old, dry at the edges. He put some in a bowl and poured hot water from the kettle over it and put an umeboshi on top, the way Gin did on mornings when there was nothing else, and ate the whole thing standing at the counter, looking at the wall.

At seven, he rang Itsuki.

"I'm telling you first," he said, when Itsuki picked up, before Itsuki could say anything. "He came. Last night. The thing in Gin. He came to the roof across the road and he knocked."

There was a long silence on the line.

Itsuki's voice came back flat with shock. "He *knocked*."

"He asked." Rei looked down at the empty bowl. "He said please."

Itsuki did not say anything for so long that Rei thought the call had dropped. Then he heard the click of a lighter, and a long breath out.

"I'm coming over," said Itsuki. "Don't do anything. Don't agree to anything. Don't..." He stopped, and Rei heard him breathe out smoke. "You already agreed to something. Didn't you."

Rei looked at the window, where Gorō was still standing on the sill, still staring at the empty roof.

He looked away from the window. "Yes."

"Fuck," said Itsuki, with feeling. "Fuck. Okay. Put the kettle on. I'm bringing Kato. And I'm bringing a fucking big jar of iron."
