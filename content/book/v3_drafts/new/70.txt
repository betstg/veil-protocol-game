#title What the Director Knows

The Director sent for him on Tuesday afternoon.

Not by email. Not by telephone. His secretary came down to the third floor in person, a small neat woman in a grey cardigan whom Rei had seen perhaps twice in nine months, and stood beside his desk until he looked up, and said, *the Director would like to see you now, Uedera-san, please bring nothing*, and walked away.

He went up in the lift with his hands empty.

The eighth floor was the quietest place in the building. Thick carpet. Wood panelling gone dark with age. A long corridor of closed doors with no names on them, and at the end, a door that was open.

Adachi was standing at the window, the way he always stood, with his back to the room and the worn transit card turning in his fingers.

The card did not stop turning. "Close the door, Uedera. Sit down."

Rei closed the door and sat.

Adachi did not turn round. He stood at the window and looked out over Ōji, over the station and the tracks and the river beyond, grey-green in the heat, and the card turned, and turned, and turned.

"Moriya filed a record on Monday morning," he said at last. "I read it on Monday night. I've read it four times since." The card stopped. "It's very accurate."

Rei did not say anything.

"I'm going to tell you some things," said Adachi. "Most of them I've never told anyone. Some of them I should have told your brother three years ago, and didn't, because I was being careful." He turned round. His face was grey and tired and older than Rei remembered from the winter, as if someone had been pressing on it from the inside for a long time. "Being careful is a thing I'm very good at. It's how I've stayed in this office for twelve years. It's also the reason your brother is wearing a demon and an old man is dead."

[break The roof]

He sat down behind his desk. That was strange in itself. Rei had never once seen him sit down.

"I was a field officer," said Adachi. "Before this. Before the suit and the eighth floor. Vanguard. I was quite good." He set the transit card on the desk in front of him, very precisely, square to the edge. "Twelve years ago, I was on a roof in Kita, in the rain, at two in the morning, on a case that had gone badly wrong. A family on the floor below, and something in the walls, and I'd been holding it back for an hour and I was nearly done. And a man came up the fire escape."

He looked at the card.

"An old man. Very ordinary. A cardigan. Slippers, I think. He walked across the roof in the rain, and put his hand on my shoulder, and the thing in the walls stopped. Just stopped. As if somebody had told it to go home and it had remembered it had one." Adachi's voice did not change. "He told me what he was. He told me there were thirteen of them. He told me which one he held. He told me he had come because he could not stand to watch a man hold a door alone for that long when he could help."

Rei had gone very still.

"Duty," said Adachi. "He held Duty. For forty years. From a little flat behind the Akabane shopping street, with a cat and a garden on the balcony and a job doing the accounts for a tofu shop." He almost smiled. "Accounts. Of course."

He picked up the card again.

"I retired from the field that winter. I couldn't do it any more. Not after knowing. I'd spent eight years trying to make the Division into something better from the bottom. I've spent twelve since trying from the top. Something that might be fit for the people he'd told me about. It isn't. But it's closer." The card turned. "And I visited him. Once a month. For five years. We drank tea. He never told me anything I asked. He told me a great many things I didn't."

Rei heard his own voice come out very small. "He died. In 2009."

"In 2009." Adachi's eyes did not move from Rei's face. "I was there. In the little flat. With the cat. He'd been ill for a long time and he'd told nobody. He rang me at four in the morning and said *Kenshin, come and sit with me*, and I went." The card stopped. "When a holder dies, the seat looks for someone with the right shape. Someone in the room. Someone near. That's how it passes. That's how it always passed. He told me that, once, years before, as if it were a thing about the weather."

He was quiet for a moment.

"I sat with him. I held his hand. And when he went, I felt it. The seat. Looking. Going round the little room like a dog that's lost its owner. Sniffing at the cat. At the curtains. At me." His voice was very level. "And it went past me. I wasn't the right shape. I'm Rage, mostly. A little Fear. I'm a great many things, but I'm not that. Nobody in that room was. And it went out of the window, and up, and it has been dark ever since."

He put the card down.

"Something has been holding it from underneath for seven years. I've known that. I've felt Kita going wrong. I've watched the thin spots open. I've read every report. And I've been waiting for someone with the right shape to walk into this building." He looked at Rei. "Seven years ago, one did. It took me four to see it."

[break Gin]

"Your brother," said Adachi, "came into this building at twenty-two and sat the assessment and the machines said he was a Ritualist. Middling. Reliable. Nothing remarkable." The card turned once. "I watched him for a year. On cases. Through the Mirror Room. Once, in person, from across a street in Ōtsuka, while he talked a frightened woman out of a cupboard. And I knew. Not what he was. What he could be. He was Vanguard, at least, under all that. Pressed down flat. Suppressing it himself, every day, for reasons he never told me. And under that, something else. Something with a shape I'd seen once before, on a roof in the rain."

Rei could not breathe properly.

"I knew," said Adachi. "Three years ago, I knew. I should have called him up here and closed the door and said *I know what you are, and I know what you could be, and there is a chair in an old library in Ochanomizu with dust on the seat*. I should have done it that week." His voice did not rise. It went lower. "I didn't. I was being careful. I thought there was time. I thought, let him come to me. Let him find it himself. It's better if he finds it himself."

He looked down at his hands on the desk.

"Last July he came up to this floor and asked my secretary for an appointment. She gave him the first Monday in September. I didn't move it. I had a budget hearing." He turned the card over. "He was taken on the twelfth of August."

The room was very quiet. Outside, very faintly, a train went over the bridge.

"I'm not telling you this so you'll forgive me," said Adachi. "I don't want forgiving. I'm telling you because you ought to know what the man at the top of this building knew, and when, and what he did about it. Which was nothing." He set the card down square again. "That's what I know. That's all of it. Now you know it too."

[break What Rei said]

Rei sat in the chair across the desk and looked at the Director for a long time.

He thought about the radio in the evidence archive. *Never accuse on a radio's word alone.* He thought about Katagiri on her stool, with her cold tea. He thought about Hagiwara at her window, with her fist closing and opening. He thought about Tanizaki, on the step, holding out a cup.

*You get one attempt,* Gin's book said, in the margin beside the Director's name. *And he will let you make it, and he will not say anything when you finish.*

He did not lie.

"I'm working with it," he said. "The thing in Gin. It has a name. It gave it to me on Saturday. It's going to help us stop the thing in Asagaya, on the thirteenth, at the old freight yard past Tabata. And then it's going to try to give him back." He kept his hands on his knees. "Mori knows. Chen Lian knows. Moriya knows some of it. Shimizu doesn't. Okada doesn't. Nobody upstairs knows." He met Adachi's eyes. "You can stop it if you want to. You can stop all of it. You've got the authority. I'm telling you anyway."

Adachi looked at him.

That look. Fast and complete. The look that seemed to finish before it started. It went over Rei's face and down and up and through, and Rei sat in it and let it, the way he had sat in Keiko's look at the Café Asahi, all the way down, like the front page of a newspaper.

The card stopped turning.

"Your brother never lied to me," said Adachi slowly. "Not once in six years. I told you that, in the winter." He put the card down. "You've just told me you've made an alliance with a top-grade demon to fight another top-grade demon in an unregistered operation on the first night of Obon in my district, without the knowledge of your field supervisor, your training officer, or the Classification Board." He paused. "And you've told me to my face, in my office, knowing I could end your career with one phone call."

Rei did not look away. "Yes."

Adachi leaned forward, very slightly, both hands flat on the desk. "Why?"

Rei thought about it.

"Because Gin had an appointment," he said. "For the first Monday in September. And he never got to keep it." He swallowed. "So I'm keeping it for him. A month early."

[break The network]

Adachi was silent for a very long time.

Then he got up. He went to the window. He stood with his back to the room, and Rei watched his shoulders, and saw them go down, very slightly, the way shoulders go down when a man has been carrying something heavy for a long time and has just been told he can put one corner of it on a table.

"There are people," said Adachi, without turning round, "who used to work in this building. Who retired, or left, or were moved, or were never on the books in the first place. They're not in any database. They're not on any register. They don't answer to the Classification Board or to Marunouchi or to the ninth floor." He paused. "They answer, occasionally, to me. Not because they owe me. Because I asked them once, a long time ago, if they would be willing to be asked."

He took a phone out of his inside pocket. Not his Division phone. An old one. A folding one, grey, scratched, the kind nobody had carried for ten years.

"Some of them are very old. Some of them are very strong. One of them is thirty-four, and stronger than anybody I've ever met, and has been teaching himself something he isn't supposed to be able to do, and told only me." He opened the phone. "He's stood across rooms from your brother this year. At functions. At hearings. He's never said a word to me about what he saw. But I've watched his face when he looked, and I know that face. It's the face of a man who's seen something wrong and been told to wait." Adachi's thumb rested on the keys. "I'm very tired of telling people to wait."

He pressed a number. Held the phone to his ear. Waited.

Somebody answered.

"Kurosawa," said Adachi. "It's me. I'm asking." A pause. "The thirteenth. The old freight yard past Tabata, by the river. The meeting hour." Another pause, longer. "Yes. That one. Bring whoever will come." A pause. "No. Not an order. I don't give you orders. I'm asking." He listened. Something moved at the corner of his mouth. "Thank you."

He closed the phone.

He turned round. He looked at Rei.

"I can't stop Hagiwara," he said. "Not yet. Moriya's record is a start. It's not enough. Her money comes from people I can't touch, and her law is written in a way I can't break without breaking the Division with it. But I can do this." He put the old phone back in his pocket. "On the thirteenth, the Division's field teams will be deployed to every Obon festival in Kita, Toshima, and Arakawa, on public safety duty, as they are every year. Every single one of them. Marunouchi's teams too, if Hagiwara wants to argue with me in front of the ministry about why they shouldn't be." He folded his hands behind his back. "There will be nobody from this building anywhere near the old freight yard past Tabata. Nobody official. Nobody who has to write a report about what they see." He paused. "And if anybody asks me, afterwards, what happened there, I will tell them I was at a budget hearing."

Rei stood up. He did not know what to do with his hands. He bowed. Low. Lower than he had ever bowed to anyone in the building.

"Don't," said Adachi. "Don't thank me. You'll embarrass us both."

He turned back to the window. The transit card had come out of his pocket again, and it turned, and turned, in his fingers.

The card turned once. "Uedera."

Rei stopped at the door.

"When you see him," said Adachi, without turning round. "Your brother. When he's himself. Tell him the appointment's still open." The card stopped. "Tell him I moved the budget hearing."
