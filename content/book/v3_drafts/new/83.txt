#title Six Truths

The ring started shaking at a quarter to three in the morning.

Rei felt it before anyone told him. He was standing at the edge of the grey beach with the river coming up black and ordinary through the fog, and the hum from somewhere far below went up half a note, like a fridge humming higher when somebody opens the door. Then the twelve lights in the white flared. All of them at once. Hoshino's kitchen fire. Mori's hard orange coal. Mio's grey sea. The gentle library light where Watanabe had been was gone, but the chair was still warm, and even that warmth jumped.

Behind him, in the weeds, Kato's phone rang.

Kato answered it with one hand. The other was still holding Shimizu's, and he had not let go of it for two hours, and Rei did not think he was going to. He listened. His face went the colour of paper.

He held the phone out across the weeds toward Rei. "It's Chen Lian. She says the room's moving."

Rei walked back to the gate and took it. "Chen Lian."

"The table's walking." Her voice was very calm, as only very frightened people manage. "Across the floor. By itself. Hoshino's got both hands on it like it's a dog that wants to go out. The lamps are swinging. Mio says the river in Kyoto has stopped making any noise at all." She breathed. "Something's moving under all of us, Uedera. Something that has never moved in any account I have ever read."

Rei turned his head toward the river. "The light."

There was a silence on the line.

"It's turned round," said Rei. He did not look at the river with his eyes. "It's facing north. It's facing me."

Chen Lian said something in Chinese that Rei did not understand and did not need to.

Then she spoke in Japanese again, very fast. "Listen. Everything anybody ever wrote down says the light holds the far side off the near side. Nobody knows what it is. Nobody's ever looked at it and come back right. But if it lets go, even to turn round, even for a minute, there's nothing between. And if what turns round is just the light, just force, with nobody in it." She stopped. "It goes through the round room and through us and through Tokyo like a river through a paper wall."

Rei pressed the phone harder to his ear. "How long?"

"I don't know. Nobody knows. It's never happened." Her voice cracked very slightly on the last word. "Uedera. Whatever you're going to do. Do it now."

[break The ledger]

He gave the phone back to Kato. He did not remember walking down the yard. He was just there, on the grey sand, beside the folded dress and the cracked stone, where Gin's body knelt with its head bowed.

It lifted its head when he came.

Rei could see them both now. He did not know how. The same face, the same narrow jaw, the same dark eyes, and in the eyes two people looking out, like two people at one window. Gin, tired and furious and alive. The other one, old and careful and very still.

Rei crouched down in front of them. "The light's turning round."

"I feel it." The voice, polite, quiet. The body's hands lay flat on its knees. "On the far side we felt it under everything. Always. Holding. We never once felt it move." It lifted its eyes toward the north, where there was nothing to see but the black river and the lights of Kita on the far bank. "I was a man once, a very long time ago. I think it was too. I think there is somebody in it. If it turns from the wall without remembering who, it will come up as what it has been. A weight. A light. Nothing else. It will not mean to break anything. It will not mean anything at all."

Rei put one hand down on the cold grey sand. "Then somebody has to tell it who it is."

"Nobody knows who it is," said the voice. "I never once asked. It did not occur to me that it had a name."

Rei sat back on his heels. He thought of the white, and the warmth on the back of his neck, and the push on his chest that had not been a hand. *Not yet.*

And the body's jaw set.

Rei knew that jaw. He had watched it set over bad reports at the kitchen table for years. It meant Gin had remembered a thing he had filed somewhere and was annoyed with himself for not filing it better.

Gin rubbed his face with both hands. "Hang on. Hang on. Fourth gallery."

Rei stared at him. "What?"

"The year before. I was in the Archive every Tuesday for six months. Chen Lian thought I'd moved in." The body's hand came out and gripped Rei's arm, hard, as Gin used to grip it at a crossing when Rei was small. "Kasumi Shin's family book. The old one. Tied up with a purple cord. From the people who did the work before there was a Division." His eyes had gone very far away. "Most of it's rubbish. Who got paid. Which bridge had a drowned woman under it. But at the back there's a roster. An office that worked the road by the river at dusk. Kyōhō years. Edo."

"Chen Lian read me a page from it," said Rei. "White flowers. Bon. The page after it's torn out."

"I know," said Gin. "I tore it out."

Rei stared at him.

"I didn't want anybody else reading it," said Gin. "It's in my notebook. In my desk at work, under the stapler. I was going to show the Director. I had an appointment." His jaw set. "Listen."

Rei did not breathe.

"The Office of the Meeting Hour," said Gin. "That's what it called itself. A list of the men on the road every night for about twenty years, and next to each name what they found. *Investigator of the road.* That's what they were called." He swallowed. "And on the page I tore out, one Obon, one name, a man who went north to the river alone to find the woman with the teapot. And next to it, in a different hand, *did not return.*"

Rei's hand closed in the sand. "And nobody looked for him."

Gin's eyes came back. He looked at his brother for a long moment.

"Nobody looked for him," he said. "Not one line. Next night the roster just goes on without him. I copied it into my notebook because it made me so angry I couldn't sleep." The body's grip on Rei's arm tightened. "An investigator walks out to do his job and doesn't come back and his own office crosses him off like a broken umbrella. I thought that was the worst thing I'd ever read."

Somewhere very far down, under the beach, under the river, under the city, the hum went up another half note.

Rei put his own hand over his brother's, on his arm. "Say it. The name."

Gin said it.

[break Down]

He went down as he had gone in July. Not by sitting in anything. By letting go.

The empty frame in his chest stood open. Everything came in through it, the way it had all night, the dead and the hollow ones and the thousand small fires on the doorsteps of Kita. He did not try to stop any of it. He did the opposite. He leaned back into it, like leaning back into water when you have decided to float, and let it carry him.

Gin's hand was on his head when he went. He felt it there for a long time, all the way down.

And then he was standing in white.

It was not quiet this time.

The twelve lights in their ring were roaring. Not burning. Roaring, like twelve fires in a high wind, leaning all one way, toward the centre. Rei felt the people in them. Hoshino with his hands flat on a walking table, grim and patient. Mio, very far off in Kyoto, holding a grey river still with nothing but her own stubbornness. Mori's orange coal, which was not in the round room at all, but standing on a black beach in Kita with his boots in the sand, holding his light up anyway, out of habit, like a man holding up an umbrella in a storm he has already decided to ignore.

And the gap. The thirteenth. Hollow, with the print of a palm and five fingers still on its edge.

He did not touch it.

He looked down.

The light was below him. And it was not facing away any more.

You could not look at it. That was the first thing. Rei looked, and something in the back of his skull went white and thin and high, the hum he had carried since September rising to a scream, and for one moment he knew exactly what would happen if he kept looking. He would go mad, or he would die, and either one would be very quick and quite peaceful.

He did not look at it.

He looked past it.

Like looking past the headlights of a car at night to see who is driving. Like looking past a man's job to see whether he has eaten. He let the light be there and he did not look at it, and he looked at the shape inside it, and the shape inside it was a man.

Not very tall. Not very old. Thirty, maybe, or a little past. Black hair tied back, gone strange and pale at the edges where the light leaked through it. Fine plain features. Shadows under his eyes the colour of an old bruise. A long dark coat that had been blue once, a very long time ago, before somebody stopped paying for it to be washed.

He was standing with his back to Rei, facing north.

And he was turning.

Slowly. So slowly. Like a very old door on a very old hinge. And every inch he turned, the white shook, and the twelve fires roared higher, and far above, in a round room in Ochanomizu, a heavy table walked another hand's width across the floor while a cook in an apron leaned on it with all his weight.

Rei stood at the edge of the light and did not know what to say first.

So he said what he knew.

[break One]

Rei's mouth was dry as paper. "You were an investigator."

The turning stopped.

Not for long. A breath. But it stopped, and the man's right hand came up in front of him, the way a man lifts his hand to look at a cut, and he looked at it. At the side of the first finger. At the ink.

Rei's eyes stung. "My brother writes everything down too. Every night, at the kitchen table. You were somebody who wrote things down for a living. Cases. People. What they were waiting for. *Investigator of the road.* That's what the roster calls you." His voice was shaking and he let it. "That's what you were before you were this."

The light around the man went down. A little. Like a lamp when somebody turns the wick.

[break Two]

Rei took a breath that hurt. "You're from Edo."

The man's head moved. Not toward him. Toward nothing. As if he were listening to a street a long way off.

"Before this city had this name. When it had the other one. The roster my brother found was from the Kyōhō years. That's three hundred years. You've been down here three hundred years, holding this, and the last city you walked around in had wooden gates with roofs on them and lanterns hanging from the beams." He took a step closer to the light. It burned his face like standing too near a stove. "You were born in Edo. You walked in Edo. That's where you're from."

Something in the white smelled, very faintly, of lamp oil.

[break Three]

Rei swallowed against the hum. "You worked for an office."

The man's hand closed. Slowly. Over the ink.

"Not the Division. Not any of the ones with a seal. A little one. One that went out on the road by the river at dusk, every evening, because that was when the doors opened." Rei could see it now. He did not know whether he was seeing it or the man was showing him. A gate. A grey tiled roof. A paper lantern, orange, with three black characters on it. "The Office of the Meeting Hour. *Ōmagatoki.* You went out of that gate every night with a notebook in your coat."

The coat changed colour.

Rei watched it happen. The faded dark went darker, and then it went blue. Not bright. Indigo. Heavy cotton. A colour you could have bought at a dyer's shop by a canal three hundred years ago. And in the inside pocket, against the man's ribs, something square and flat that had not been there before.

[break Four]

This one he did not get from Gin. He did not get it from a ledger.

He got it from his own chest. From the empty frame where his door had been, with everything coming in.

"The others held the line," Rei said. "The other men in the office. They stood on the side of the living with salt in their sleeves and they kept the other side off." He was very close to the light now. His eyes were running. He did not wipe them. "You didn't. You walked down the middle of it. With the living on one hand and the dead on the other. You didn't cut anything off. You didn't let anything run you. You just kept feeling all of it, and listening to both sides, and writing it down." He breathed. "That's why it's you down here. Anybody purer would have broken. Anybody harder would have gone bad. You walked the middle. You still are."

The man turned his head.

Not his body. Just his head. Over his shoulder. Enough that Rei could see the side of his face, and one eye, dark and calm and ringed with tiredness, looking at him out of the light.

It was not a terrible face. It was the face of somebody on the last train home who has just noticed that the boy across the aisle is awake too.

[break Five]

He lifted his hand and pointed up. At the ring. At the hollow gap with his own palm print on its edge.

"That's the thirteenth," he said. "Nobody gives it a name. Adachi's old man on the roof said you can tell who belongs in it by who stays." He looked back at the man. "It's Duty. That's what it is. You've got it in you. That's why you could hold all this, all on your own, for three hundred years, and never put it down. Anybody else would have." His voice broke properly then. "But you're not tied to us any more. Not to the living. You know what you have to do. You've always known. You don't know what you owe us. Nobody down here could tell you. So the chair up there stayed empty, and you held the whole thing from underneath instead, on your own, and nobody came down to say thank you, or to tell you that you could stop."

The light went down again. Much further this time.

Rei could see the man's hands now, both of them, without squinting. They were shaking. Very slightly. As Watanabe's had shaken at the end, when the grey reached his shoulders.

[break Six]

One left.

Rei could feel it in his mouth. Two sounds and then two more. Gin's voice saying them on a grey beach with his hand on his brother's arm. A name copied into a black field book by a furious young man who could not sleep because nobody had looked for someone.

He could feel it. A wrong word now and the man would turn the rest of the way round with nothing in his face but light.

He thought of Gin's notebook. The neat hard writing. The name in it, underlined twice.

He said it as he had said Mika's name across the yard. Not loud. Set down. Like a cup on a table.

He opened his hand toward the light. "Kuze Kaname."

[break The tea]

Nothing happened.

For one long moment nothing happened at all, and Rei stood in the white with the twelve fires roaring round him and thought, very clearly, *I got it wrong.*

Then the man laughed.

It was a small sound. Hoarse. Surprised out of him, like a laugh out of somebody who has not used one for a very long time and has forgotten where he put it. He put his inked hand over his mouth, as if to catch it.

And the light came off him.

Not out. Off. Like a coat. It lifted from his shoulders and his hair and his face and hung behind him in the white, enormous, still burning, still holding the far side off the near side, but separate now, a thing he was carrying and not a thing he was. And the man stood in front of it in an indigo coat with a notebook in the pocket, thirty-three years old for three hundred years, and looked at Rei with his whole face.

He blinked, slowly, like a man waking on a train. "Ah," said Kuze Kaname.

His voice was soft and dry and very courteous. It had an old century in it, like a house that still smells of the people who built it.

"Ah. That is my name, isn't it." He lowered his hand from his mouth and looked at the ink on it. "I put it down somewhere. I meant to pick it up again in the morning." He lifted his eyes to Rei. "It has been a long morning."

Rei wiped his face with the back of his wrist. "There was one more thing. Not one of the six. Something I just know."

Kuze inclined his head. "Tell me."

"Somebody was waiting for you. At the gate. With tea." Rei's voice came out rough. "You're late. It's gone cold."

Kuze looked at him for a long time.

Then he closed his eyes. And Rei saw, very clearly, in the white behind him, for one second only, a gravel yard and a well and a young man in a plain dark coat standing on the step of a long low building with a tray in his hands, scowling up the road at the dusk.

Kuze let out a long breath. "Yes. I imagine it has."

[break The wall]

He opened his eyes.

"You came a very long way down to tell me that," he said. "And you asked me for nothing. Twice now." He smiled. It made him look younger and much more tired. "You are the boy with no door."

Rei straightened up. "Uedera Rei."

"Uedera Rei." He said it carefully, as Gin wrote a name in a case file. As if it mattered that it was spelled right. "Your brother found me in a ledger, I think. I felt him reading. A year ago, a little more. Every Tuesday. He was very angry on my behalf." His eyes went somewhere far above, toward the beach, toward the body kneeling on it with two people inside. "Tell him thank you. Nobody was ever angry on my behalf before."

Rei jerked his chin upward. "Tell him yourself."

Kuze's smile went wry at one corner.

Then it went away.

He looked over his shoulder at the light hanging behind him in the white. The wall. The weight. Still burning. Still holding. But held now by a man who knew he was holding it, and knew he was a man, and knew, Rei could see it come into his face like weather, that he had been holding it too long.

"I must turn round now," Kuze said quietly. "I am awake. I cannot stay facing the wall, now that I know it is a wall. And when I turn, for a little while, nobody will be holding it." His dark eyes came back to Rei. "Do you understand what I am telling you, Uedera Rei."

Above them the twelve fires leaned in. Far away, on a beach in Kita, the shapes in the fog stopped going away, and stood very still, and began, slowly, to turn back.

Rei understood.

He looked up at the gap in the ring. The hollow thirteenth. The print of his own hand on its edge, still warm, after three weeks.

He let his hand fall to his side. "Yeah. I know."

Kuze Kaname looked at him for one more moment, with something in his face that Rei had seen in Gin's face only a few times in his life, and never when Gin knew he was watching.

Then he turned round, and walked away from the ring into the white, slowly, as a man walks off a platform at the end of a long shift with his coat over his arm.

Behind him, all round the ring, the twelve lights began to shake.
