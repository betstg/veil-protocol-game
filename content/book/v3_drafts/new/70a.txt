#title The Room Without a Number

The sixth floor was Internal Affairs and Audit, and on the last night of Obon it was completely dark.

Adachi did not turn on the lights. He walked down the long corridor with the thick carpet that swallowed footsteps, with Riki limping beside him on the lead, and Rei behind, and Kato and Itsuki behind Rei, and the only light came from the city through the windows at the far end, orange and grey and full of smoke from ten thousand small fires.

Halfway along, between two numbered doors, there was the door with no number.

Rei had walked past it in December, on his first day, with Sena. Plain dark wood. And a brass lock of a shape he had never seen before or since, like a flower with too many petals, folded in on itself, with no keyhole anywhere in it.

Adachi stopped in front of it.

"Twelve years," he said. "I've had the key to this for twelve years. I've opened it twice. Once when they gave it to me, to see. Once in the spring, after the Tendo business, to make sure it was still there." He took something out of his breast pocket. Not a key. A small flat disc of dark wood, the size of a coin, carved on one side with the same many-petalled flower. "I went to Ōtemachi on Sunday, after the old man. I asked for the heads of the families. All seven. In person. I've never done that. No Director has done that in thirty years." He turned the disc in his fingers. "I told them what I knew. I told them what was coming tonight. I asked them to let me open this room for one of my officers." He looked at the door. "It took them until yesterday. The Kuroda voted first. Sōun. He did not say why."

Itsuki, behind Rei, did not say anything at all.

Adachi put the disc into the centre of the brass flower. The petals turned. Not all at once. One at a time, slowly, folding back from the middle, with a sound like a very old clock being wound.

The door opened inward.

[break The room]

There was a table. There was a chair. That was all.

The room was small and square and had no window. The walls were bare plaster gone the colour of weak tea. The floor was old boards. A bare bulb hung from the ceiling on a cord, and when Adachi pulled the string it came on, yellow, and swung a little, and the shadows of the table legs swung with it.

On the table, in the exact middle, there was a pager.

It looked like Rei's Capturer. The same shape. The same narrow green screen, the same row of small round lights. But it was older, and heavier, and made of some dark metal instead of plastic, and there were seals all round its edges in tiny black brush characters, so small and so many that the whole case looked as if it had been wrapped in thread. One of the buttons had been filed off flat. Another had been soldered shut.

The single larger light under the screen was on. Not green. Not yellow. Not red. A colour Rei had never seen a light go. A deep, hot, burning orange, like a coal at the bottom of a grate.

Riki sat down in the doorway and would not go any further.

"It's a Capturer," said Adachi. "The first one ever made that way. Built in 1981 for one thing only. Everything else was copied from it, smaller and weaker. Yours included." He looked at Rei. "Yours is made for a frightened girl in a phone box. It holds small things. This was made for this."

"What is it?" said Itsuki.

Adachi did not answer him. He looked at Rei.

"Nobody in this building knows," he said. "The file says *high-grade entity, origin unknown, contained following the Shinjuku incident of 1981.* That's all anybody ever wrote." He paused. "But you know what's in that old room in Ochanomizu. You've counted the chairs. I'd like you to tell me."

Rei stepped into the room.

He did not lift the latch. He did not need to. The orange light was looking at him already. He could feel it on his face like the heat from an open oven.

He sat down in the chair.

[break Courage]

The screen lit up.

Not letters, one at a time, the way his own Capturer wrote. All at once. The whole screen, line after line, in a fast crowded jagged hand, as if someone were shouting through a slot in a door.

*SIT DOWN. GOOD. NOBODY SITS. THEY STAND IN THE DOOR. THEY ARE AFRAID. YOU ARE NOT AFRAID. YES YOU ARE. YOU ARE VERY AFRAID AND YOU SAT DOWN ANYWAY. I KNOW WHAT THAT IS. I AM WHAT THAT IS.*

Rei looked at it.

"You're one of the thirteen," he said. "Not a holder. From the other side."

The screen went blank. Then, slower.

*YES.*

*NOBODY KNEW THAT. FORTY YEARS. NOBODY ASKED.*

"What are you?"

*WHAT DO YOU THINK. I CAME THROUGH. ON MY OWN. INTO YOUR CITY. NOBODY ELSE WOULD. NOBODY ELSE DARED. I WANTED TO SEE.* A pause. *I SAW. IT WAS TOO MUCH. IT WAS LOUD AND BRIGHT AND FAST AND I WAS IN A MAN AND THE MAN WAS AFRAID OF EVERYTHING AND I MADE HIM NOT AFRAID OF ANYTHING. ANYTHING. TRAINS. FIRE. HEIGHTS. OTHER MEN.* Another pause. *HE DID NOT LAST. THEN I WAS IN A ROOM. THEN I WAS IN A BOX. IT HAS BEEN A LONG TIME. I HAVE THOUGHT A GREAT DEAL. SOME OF WHAT I THOUGHT IS WRONG. I KNOW THAT. I CANNOT TELL WHICH PART.*

Rei felt Kato, in the doorway, go very still.

"Courage," said Rei.

The orange light flared.

*YES.*

Behind him, Adachi let out a long breath.

[break The bargain]

*YOU WANT SOMETHING,* said the screen. *THEY DO NOT OPEN THIS DOOR FOR NOTHING. TELL ME.*

Rei told it. Kanna-sensei in Asagaya. The white flowers on the thin places. The freight yard by the river. The sending fires. Every door in the city at once.

The screen was blank for a long time.

*LET ME IN,* it wrote.

Rei did not move.

*THE OTHER ONE IS IN YOUR BROTHER. THE MENDER. I FEEL IT FROM HERE. IT CAME THROUGH TO FIGHT HER. GOOD. BRAVE. STUPID. ONE IS NOT ENOUGH.* The letters came faster. *LET ME IN. INTO YOU. YOU HAVE ROOM. YOU HAVE SO MUCH ROOM. I WILL NOT HURT YOU. I WILL MAKE YOU NOT AFRAID. AND THEN I WILL CALL THE OTHERS. ALL THIRTEEN. THROUGH YOU. WE WILL COME AND WE WILL STAND IN HER DOORS AND SHE WILL SEE WHAT A REAL CROSSING LOOKS LIKE.*

"No," said Rei.

*WHY NOT.*

"Because that's what she's doing." Rei kept his voice level. "That's her whole plan. Bringing everything across at once. Into people. Into the city." He leaned forward. "She started before you came through. Before 1981. She's been on this side longer than anybody knows. She was marking doors with white flowers when this city was called Edo."

The orange light went very still.

For a long time, nothing.

Then, very slowly, one letter at a time, the way Rei's own Capturer wrote, the way a frightened girl in a phone box had written.

*before me.*

"Yes."

*then i did not come first. i thought i came first. forty years i thought i was the brave one. the first one.* A pause. *she was already here.*

"Yes."

*then this is not a fight,* wrote the screen. *this is a flood. you do not fight a flood. you cannot stand in front of it. it goes round you.* The light pulsed. *what does a flood want.*

"To get in," said Rei.

*no. a flood wants to go downhill. it goes where it is easiest.* The letters came faster again. *SHE HAS OPENED DOORS. THE DOORS ARE OPEN BOTH WAYS. A DOOR IS ALWAYS OPEN BOTH WAYS. TONIGHT YOUR WHOLE CITY IS FULL OF THE DEAD. EVERY FAMILY'S DEAD. COME HOME FOR YOUR FESTIVAL. MORE OF THEM THAN SHE HAS HOLLOW PEOPLE. MORE THAN SHE HAS THINGS WAITING ON THE OTHER SIDE. AND TONIGHT THEY ARE ALL GOING BACK ANYWAY.*

Rei sat very still in the chair.

*ASK THEM,* wrote the screen. *ASK THEM TO GO BACK THROUGH HER DOORS. ALL AT ONCE. ALL OF THEM. NOTHING COMES IN THROUGH A DOOR WHEN A CROWD IS GOING OUT OF IT. YOU KNOW THAT. YOU HAVE STOOD ON YOUR TRAIN PLATFORMS.*

"They won't listen to me," said Rei. "Not all of them. I'm one person."

*YOU TALK TO THE DEAD,* wrote the screen. *I HAVE BEEN WATCHING YOU THROUGH THIS BOX SINCE YOU SAT DOWN. YOU ARE MADE OF IT. THE GIRL IN THE PHONE BOX. THE BOY ON THE KERB. THE OLD WOMAN AT THE SHOP. THEY LISTENED. THEY WILL LISTEN.* A pause. *AND IF THEY DO NOT, YOU WILL STAND THERE AND ASK ANYWAY, BECAUSE THAT IS WHAT YOU ARE. I KNOW WHAT THAT IS.*

[break The guarantee]

Adachi came into the room.

He stood beside the table, looking down at the burning orange light, with his hands behind his back.

"I can't let it out," he said. "Not on my own word. The families gave me this door for one conversation." He looked at Rei. "But if it went to the river tonight in your pocket, and you opened it there, and it helped, I would put my name on that. I would answer for it." He paused. "Only if you can tell me that it will be on our side. Not hers. Not its own. Ours. All the way to morning. Can you tell me that?"

Rei looked at the screen.

It was blank. Waiting.

He thought of Majima coming over Tanizaki's fence with his hands open. Okada on the yellow line, not looking at him, with her hand held out. Moriya hopping across Sena's burning office on one leg to put two fingers on a man's neck. Itsuki in the doorway behind him, who had shouted at his brother in a cemetery tonight because somebody had to. Kato, who had stood on a landing in Nakano and let himself be heard. Usami, who was afraid of everything and had said *I'd like to try*.

"Yes," said Rei. "I can tell you that."

"How?"

"Because there are a lot of brave people on our side," said Rei. "More than on hers. It'll want to be where they are." He looked at the orange light. "Won't you."

The screen lit.

*YES,* it wrote. *YES. YES. FORTY YEARS IN A BOX. YES.*

And then, smaller, slower.

*i would like to see brave people again. before i go home. i do not think i have seen any since 1981.*

Adachi looked at Rei for a long moment. Then he reached down and picked up the old dark pager, very carefully, in both hands, as you pick up something that might be hot, and held it out.

"Then take it," he said. "You're responsible for it. I'm responsible for you." He let go. "That's how this building is supposed to work. I'd forgotten."

The pager was heavy in Rei's hands, and warm, warmer than metal had any right to be. The orange light beat slowly against his palm like a pulse.

In the doorway, Itsuki had his sketchbook open against the frame and was drawing very fast. Rei could see the page from where he sat. It was not white any more. It was the river. A wide dark river at night, and the railway bridge, and hundreds and hundreds of small lights going down the bank toward the water, all in the same direction, like a procession.

Itsuki looked up at him over the top of it.

"I can see it again," he said. His face was wet. "I can see the river."
