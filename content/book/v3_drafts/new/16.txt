#title A Small Light

*The Font.*

It does not sleep. People who know it exists, and there are very few, say that it sleeps. They are wrong. It works. It has been working for so long, without stopping, that the work has closed over it like water, and it has gone down into a kind of trance, the way a man at a loom for forty years stops seeing the threads and only feels them. It does not know that it is in a trance. It only knows the work.

It is very large. It is not a thing that has a size. It is under the city, and under the river, and under the old red gate. Around it, scattered across the city and beyond, are the lights of the Thirteen. Living people, each one holding one feeling open and steady all their lives. Grief in Kyoto. Rage somewhere hard to find. Love. Fear. Wonder. And the others. The Font feeds them from the centre, and they keep the thin skin between the living and the dead pulled tight, like the skin of a drum.

There are twelve lights.

The thirteenth chair is empty. Duty. Nobody sits in it, and nobody living holds it, and so the drum is not quite tight, and there is a place at its edge where it gives when you press.

The Font has Duty inside it. It has always had all thirteen inside it. That is what it is. It knows what it must do. It does it, every moment, without rest. But Duty with nobody in the chair is Duty with no thread to the living. The Font knows what it must do. It no longer knows what it owes to them, the living, up there in the light, with their trains and their tea. It cannot remember why the work is for them. It only remembers that the work must be done.

It does not remember very much at all.

It does not remember having a name.

[break Something came]

Tonight something came through.

Something small. Something that should not have been able to come so far. A little light, steady and stubborn, walking through the white toward it, with a cold thing held against its chest. And wrapped round the little light, clinging to it, a dirty hungry shape full of wanting, that had looked out at the city through a hundred rats.

The Font did not mean to burn the dirty shape. It simply did. The shape came too close to the centre, and the centre is the centre, and things that come too close to it burn. The Font barely noticed.

It noticed the little light.

The little light stood at the edge of everything, very small and very far away, and asked it a question. The Font had been asked questions before. Not often. Once every few years, someone pushes through the white, half drowned, and asks something. *Will I live. Does she love me. Where is it hidden.* The Font answers. It is one of the rules. It does not remember who made the rules. It answers, and then it pushes them back, gently, because the living cannot stay here. It does not want them to burn.

This little light asked about a brother.

Not about itself. Not *will I live*, though it was very close to not living, closer than it knew, with blood running out of its nose onto the grass of a riverbank in a world the Font could only half remember. It had one question, and it spent it on somebody else, without even stopping to think. The Font found that it did not know what to do with that. It turned the question over, like a stone found on a beach, to see the colour underneath.

The Font looked for the brother. It was not hard. The brother was very near. Very near the empty place at the edge of the drum, the place where Duty should be. Something was wearing him. Something old and courteous and patient, with a debt in its heart. The Font knew that thing. Long ago, it had known that thing very well.

*Yes,* said the Font. *He is alive.*

It put a hand on the little light's chest and pushed it home.

[break A hand]

That was the strange part.

A hand.

The Font had not had a hand for a very long time. It did not have one now. But when it pushed the little light back across the white, it pushed it with a hand. It felt the shape of it. Five fingers. A palm. The warmth of a chest under the palm, a heart beating, slow and frightened and brave.

It was a man's hand. A tired man's hand. There was ink on the side of the first finger, as if the hand had written a great deal, a long time ago, in a different kind of light.

The Font did not know whose hand it was.

It lay very still for a long time afterward, in the white, under the city, feeding its twelve lights beside the empty chair, and thought about the hand.

Then, slowly, the work closed over it again.

But somewhere very far down, so far that even the Font could not reach it, something had turned over. Like a person in a bed in the early morning who hears, from a long way off, a voice they used to know, calling a name that might once have been theirs.

The little light was walking toward it. The Font could feel it now. Very far away. Very small. Walking.

It did not know why, but it did not mind.
