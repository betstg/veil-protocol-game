#title Two Cups

*Gin.*

This time he woke slowly, like a man coming up from the bottom of a deep pool, and the voice was waiting for him.

*You're awake,* it said. Not *you should not be awake*. Just *you're awake*. As if it had been expecting him.

He was sitting at the table in the small yellow kitchen with the frosted window. The dry-cleaning calendar on the nail said May now. Somebody had turned the pages. It was night, and it was raining, and the kettle was on the gas ring, starting to hum.

His hands were on the table. He looked at them. He tried to move a finger.

It moved.

Not much. Not easily. Like moving through cold honey. But it moved, and the voice did not stop it.

*I am letting you,* said the voice. *A little. For a while.* A pause. *I wish to talk.*

[break The talk]

*Talk,* said Gin. His thought came out rough and slow, like a voice after a long illness. *You want to talk. After the road.*

*Yes. After the road.* The voice was quiet. *I have been thinking about the road. About what you said. You said I do not understand anything.* Another pause. *You were right. I wish to understand.*

The kettle began to whistle. Gin's hands got up, by themselves, and went to the stove. He let them. He was too tired to fight over a kettle. They turned off the gas. They lifted the kettle. They poured the water into the small clay teapot, slowly, the right way, the way Tanizaki taught him.

They took down one cup from the shelf.

*Two,* said Gin.

The hands stopped.

*Two cups,* said Gin. *Every night. For years. Even when he wasn't there. You've been pouring one.* He was so tired. *If you want to understand something, start with that.*

The hands stood still at the shelf for a long time.

Then, slowly, the right hand reached up and took down a second cup. It put it on the table beside the first. It poured tea into both. Two cups, steaming, side by side, on the small table in the yellow kitchen in the rain.

The body sat down in front of them.

*Why?* said the voice. *Why two? When there is no one to drink the second?*

*Because one day there will be,* said Gin. *That's what it means. It's not for him. It's for the day he comes home.*

The voice was silent.

*You understand tasks,* said Gin. *Everything's a job to you. Something to be done, and then done.* He looked at the two cups through his own eyes. *The second cup isn't a job. Nobody asked for it. Nobody's going to drink it tonight. It's just a promise you make to yourself that you won't give up.*

*A promise,* said the voice. Very carefully. As if the word were hot. *A promise to whom?*

*To nobody. To the morning.*

*That is not possible. A promise must be to someone. A promise is a chain between two.*

*Not for us,* said Gin. *We make them all the time. To nobody. To ourselves. To people who'll never know. And we break half of them. And we make them again the next day.* He felt, very far away, a kind of laugh, his own. *You'd hate it. It's very untidy.*

[break What it wanted]

They sat with the two cups for a long time. The rain went on.

*Tell me what you want,* said Gin at last. *You've been in me for nine months. You've walked me round this city like a dog. You stood on a kerb and watched a boy bleed. What is it for? What do you want?*

The voice was silent for so long that Gin thought it would not answer.

Then it said, *There is an old thing in the city.*

Gin waited.

*Very old. It feeds on despair. It sits in a white room, inside a kind woman, and pours tea for tired people, and drinks them.* The voice was very calm and very cold. *It and I have known each other for a long time. Longer than this city has had its name. We have hated each other for all of it.*

*Why?*

*Because of what it is. Because of what I am.* A pause. *Some things do not need a reason. You have a word for it. You have many words for it. None of them are big enough.*

Gin felt something in the voice that he had never felt in it before. Not anger. Something colder and older and much more patient than anger.

*It wants a chair,* said the voice. *A seat at a table on your side. It is eating the man who holds it, very slowly, from far away. When the man dies, it will try to take his place.*

*And then what? It breaks the veil?*

*Perhaps.* The voice did not seem very interested. *Perhaps the veil falls. Perhaps it does not. That is not my concern.* A pause. *My concern is the old thing. I want it ended. I have wanted it for a very long time. From the far side I cannot reach it. It is too well hidden in the woman. I need hands on this side. Strong hands. Hands that know how to fight things like it.* A pause. *Yours were the best I could find. You were close to my shape. You had nobody round you to keep things out. You were tired.*

Gin looked at his hands on the table, either side of the two cups.

*You could have asked,* he said.

*Asked?*

*Asked. Instead of taking. You could have come to my door and knocked and said, there's an old thing eating people, I need your help to kill it.* Gin's thought was very tired. *I'd have said yes. That's my whole job. That's all I've done for six years. Stop old things that eat people.*

The voice was silent for a very long time.

*It did not occur to me,* it said at last. Slowly. Like a man discovering a door in a wall he has walked past every day for years. *It did not occur to me to ask.*

[break The deal]

*Here's what I want,* said Gin. *Since we're talking.*

*Tell me.*

*No more kerbs. If someone's dying in front of me, and I can help, you let me help.* He felt his own will, thin and stubborn, pressing against the warm heavy weight that held him. *And you don't touch my brother. Ever. Whatever happens with your old thing. You don't touch Rei.*

*Your brother is not my concern,* said the voice. *I have no reason to touch him. I have no reason to do anything with him at all.*

*That's not the same as not touching him.*

*No,* the voice agreed. It sounded, for the first time, very faintly puzzled. *You want a promise.*

*I want a promise.*

The voice did not answer for a long time. Gin felt it turning the word over. *Promise.* Feeling the weight of it.

*I will not promise,* it said at last. *I do not make them carelessly. A thing like me does not break its word, and so a thing like me does not give it until it knows what it will cost.* A pause. *I do not know your brother. I do not know what will happen. I will not promise about a thing I do not know.*

Gin was silent.

*The other,* said the voice. *The kerb.* It was quiet for a while. *If I can let you help, and it does not cost me the work, I will let you help. That is not a promise either. It is what I will do.*

*That's not a promise.*

*No.* The voice almost sounded amused. *It is very untidy. You said I would hate it.*

Gin felt something then that he had not felt in nine months, inside his own body. Something small and warm and painful, deep in his chest. It took him a long moment to recognise it.

Hope.

*Drink your tea,* said the voice. *Both of them. It is getting cold.*

His hands lifted the first cup. He drank. Then the second. It was the same tea. It tasted different.

The dark came up slowly after that, like warm water. But this time, before it closed over him, he felt the voice do something it had never done.

It washed both cups. And dried them. And put them back on the shelf, side by side.
