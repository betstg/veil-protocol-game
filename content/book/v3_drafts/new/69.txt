#title One Minute

On Monday night, Rei called him.

He did it at the kitchen table, at half past two in the morning, with the Capturer in front of him and Gorō on the back of Gin's chair. He had not slept. He had lain on the sofa with his foot off the end for three hours looking at the ceiling, and then he had got up, because of what Gin had said through the thing in the garden, and because he could not stop thinking about the word *deal*, and because in five days the dead would come home and the far shore would open by the river past Tabata and he did not know if any of them would be standing on the other side of it.

He did not press TALK. He did not need to. He had a name now.

He put his hand flat on the table and said it. Quietly. Not to the box. To the dark.

"Yushiro."

Nothing happened for a long time. The fridge hummed. A motorbike went by at the bottom of the hill.

Then the Capturer's single round light came on by itself, soft and steady, and on the green screen, in those neat careful letters, one line.

*i am here. i am on the stairs. may i come up.*

[break The walkway]

He went out onto the walkway in his socks.

The night was very hot. The air smelled of the city in August, hot concrete and drains and somebody's mosquito coil two floors down. The orange lamp at the top of the stairs was on, and a moth was beating itself against it, over and over, with a small dry tapping sound. Two doors along, the Yamadas had left their futon hanging over the railing again.

Yushiro was standing at the top of the stairs.

He stood very still, the way he always stood, in Gin's body, in a dark T-shirt, with Gin's long hair loose now, past his shoulders, and the jacket over his arm again, though it was nearly thirty degrees. He did not come along the walkway. He stood under the orange lamp with the moth tapping above his head and looked at Rei, and at the door behind him, and at the brass hook just visible inside, in the genkan, empty.

He shifted the jacket on his arm. "You called."

Rei stood at the railing with his hands on the warm metal. "I want to talk to him."

Yushiro did not move.

"I know you can't let him out," said Rei. "Not all the way. I know what it costs you. I know it isn't safe. I'm not asking for an hour. I'm not asking for him to come home." His voice did not shake. He had practised, on the sofa, in the dark. "I'm asking for one minute. With him. Not through you. Him."

The moth tapped against the lamp. Tap. Tap. Tap.

"I have never done that," said Yushiro. "I have let him move a finger. I have let him look. I have let him say a word, sometimes, through my mouth, when I was too tired to stop it. I have never stepped back. I do not know if I can step back and still be holding on."

Rei did not let go of the railing. "Can you try?"

Yushiro looked at him for a long time under the orange light.

"He is asking me the same thing," he said at last. "He has been asking me since Saturday. Every minute. He is very loud." A pause. "He says he has been asking me for a year and you have done it in one sentence and it is very annoying."

Rei almost laughed.

"One minute," said Yushiro. "I will try. I do not know what will happen. If I cannot hold on, I will take him back before the minute is gone, and you must not try to stop me. If you try to stop me, you will lose him, and so will I." He took a breath. Gin's chest rose and fell. "Do you understand?"

Rei nodded, hard. "Yes."

Yushiro lifted one hand, palm out, a little way. "Come here, then. Not too close."

Rei walked along the walkway in his socks. He stopped an arm's length away. Under the lamp, his brother's face was orange and black, all bones and shadows, and the eyes were very still.

He looked at his phone.

3:46.

Yushiro let his hand fall. "Now," he said, very quietly. "Count."

And closed his eyes.

[break Sixty]

The body swayed.

It swayed like a man on a train when it brakes, and caught itself with one hand on the railing. And when the eyes opened again, they were not still.

They were moving. Everywhere. Over Rei's face, and down, and up, and over his shoulder at the door, and at the hook, and at the walkway, and at the moth, and back to Rei's face. Quick and dark and attentive, noticing everything, the way they had noticed everything every day of Rei's life, like a man who has to write it all down and has lost his pencil.

The mouth opened. Closed. Opened.

The lips pulled back from the teeth as if the word hurt coming out. "*Rei*."

It was Gin's voice. Not the polite one. Not the careful one setting each word down like a cup. Gin's. Rough and dry and a little too fast, with a crack in the middle of it.

Rei could not move.

"Don't," said Gin. "Don't stand there. Fifty-five seconds. Don't waste them standing there like an idiot." His hand came off the railing. It was shaking. It came up, slowly, as if it weighed a great deal, and stopped in the air between them. "Can I. Is it. I don't know if I can do it on my own."

Rei stepped forward. Into it. Under it.

Gin's hand came down on his head.

Heavy. Warm. Shaking. On the top of his head, the way it had when he was four and frightened of the thing at the window, the way it had when he was eight at the kitchen table learning to close the door in his chest, the way it had the morning of his police exam, ruffling his hair so hard he had to fix it again in the train window. The fingers moved, once. Not ruffling. Just holding. Just checking he was there.

"You're so thin," said Gin. His voice broke right in half. "You're so *thin*. What have you been eating. Don't tell me. I know. I know what you've been eating, I've been watching you through that thing's eyes for a year, rice and hot water and those horrible pickled plums, every morning, standing at the counter, you absolute..."

Rei's hands came up and found the front of the dark T-shirt and held on. "Gin."

"I know. I know." Gin pressed his hand harder against Rei's head, as if he could push the words in through his skull. "Listen. Forty seconds. Listen. The rent. You paid the rent?"

Rei nodded under the hand. "Okada's guarantor."

Gin stared at him.

"*Okada*," he said. "Okada Shizuka is your guarantor. God help us." Something in his face cracked, and it was almost a laugh. "She's going to come round and check the fridge. Every week. With a notebook."

Rei felt himself smile and could not stop it. "She already did."

Gin made a sound. It was not a laugh. It was not a sob. It was both, and neither, and it came out of him like something tearing.

"Listen," he said. "Thirty. Listen to me. The thirteenth. She's going to tell him to sit. Kishin. She's going to call in what he owes her and tell him to sit in the chair, and he won't be able to say no. Not on his own. He doesn't know how. He's trying. He's trying so hard, Rei, you have no idea, he's been trying for a year. But it's a debt older than the river and he can't say no to it on his own." His hand slid down from Rei's head to the back of his neck, and gripped. "But he gave you his name."

Rei's throat closed round it. "Yes."

"So you've got a claim too. Hers is old. Yours is new. But it's a claim. When she says *sit*, you say his name. You say it louder than she does. You say *Yushiro, don't*. You make him choose which one of you he owes." Gin's eyes were burning. "That's the only thing that'll work. I've been thinking about it for a year. That's all I've had to do, for a year, is think. That's the only thing."

Rei held his brother's eyes. "Okay."

"Twenty. Don't sit in the chair for her. Ever. Whatever she offers. Whatever she says I'll be. I don't want to come home that way. I'd rather not come home." His grip tightened. "Promise me."

Rei said it into the hot dark between them. "I promise."

"And the man at the bottom of the light." Gin's voice was going strange. Thin. Further away. "He's got a name too. He's forgotten it. Find it. Somebody has to give it back to him. I never could."

Rei's grip on the T-shirt tightened. "Gin..."

"Ten. Rei. Ten." The hand at the back of Rei's neck was shaking so hard now that it hurt. "You still have my key?"

Rei pulled the cord out of the neck of his T-shirt. The brass key on its plain steel ring. The little dent where it had caught in a car door, years ago, and Gin had sworn about it for a whole afternoon.

Gin looked at it.

"Good," he said. "Keep it. Don't give it to anyone. Don't put it on the hook." He swallowed. "I'll need it. To get in. When I come home."

Rei's fist closed round the key. "When."

"*When*," said Gin. He let go of Rei's neck. His hand came round, slowly, shaking, and closed over Rei's hand, over the key in Rei's fist, the way it had closed over Rei's hand on the way to school when he was small and had to cross the big road at the bottom of the hill. "When. Not if. When. Say it."

Rei said it the way you say a promise. "When."

"Good boy." Gin's face was wet. His eyes were going still. Slowly. From the edges inward. Like water freezing on a pond. "Five. Eat something. Get off the sofa. Tell Itsuki to stop smoking. Tell Kato I can hear him too, sometimes, through the walls, through all of it. Tell Haruki to spend the money. Tell..."

He stopped.

His eyes were nearly still now. Only the very centre of them was still moving. Still looking. Still noticing. Still trying to write it all down.

His mouth barely moved. "Two. Rei."

Rei pressed his forehead against his brother's shoulder. "I'm here."

"I know," said Gin. "I always know where you are. I always look up."

[break 3:47]

The hand on Rei's hand went heavy.

Then it went still. Then it went careful. And the fingers opened, slowly, one at a time, very politely, the way you let go of something that does not belong to you.

The eyes were still.

Yushiro took a step back along the walkway, under the orange lamp, and stood with Gin's hands at his sides and Gin's face wet and Gin's chest going up and down very fast, as if he had run a long way.

He did not say anything for a long time.

"I held on," he said at last. Very quietly. As if he could not quite believe it. "I stepped back, and I held on. I did not know I could do both." He looked down at his hands. At Gin's hands. "I did not know there was a way to let go of something without losing it."

Rei stood on the walkway with the key in his fist and his face wet and the moth tapping against the lamp.

He wiped his face with the heel of his hand. "Thank you."

Yushiro looked up.

"You do not owe me for that," he said. "I want to be very clear. That was not a debt. I will not hold it against you." He paused. "He asked me to give you one more thing. He says he did not have time." Another pause. "He says, *it's not your fault. None of it. Not one bit. Not the old man. Not the girl. Not me. Not the platform. Not any of it. Tell him I said so and tell him I'll know if he doesn't believe it.*"

Rei closed his eyes.

When he opened them, the top of the stairs was empty. The orange lamp was on. The moth was still beating itself against it, over and over. The Yamadas' futon hung over the railing two doors down.

He looked at his phone.

It was 3:47.
