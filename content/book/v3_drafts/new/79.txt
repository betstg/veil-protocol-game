#title Together

*Gin.*

They had been standing at the edge of the grey beach for a long time before she looked at them.

The voice had walked them down there while everyone else in the yard was running the other way, after the hollow ones, into the streets. Down between the rusted rails, past the freight cars, to the place where the weeds stopped and the grey began. It had stood them there, very straight, with Gin's jacket sleeves pushed up and Gin's hands at his sides, and waited.

Gin was not underneath.

He noticed that first. He was not at the bottom of the dark, looking up at a circle of sky. He was not in the back room. He was here. Behind his own eyes, with the river in front of him and the smell of hot weeds and iron, and the voice was here too, right beside him. Not on top of him. Beside.

*When did you do that,* said Gin.

*On the bench,* said the voice. *Outside the tobacconist's. The night the old man died. I did not decide it. I noticed afterwards that I had done it.* A pause. *I have not been pushing you down since then. You have not noticed because you have been asleep most of the time, from the grief. Now you are awake.*

Gin looked at the grey beach.

*It's past the twelfth,* he said. *It's been a year. More.*

*Yes.*

*The Director told Rei a year. Nobody lasts past a year.*

*Nobody lasts past a year when the thing wearing them keeps taking.* The voice was very plain. *I stopped taking. I did not know you could. I did not know there was anything to do with a person except wear them or leave them.* A pause. *You taught me there was a third thing. So did the old man. So did your brother, without meaning to, every time he went to a stranger instead of to you.*

Gin did not say anything for a while.

*What is it,* he said at last. *The third thing.*

*I do not know the word,* said the voice. *I think it is what you do on a bench when somebody sits down beside you. You move over.*

[break The beach]

On the grey beach, at the low white table, the small woman in the grey cardigan was still holding the night open with both hands. The bell from the temple in Takinogawa had made it slip. She had caught it. Now other bells were ringing, faint and far, from all over Kita, one after another, every hour, and every one of them made it slip a little more, and every time she caught it again.

And the lights were still going home past her, in a long bright stream, down the beach and into the fog, without stopping, without looking at her. She could not stop them. She had stopped trying.

She was looking at the two of them now.

*I have something to tell you,* said the voice. *Before she speaks.*

*Go on.*

*I came through to end her. That was the whole of it. I did not care about the veil or the city or you. I chose you because you were the right shape for me to be strong in, and you were not protected, and the skin round you was thin.* It said it very plainly. *She is older than I am. Much older. She has been on this side since before this city had this name. If I go into her and I lose, I will not come back out. And if I do not come back out, neither will you.*

Gin looked at the woman on the beach.

*Before,* said the voice, *I would have taken you down with me and not thought about it. You were the coat.* A pause. *I do not want to do that now. I want you to know what I am going to do before I do it. And I want you beside me when I do it, awake, not underneath. And if you want to go back to your brother instead, now, tonight, I will step out of you on this beach and go into her alone, without your gifts, and lose. I would rather lose than take you down there without asking.*

Gin stood very still on the edge of the grey.

*That's not a choice,* he said. *That's you being an idiot.*

*Possibly.*

*If you go in alone you lose, and she has nobody in her way, and she walks up this beach and through Rei. That's not a choice.*

*No,* said the voice. *I know. I wanted to offer it anyway. The old man would have said that was correct.*

Gin laughed. He did not mean to. It came out of his own mouth, out loud, rough and short, on the edge of the grey beach, and the woman at the table heard it and her head tilted.

*Together, then,* said Gin. *Both of us. Awake. You don't take, I don't hide. We go in together and we come out together, or we don't come out.*

*Together,* said the voice. It said the word slowly, as if it had never said it before and wanted to get it right. *Yes. I will hold you to that. That is a promise. I will keep it.*

And Gin felt it. Something settling. Something very small and very heavy clicking into place between them, like a latch dropping into a hook.

[break The gate]

Gin turned round, and looked back at the yard.

He saw the yard nearly empty of the living. A few hollow ones still standing among the rails with their flowers, heads lifted, as if listening to a bell somewhere far off. White chrysanthemums trampled into the weeds everywhere, hundreds of them. He saw a white door painted on the side of a rusted freight car, and a street inside it, and a boy with paint to his elbows standing on a crate with both hands flat on the frame, and another boy with a silver ring holding him up from behind. And past them, down the beach, the lights still going home. Thousands of them. He saw a broad man in dark clothes sitting in the weeds by the gate with a girl in a pink hoodie asleep against his chest and an old dog lying across his feet. He saw a big man in a black jacket coming back in through the gate from the streets, with the air round him shimmering.

He saw his team, coming back in from the streets behind him. Shimizu, walking fast. Kato with his knight. Moriya on his crutch. A soft-faced young man in a cardigan he did not know, with something wound round him like a coat. A young man in a grey suit he did know, from a desk on the sixth floor. Okada.

Okada.

*She has been watching for him,* said the voice. *For five years. You asked her to.*

*I know.*

*She kept it.*

*I know.* Gin's throat was very tight. *She always keeps everything.*

And he saw Rei.

Rei was standing in the weeds halfway between the gate and the grey beach, with a girl in a hood sitting on a rail behind him with her head between her knees. He had just arrived. Gin could tell. He was swaying a little, like someone who has come off a train too fast. His nose had bled and dried and bled again. There was blood at his ears. His clean shirt was filthy. Gin's key hung on its cord outside it, on his chest, catching the white light from the river.

And where his door should have been, there was nothing.

Gin saw it. He had always been able to see it. He had made it. At a kitchen table, when Rei was eight, with his hand on his brother's back, saying *like a door, like this, close it, close it, good*. He had made it so his brother could sleep. So the things at the window would stay outside. So Rei would never have to stand in the middle of everything with nothing between him and all of it.

It was gone. Off its hinges. Laid down in the weeds.

And Rei was standing in the middle of all of it, with everything coming in, holding it.

*He took it off,* said Gin. His voice inside was very small. *He took it right off.*

*Yes,* said the voice. *He did that at midnight. Before you could stop him.*

*I spent twelve years teaching him to keep it shut.*

*Yes.* A pause. *And he kept it shut for twelve years. And then, tonight, when it mattered, he knew exactly where the hinges were.* The voice was quiet. *Somebody taught him that, too.*

[break Brothers]

They walked up the yard toward him.

A few of the hollow ones were still standing among the rails, with their flowers and their dark eyes, listening to the bells. They did not stop for them. But they parted round Gin's body like water round a stone in a stream, without looking, without knowing why.

Rei turned his head.

He saw them coming. Gin watched his brother's face change. Watched it go from the flat, swaying, bleeding exhaustion of a boy who has been everywhere at once to something else. Something Gin had seen on that face exactly once before, a long time ago, through a car window, at the bottom of the hill, on a night when Gin had been very late and had come round the corner under the street lamp and looked up, and there had been a small boy at the railing in pyjamas, waiting.

They stopped in front of him.

Close. Closer than on the walkway. Close enough that Gin could smell the smoke on him, and the blood, and the iron filings, and under all of it, faint, the soap they had always bought, the cheap one, because Rei liked the smell.

Neither of them said anything.

The voice did not move his hand. Gin did not move it either. They moved it together. Gin felt them do it. Two people lifting one hand, slowly, carefully, as if it were very heavy and very valuable, and putting it on the top of Rei's head.

Rei closed his eyes.

"Hey," said Gin. His own voice. With the voice right beside it, not speaking, just there. "You took your door off."

His eyes stayed shut. "Yeah. Sorry."

"Don't be." Gin's hand moved, once, on his brother's hair. Not ruffling. Holding. Checking he was there. "I'm going to kill you later. But don't be."

Rei laughed. It came out wet and broken.

Then he opened his eyes and looked past Gin, at the grey beach, at the small woman by the low white table. And Gin felt the voice look too. And felt the woman look back. At both of them. At the two people in one body standing with a hand on a boy's head in the middle of a freight yard full of the walking dead.

She smiled.

Not the kind smile. A different one. Old. Very old. Older than the river. The smile of something that has waited a very long time for an old enemy to come down to the water, and sees him coming, and sees what he has brought with him.

*Ah,* she said, in the voice from the bottom of a lake. Not out loud. In the air. In the weeds. *Ah. There you are. All three of you.*

The voice went very still.

And then, for the first time in a year, Gin felt it reach for his hand. Not to move it. Not to take it. To hold it. Like a child reaching for somebody's hand at a crossing, without looking, because the road is very wide and the traffic is very fast.

Gin held on.
