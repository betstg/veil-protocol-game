#title Telepath

The raid on the flat in Nakano went wrong in the first eleven seconds.

It was the second week of May. Yua had found it. A small flat on the fifth floor of an old building near Nakano station where the woman in the grey coat brought the girls and boys from Kabukichō before they went to the white house. A waiting room. A place to be kind to them first. Yua had followed her, three Sundays running, at a distance, in a different hoodie each time. She had charged Rei two hamburgers and twenty thousand yen and made him promise not to tell anyone where he got it.

Shimizu took it to the Director. The Director signed it. Seven o'clock on a Thursday evening. The five of them, and two field officers from the third floor, and Kuma.

Aoi came on crutches. Nobody could stop her. Her leg was in a hard black brace from the hip to the ankle, and she was white with pain by the time they got up the stairs, but she had a bag of seals over her shoulder and she said if anyone told her to wait in the car she would put a seal on their mouth.

At seven exactly, Shimizu knocked on the door of 503.

It opened by itself. The flat behind it was empty.

[break Empty]

Not abandoned. Empty. Cleaned. The floor was swept. The windows were open. There was no furniture, no bags, no cups, no cushions, nothing. The walls had been washed so recently that they were still damp. The air smelled of bleach and, under it, faintly, of green tea.

In the middle of the bare floor, on its own, there was a single white chrysanthemum.

And beside it, a note. One line, in soft, round, beautiful handwriting.

*Thank you for coming. We have moved. Please give my love to Rei.*

Nobody said anything for a long time.

Kuma walked into the middle of the room and sniffed the flower and growled, very low, and backed away from it.

Shimizu stood in the doorway. Rei watched her face. He watched her see it. All the ways it could have gone. And the one way it had gone. The small line between her eyebrows went so deep that it looked painful.

"They knew," she said quietly. "They knew we were coming. They knew when. They knew who." She looked at the note. "*Please give my love to Rei.*"

Everyone looked at Rei.

Then, one by one, without meaning to, they looked at Kato.

[break The looks]

Rei saw it happen. He saw it go round the team the way a cold draught goes round a room.

The two field officers from the third floor looked first. Fast, then away. Then Aoi, on her crutches, frowning, then shaking her head as if to shake the thought out of it. Then Tendo, by the window, who looked at Kato for one second and then looked away very fast, as if ashamed of himself. And then, last, for one second, Shimizu.

Only for one second. Then she looked away, at the flower on the floor. But it was enough.

Kato was standing by the door with his hand in his pocket on his king.

He had heard every one of them. Rei knew that. He had heard every single thought go round the room. *The telepath. He hears everything. He'd know the plan. He'd know the time. Who else could it be.* Rei saw Kato's face. It did not change. It did not move at all. It went perfectly smooth and still, like a lake when the wind stops.

"Okay," said Kato pleasantly. "Okay. I know what you're all thinking. Obviously." He smiled. It was a very good smile. It was the smile he used on people in the copy room. "It's fine. It's the obvious answer. The telepath did it. Good guess. Very logical."

"Kato," said Rei.

"No, really. It's fine. I'm used to it." Kato turned the king over in his pocket. "This happens every year or so. Something goes wrong and everybody looks at me. It's sort of like my birthday." He looked at the floor. "I'll go and tell Moriya. Save everybody the paperwork."

He turned and walked out of the empty flat and down the corridor toward the stairs.

[break What Rei did]

Rei went after him.

He caught him on the fourth-floor landing. Kato was standing at the top of the next flight of stairs, very still, holding the rail with one hand, not going down.

"I didn't," said Kato, without turning round. His voice was flat. "Just so you know. I didn't. Not that it matters."

"I know you didn't."

"You don't know that. You can't know that. Nobody can." Kato's knuckles were white on the rail. "That's the whole point of me. Nobody can ever know what I'd do with what I hear. They have to trust me. And nobody trusts a telepath. Not really. Not when it counts." He laughed. It was a horrible laugh. "Shimizu looked. Did you see? For one second. Shimizu. Who's known me for two years. Who I've never once lied to." His voice cracked. "Even Shimizu."

"I didn't look," said Rei.

Kato went very still.

"Listen," said Rei. "Go on. Listen to me. Right now. Hear what I'm thinking."

Kato turned round slowly. He looked at Rei. And Rei did not think about the weather. He did not try to be careful or polite. He opened his head, the way you open a window, and thought as loudly and as clearly as he had ever thought anything.

*You didn't do it. I know you didn't. I didn't wonder. I didn't check. I didn't think it for one second. You're my partner. You knelt on Aoi's leg for eighteen minutes. You came to the basement nine times. You're the one who told me not to go in that flat with the cats and I didn't check then either and I'm never going to check. Not ever. Not about you.*

Kato stood on the landing with his hand on the rail and stared at him.

His face did something. Rei had never seen it before. It crumpled. All of it at once, like paper in a fist. And Kato Ryusei, who had said approximately four genuine things in two years and made a joke about everything else, sat down on the top step of the stairs in an empty building in Nakano and put his face in his hands and cried.

Rei sat down next to him. He did not say anything. He did not need to. Kato could hear everything he was not saying.

[break Moriya]

Moriya interviewed both of them the next morning. Separately. In the dull room with the filing cabinets and the plant and the cold cup of tea.

He interviewed Kato for three hours. He interviewed Rei for ten minutes.

"Did Kato Ryusei have access to the time and location of the operation?" he said, reading from a file.

"Yes."

"Did you have access?"

"Yes."

"Did anyone else?"

"Everyone in the team. The two officers from the third floor. The Director. Whoever the Director told."

Moriya wrote. "Did you suspect Kato Ryusei of passing information to the subject of the operation?"

"No."

"At any point?"

"No."

"Why not?"

Rei looked at him. "Because he's my partner. I don't check."

Moriya stopped writing. He looked up from the file for the first time. He looked at Rei for a long moment, slowly, the way he looked at everything. His hands. His shoulders. The marks on his throat from the alley in Shin-Ōkubo, fading to yellow now.

"That's not evidence," he said.

"I know."

"It's the opposite of evidence. It's the thing that makes investigators lose their jobs." Moriya picked up his cold tea and looked at it. "Your brother used to say the same thing. About Shimizu. About Okada. *I don't check.*" He put the tea down. "He was right more often than he had any business being."

He closed the file.

"Kato didn't do it," said Moriya. "I'll put that in the accurate record. Not because of what you said. Because I've been watching Kato Ryusei for two years, and he's the only person on the second floor who never goes near the eighth." He stood up and went to the window. "Somebody on your team did. Or somebody near it. Somebody who can be in a room and not be heard." He looked out at the trains. "I don't know who yet."

Rei did.

He had known on the landing, the night before, sitting next to Kato on the step. He had known in the empty flat, the moment the note was read. *Please give my love to Rei.*

Kato could hear everyone on the team. Shimizu. Aoi. The officers from the third floor. Rei.

Everyone except one.

[break The hum]

That afternoon, Rei sat at his desk on the second floor and looked across the room at Tendo Akira.

Tendo was at his desk by the window, leaning back in his chair with his ankle on his knee, reading something on his phone and smiling. His beautiful suit. His hidden lanyard. His easy, handsome, open face.

*Like a radio between stations,* Kato had said. *All the time.*

Rei opened the door in his chest, carefully. One finger. Two. The latch held. He looked at Tendo the way Asakura Mio had taught him by the river in Kyoto. Not at what he had done. At what moved him. At the river.

And he could not see it.

He could see the surface. The smile. The easy confidence. A man who had always had everything handed to him. But underneath, where the river should be, where in everyone else there was grief or joy or fear or longing, there was something in the way. Something lying across Tendo's river like a log across a stream. Smooth and grey and very still. Humming.

Rei had seen something like it before. Once. In a cold flat in Ōtsuka full of paper.

A contract.

Tendo lowered his phone and looked across the room and saw Rei looking at him. He smiled, the real smile, the slightly embarrassed one. He picked up a paper cup from his desk and held it up. *Coffee?* he mouthed. *I'm going down.*

Rei could not answer. He shook his head.

For half a second, across the room, Tendo's pupils went wide. Black to the edges. Looking at Rei the way the thing in the priest had looked at the hall, all of him at once, hungry and delighted. Then they were brown again, and Tendo was smiling, and his lips shaped one soundless word, the way they always did. *Anomaly.*

Tendo shrugged, cheerfully, and went out to get coffee for everyone, the way he did every afternoon. He brought one back for Rei anyway, and put it on his desk with a cream bun beside it, and squeezed his shoulder on the way past.

Rei sat looking at the coffee for a long time. He did not drink it.

It was the worst afternoon he had spent in the Division.
