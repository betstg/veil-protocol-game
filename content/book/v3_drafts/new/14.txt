#title Kato

The message came three days after Akabane, from a number Rei did not know.

*Family restaurant by Itabashi station. The one with the green sign. 11pm. Drink bar's on me. Okada says hi. By which I mean Okada said nothing at all, but she gave me your number, which for Okada is basically a hug.*

Rei read it twice, standing in the corridor at the station between the photocopier and the water cooler. Then he went home, and sat at the kitchen table, and turned to the back of Gin's book, to the torn pages under the heading *The Ones Worth Knowing*, and ran his finger down them until he found the name he was looking for. He had a feeling. He was right.

*Kato Ryusei. Trainee, Field. Telepath. Smarter than he lets on, which he knows, which makes the act a bit exhausting to watch. Carries a white chess king in his jacket pocket and turns it when he's nervous, which is always. Will get you what you need through exactly the channels he pretends not to use. Has said maybe four true things to me in two years. All four were correct. If Rei ever meets him, Rei should not be polite. Polite is the worst thing you can do to him.*

Rei read the last line three times. Then he closed the book and looked at the hook by the door, where nothing hung, and said out loud to the empty flat, "What does that even mean," and the empty flat, as usual, did not know either.

[break The green sign]

Itabashi is the ward west of Kita. It is mostly flats and small factories and long straight roads that go on for a long time without anything happening on them. The family restaurant sat on a corner by the station under a big green sign, its tall windows full of yellow light. Family restaurants in Japan stay open all night. They have enormous menus with a photograph of everything, from spaghetti with cod roe to pork rice bowls to chocolate parfaits taller than a child, and a machine called the drink bar where you pay once and refill your glass as many times as your bladder will allow. At eleven at night they fill up with students cramming, taxi drivers eating with their elbows out, and people who have nowhere they want to go home to.

[need ch14_famires | A Japanese family restaurant at 11pm, big windows, booths, students studying, a trim young man in a neat dark jacket in a corner booth turning a small white chess king in his fingers, two glasses of melon soda from the drink bar, Rei sliding in opposite]

He saw Kato Ryusei at once.

He was alone in a corner booth with his back to the wall, where he could see the whole room and the door. About twenty. Trim, neat, very still, in a dark jacket with the sleeves pushed to the elbow and a plain white T-shirt under it. Short tidy hair. Two tall glasses of fluorescent green melon soda sweating on the table in front of him. And in his right hand, turning slowly over and over between his fingers, a small white chess piece. A king.

He did not look up when Rei came in. While Rei was still five metres away, threading between tables, he said, "Yes. I'm the one in the book."

Rei stopped dead.

"Sit down," said Kato. "You're blocking the waitress. She's about to ask if you'd like to see the dessert menu. You wouldn't. You'd like the cheese hamburg steak with rice, but you're going to order fries, because you're doing sums about rent in your head." He pushed one of the green glasses across the table with one finger. "I got you a melon soda. Everybody likes melon soda. It's the only thing the human race has ever agreed on."

Rei sat. He sat very straight, the way he sat in front of Inspector Kumagai, hands on his knees under the table.

The waitress arrived at his elbow. "Good evening, would you like to see our dessert..."

"No. Thank you." Rei cleared his throat. "Just fries, please."

The waitress went. Kato turned the king between his fingers and did not smile. Quite.

"Excuse me," said Rei, carefully. "How did you know that?"

"Excuse me," Kato repeated, in Rei's exact voice, and then looked a bit sorry he had. "You were thinking it. Loudly. You think very loudly, you know. Like someone talking on the phone on a train." He stood the king up on the table between the two glasses. "That's the first thing to know about me, so let's get it over with. I'm a telepath. Level three. That means I hear the surface of people. What you're feeling. What you're about to say. What you want, and whether you're lying about it. Not your secrets. Not your memories. Just the top layer. Like the foam on a beer." He shrugged one shoulder. "It doesn't switch off. It's never switched off. I've been listening to everybody's foam since I was eleven years old."

He said it lightly, like the setup of a joke. His eyes did not look like it was a joke.

"So. Sorry," he went on. "In advance. I'm going to hear things you don't say. Everybody hates it. Most people go very polite and very careful and start thinking about the weather on purpose. Some people go angry. A guy at the academy threw a chair at me." He picked up his melon soda. "So, you know. Feel free to think about the weather. It's been very humid."

Rei looked at him. At the neat jacket. At the king standing on the table like a sentry. At the way he sat with his back to the wall and one eye always on the room, like someone who had learned to expect a chair.

*If Rei ever meets him, Rei should not be polite.*

Rei thought, very clearly, the way you shout across a car park, *Then just read it. I don't give a shit. It saves time.*

Kato's glass stopped halfway to his mouth.

He looked at Rei. And for the first time his face did something it had not planned. His eyebrows went up. His mouth came open a little. For one second he looked exactly as old as he was. Twenty. Caught.

"Huh," he said.

Rei laughed.

He did not mean to. It came out of him like a cough, the first real laugh since the twelfth of August, because the look on Kato's face was so completely ridiculous. Kato stared at him laughing, with the melon soda still hanging in the air, and visibly did not know what to do with his own face, and that made Rei laugh more, until he had to put his forehead down on the table next to the king.

"Nobody's ever," Kato started, and stopped. He put the glass down very carefully. He picked up the king and turned it, faster now. "Okay. That's new. That is new information. I'm going to need a minute with that."

"Take a minute," said Rei into the table.

"I'm taking it. I'm taking a minute." Turn, turn, turn. "Okay. Minute taken." He cleared his throat. "Your brother did that. You know that? First time I met him, in the canteen. I said sorry, the usual speech, the foam, the beer. And he thought, *I'd rather you heard it than guessed it.* Very loud. Very clear. Like a man reading out a sign. Then he asked me to pass the soy sauce like nothing had happened." He looked at the king. "I've been thinking about that for two years."

Rei lifted his head.

"Though he didn't say *shit*," Kato added. "Your brother has very clean thoughts. It's actually quite annoying."

"He's not that clean."

"No," said Kato, with a small sideways look. "He's not. But he's tidy about where he keeps the dirty ones."

[break The desk]

The fries came. Rei ate them. Kato stole three without asking, chewed, then said "sorry" and pushed the plate back across with the same one finger he had used for the melon soda.

"Right. Business." He took a folded sheet of paper out of the inside of his jacket. "Okada says you found a thing on the river. A hunger thing, looking out of rats. She says you threw iron at it, which is either the bravest or the stupidest thing a civilian has done in Kita this year, and that you came away with a dead lady's name and three files the Division didn't have." He unfolded the paper on the table. "So I went and looked."

"Looked where?"

"In the building. Where I work." Kato flattened the creases with the side of his hand. "I'm a trainee. Second year. I make copies, I carry boxes, I get shouted at. I'm basically you with a lanyard." The corner of his mouth went up. "But I've got a nice face and I talk to everybody and nobody notices me. And I can hear what people are thinking while they stand at the copier waiting for it to warm up. You would not believe what people think at a copier. I could end four marriages tomorrow."

He turned the paper round. A list of addresses. Seven. All along the Arakawa. All in the last two months.

"Your hunger thing," Kato said. "It's seven, not three. Elderly, alone, heart failure, rats every time. Somebody in the building put the pattern together a month ago and flagged it." He tapped the bottom of the page, where somebody had copied a short note and a reference number. "And then somebody else, two days later, closed the flag. *No action. Monitor only.* Signed by an office I'm not going to say out loud in a family restaurant at eleven at night."

"Public Confidence."

Kato did not answer. Which was the answer.

"Why would they leave it?" Rei heard his own voice go up and pulled it back down. "It's killing people."

"Old people. Alone. In summer. Heart failure." Kato's voice had gone flat. Not cold. Flat, like something that had been run over. "If the Division goes in loud along the river, people ask why. If people ask why, people start wanting to know what *else* is moving in the city. And there's a lot moving in the city right now, Rei. A lot more than there ought to be." He folded the paper back up along its creases. "Somebody upstairs has decided that seven quiet funerals are cheaper than one loud question."

Rei put his fry down. He was not hungry any more.

"There's something else," said Kato.

He hesitated. It was the first time he had hesitated about anything, and Rei felt the hairs on his arms lift.

"About your brother."

Rei waited. Kato could hear him waiting. Rei could tell, because Kato winced very slightly, as if Rei's waiting were too loud.

"His desk. In Field Operations. Third floor, by the window. The one with the cactus. I sit four desks along." Kato turned the king over in his fingers, slowly now, very slowly. "Last Friday two people from the office I won't name came up at seven in the evening, after everybody had gone home. They emptied it. Every drawer. Every file. His computer. His mug." He looked down at the table. "The cactus, Rei. They took the *cactus*. Who takes a man's cactus? You only take the cactus if you don't think he's ever coming back to water it."

Rei sat very still.

The restaurant went on around him. A student at the next table yawned enormously and turned a page. A taxi driver ordered a second bowl of rice in a voice like gravel. The drink bar hummed and dropped ice into somebody's glass. Everything went on exactly the same, which seemed impossible.

"I wasn't supposed to see," said Kato. "I came back for my phone charger. They didn't see me. They didn't hear me." He paused. "I heard them. One was thinking about whether *the family* had been told yet. The other one was thinking, very clearly, like it was a relief, *Thank God it's not our problem any more. It's upstairs now.*"

"Upstairs." Rei's mouth had gone dry. "What does that mean?"

"I don't know." Kato looked up at him, and his face was wide open, no performance on it at all. "I don't know, and I'm scared to find out, and I've been carrying it round for a week like a stone in my shoe. And then Okada gave me your number. And I thought, Gin's brother. Gin's brother is out there in Akabane throwing iron at a rat demon with a jar he bought in a hardware shop. Gin's brother would want to know." He set the king down, standing, in the exact middle of the table. "So I'm telling you. I'll probably get sacked for it. Maybe worse than sacked. I don't really care." He breathed out. "Your brother was the only person in that whole building who ever asked me to pass the soy sauce."

[break The real answer]

Rei looked at the king for a while. Then at Kato.

"Why are you helping me?" he said. "Really."

Kato looked back at him for a long moment. Rei had the feeling of being heard. Not his words. Underneath them. Like standing in the sun with your eyes closed, feeling it on your face, knowing exactly where it is without having to look.

"Do you want the funny answer or the real one?"

"The real one."

"Nobody ever wants the real one." Kato almost smiled. "Okay. The real one. I've spent nine years making jokes so people forget I can hear them. It works, mostly. They laugh. They relax. They forget. And then they remember. And they look at me the way you look at a camera in a changing room." He turned the king once more, and then put it away in his jacket pocket, and kept his hand on it there. "And you just sat down and thought *read it, I don't give a shit*. Like it was nothing. Like I was a person and not a camera." He shrugged. "Gin did that too. So I figure it runs in the family. I figure that's worth being near."

Rei did not know what to say. So he thought it instead, as clearly as he could manage. *Thank you.*

Kato heard it. Rei watched him hear it. Something went at the corners of his eyes, and he turned his head fast to the window, to the black road outside and the reflection of the whole bright restaurant floating on it.

"Don't do that," he said to the window. "Don't think nice things at me. I don't know what to do with my face."

"Sorry."

"You're not sorry. I can hear you not being sorry." Kato stood and yanked his jacket straight with both hands, a little too hard. "I'll find out what *upstairs* means. It'll take time. Don't ring me at the office. Don't text me anything you wouldn't want read out in court. If you need me, send me a photo of a cat."

"A cat?"

"Any cat. Everybody sends cats. Nobody reads cats." He dropped a thousand-yen note on the table for the drink bar and the fries. "And Rei. The thing on the river. Seven people in two months, and nobody in that building is going to lift a finger. It's going to do it again."

"I know."

"I know you know. I can hear you knowing." The joke had gone out of his face completely. "I can also hear you thinking you're going to go back there and stop it yourself. With a jar of iron and your friend who paints."

Rei said nothing.

"Yeah," said Kato. "Thought so. Just checking." He hesitated, half turned to go. "Send me a cat when you do. Please."

It was the *please* that did it. Rei looked up. Kato was already walking away between the tables, hands in his pockets, out under the green sign into the night, and he did not look back.

Rei sat alone in the booth for a long time with two glasses of melon soda and the warm place on the table where the king had stood. Then he took out his phone and looked for a photograph of a cat. He did not have one. He had never in his life taken a photograph of a cat.

He went outside and walked three streets until he found a stray tabby washing its back leg on the roof of a parked car with total disregard for the world, and he took its photograph, and saved it, and went home on the last train with the cat in his pocket.
