#title The White House

*The Healer.*

In July the nights did not get cool. The Healer had not known that about this side. On the far side there was no weather. Here the heat sat in the streets after dark like water in a bath, and the insects in the trees made a high dry noise that never stopped, and the body sweated through the grey jacket by nine o'clock and wanted to drink all the time.

It bought a bottle of cold barley tea from a machine at the corner of the street. It knew how to do that now. It held the bottle against the side of the neck, as it had seen a delivery man do, and the body liked that very much.

Then it stood under a tree across the road from the white house, and looked.

[need d3_white_house | A quiet residential street in west Tokyo on a hot July night, old wooden houses and bicycles, a vending machine glowing at the corner, and in the middle a clean modern white house with a white wall, white gate and a hedge clipped dead straight, warm light behind white blinds, a tall man with long black hair in a grey jacket standing under a tree across the road holding a bottle of tea]

It was a quiet street in Asagaya, a long way west. Old wooden houses with small gardens full of potted morning glories. Bicycles under plastic covers. An old man's vest drying on a balcony. And in the middle of them, as out of place as a tooth in a bowl of rice, the white house. Square and clean and new. A white wall. A white gate. A hedge clipped so straight it looked as if someone had used a ruler. White blinds in every window, and soft warm light behind them.

The Healer had been to many places in this city in eleven months. It had not come here. It had felt this house from a long way off for all that time, the way you feel a stove that has been left on in another room. It had kept away.

It had come tonight because it was tired of keeping away.

[break What lived there]

The old thing was inside. The Healer could feel it as plainly as the heat.

It had been on this side a long time. Longer than anyone on the far side had understood. It had been here when this street was fields. It had worn many coats. Tonight it wore a woman in her forties called Fukai Rinka, who had given herself to it gladly, years ago, and who loved it still, the way a dog loves the hand that beats it. Rinka sat in a white room on the ground floor of the white house with a teapot. The old thing sat inside her. Around them, upstairs, in other white rooms, there were others. Twenty. Thirty. Hollowed people, lying on futons with their eyes open, full of small borrowed things from the far side that the old thing had let through to fill them. Its soldiers.

*Kishin*, the Healer thought. It did not say the name. On the far side they had not had names, only what they were. This side gave you a name whether you wanted one or not.

They had known each other on the far side for longer than this city had been here. They had never liked each other. The Healer mended things. It was what it was made of. Kishin broke them and kept the pieces. There was nothing more to it than that, and there did not need to be. On the far side they had stayed out of each other's way. On this side the Healer had come through for one reason. To finish it.

Inside the body, very deep, the man stirred.

He did that more and more now. Since the spring, since the Healer had let him help, he came up a little most evenings and looked out of his own eyes and said nothing. He was looking now.

*That's the house,* said the man. *That's where they take them.*

*Yes.*

*You're not going in.*

*No.* The Healer drank some of the barley tea. *It is too full in there. Thirty of them. In its own house. I would lose, and then your body would be in that house with it.* It considered this. *I do not want that.*

*Since when do you mind what happens to my body?*

The Healer did not answer that, because it did not know.

[break The window]

There was someone in the window across the road.

Not in the white house. In the old wooden house beside it, upstairs, behind a net curtain with a light off. The Healer had felt them when it arrived and had not paid attention. Two people sitting in the dark, watching the white house. Division. They had the flat, careful smell of the Division about them. One of them was the one who listened, the tall one with the tie, from the shrine on the hill in February. The Healer had felt his mind there then. Neat. Like a library.

The other was the younger one.

The Healer felt the man in the body feel it, and go very still, and then begin to rise.

*No,* said the Healer.

*He's right there. He's right there, he's across the road...*

*He is watching the house. He is not watching for you.* The Healer held the man down. Not hard. It had learned, in the spring, that holding him hard made him fight, and holding him softly made him listen. *If he sees you now, he will come down into this street. And then he will be standing in front of that house with me.*

The man stopped rising. He did not go down either. He stayed where he was, just under the surface, looking up through the Healer at the dark window with the net curtain.

The Healer did not care about the younger one. It had no use for him. He was a distraction, and the man in the body was weaker and more stubborn every time he was near. It turned its back on the window and looked at the white house.

That was when the white gate opened.

[break The gate]

Rinka came out.

She was a small neat woman in a pale grey cardigan buttoned to the throat, despite the heat, with her hair in a low bun and soft slippers on her feet. She walked down her own front path between the clipped hedges and stopped at the gate and put her hands on it, one on each side, like a woman leaning on her garden wall to talk to a neighbour.

She looked across the road at the Healer.

Her eyes were black from edge to edge. Wet and shining under the street lamp.

"*Oh,*" she said. Her voice carried very easily across the quiet street. It was a pleasant voice. Warm. "*Oh, it's you. I wondered when you'd come and look. I've felt you walking about for months. All over the city. Like a dog that can't find its basket.*"

The Healer said nothing.

"*And look at you,*" said Kishin, through Rinka's smiling mouth. "*In a man. A strong one. Two gifts. You always did have good taste in coats.*" It tilted Rinka's head. "*Is he awake in there? He is. I can feel him. How sweet. You've let him keep a room.*"

Across the road, upstairs, behind the net curtain, the Healer felt the younger one stand up.

"*You won't come in,*" said Kishin. "*You're not stupid. You'd lose. You never did like losing.*" It smiled. "*You know what I'm going to do, I suppose. Next summer. When the dead come home. You've felt it.*"

"Yes," said the Healer.

It was the first thing it had said aloud. Gin's voice in the hot street was low and flat.

"*And you don't care,*" said Kishin. "*You never did care for them. The living. You only came to stop me.*" It laughed softly. "*We're not so different. We both want something. I want everything. You want me gone.*"

"Yes," said the Healer. "I want you gone. I will end you."

"*Will you.*" Kishin leaned on the gate. "*With what? With him?*"

The door of the wooden house opened.

[break The younger one]

The younger one came out into the street.

He came fast, in a black T-shirt soaked dark down the back with sweat, with the tall one in the tie a step behind him trying to catch his arm and missing. A black and white crow came off the roof of the wooden house and dropped onto his shoulder with a heavy flap. He stopped in the middle of the road, between the white gate and the tree, and looked at the Healer.

The Healer looked back at him.

Inside the body the man was not shouting this time. He was not doing anything. He was only looking, so hard that the Healer could feel it in the eyes, a pressure, like the beginning of a headache.

"Gin," said the younger one.

At the gate, Kishin turned Rinka's head and looked at him, puzzled and polite.

"*And who is this?*" it said.

The younger one did not look at it. He was looking at the Healer. The Healer felt the door in his chest come open, the latch on his wrist lifting, and felt him reach. Across the road. Clumsy and strong and much too open, reaching into Gin's body for Gin, the way you reach into a dark cupboard for a thing you know is there.

He did not get far. The Healer did not let him. It was not hard.

But behind the white gate, Kishin had felt him reach.

Rinka's black eyes went to the younger one properly now. Interested. And upstairs in the white house, behind the white blinds, something moved. Many somethings. The Healer felt them sit up on their futons, all at once, thirty hollow people, and turn their faces toward the windows.

"*Oh,*" said Kishin softly. "*Oh, what a lot of room you've got.*"

The tall one in the tie had the younger one by the arm now. Both hands. He was saying something low and urgent. The Healer heard the shape of it. *Not here. Not tonight. Rei. Thirty of them. Rei.*

The younger one did not move.

The Healer looked at him for one more second. At the face that was in every room of Gin's memory. Thinner than in the memories. Older. Very angry. Very frightened. Bleeding a little from the nose, already, from reaching.

He would not win anything here tonight. Neither would the Healer.

It set the bottle of barley tea down on the pavement at the foot of the tree, carefully, so it would not fall over. Then it took one step back into the dark under the branches, and used the man's other gift, and was gone.

[break After]

It came out on a footbridge over the railway lines at Ogikubo, two stations east, and stood there in the hot dark with the trains going by underneath.

Inside, the man was very quiet.

*He said my name,* said the man at last.

*Yes.*

*He looked straight at me. He knew.*

*He has known since February.* The Healer watched an orange train go under the bridge with its windows full of tired faces. *And now the old thing has seen him. And it does not know what he is. And it wants to.*

The man did not say anything for a long time.

*You left him there,* he said. *In the road. In front of that house.*

*The one with the tie was taking him away. The old thing will not come out of its gate tonight. It never comes out. It sends others.* The Healer put its hands on the warm rail of the bridge. *I could not take him with me. You know that. And I could not stay.*

*You didn't want to stay.*

*No,* said the Healer.

It was the truth. It had not cared, in the street. It had looked at the younger one and seen a distraction and an open door and nothing else.

And yet it had set the bottle down carefully, so it would not fall over, at the foot of the tree, where the younger one would see it. It did not know why it had done that. It had not decided to.

The man had felt it do that. The Healer could tell. He did not say anything about it.

They stood on the bridge until the last train had gone.
