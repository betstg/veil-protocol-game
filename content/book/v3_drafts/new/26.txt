#title Someone Dies

*Gin.*

He woke up because of the sound.

A long scream of tyres on wet road. Then a bang, not loud, a dull flat bang like a bag of rice dropped on a floor. Then a clatter of metal sliding and spinning. Then nothing.

Gin opened his eyes. Or his eyes were open, and he arrived behind them.

He was standing on a pavement at night. It was raining lightly. He knew the corner before he knew anything else. The big crossing at Higashi-Jūjō, by the shuttered pharmacy, two stations from home. He had stood on this kerb a thousand times with a bag of shopping. Once with Rei on his shoulders, when Rei was four, pointing at the trains. In front of him was a wide road, black and shining, with the lights of a crossing reflected in it, red, then green. A truck was disappearing down the road, fast, its red tail lights getting smaller. It did not stop. And in the middle of the road, in the wet black shine, lay a bicycle on its side, its front wheel still turning, and a young man.

He was a delivery rider. Gin could see the big square insulated box from a food delivery company, burst open on the road, and white plastic containers of food scattered across the tarmac. Curry. Rice. A bottle of tea rolling slowly toward the gutter. The young man lay on his back with one leg bent under him in a way that legs do not bend. His helmet had come off. He was moving his arms slowly, like a swimmer.

And there was blood. Gin could see it, even in the dark, even in the rain. It was coming from the young man's thigh, high up, near the hip. A lot of it. Too fast. Pulsing.

*Femoral,* Gin thought. Six years of field training. A hundred first-aid courses. He knew exactly what it was. He knew exactly what to do. Get across the road. Kneel. Put your knee into the top of the thigh, hard, with all your weight, and press, and do not stop pressing until the ambulance comes. He had done it twice before. Once in Akabane, once in a house in Kawaguchi. Both times the person had lived.

He was nine metres away.

*Go,* he thought. *Go. Go. Go.*

His body did not move.

He was holding an umbrella. A clear plastic umbrella, from a konbini. His right hand held it over his head, steady, and the rain ran off it in little streams. His left hand was in his coat pocket. His feet were together on the kerb. He was standing very still and very straight, like a man waiting for a bus, and he was watching.

*Go,* Gin screamed, inside. He threw everything he had at his legs. At his hands. At the hand holding the stupid umbrella. *Drop it. Drop it and go. He's dying. He's dying. I know how to stop it. Let me go.*

*I know,* said the voice.

Calm. Courteous. Very close. The old doctor in the old hospital.

*I can see that you know,* it said. *You know a great many things. It is one of the things I most admire in you.*

*Then let me go!*

*No,* said the voice gently.

In the road, the young man had turned his head. He was looking at the pavement. At the tall man with the umbrella. Gin saw his face clearly in the light from the crossing. He was about twenty. Thin. A small scar through one eyebrow. His lips were moving. Gin could not hear him over the rain, but he could read it.

*Help me.*

*Please,* said Gin. He had never said please to anything in his life the way he said it then. *Please. I'll do anything. I'll stop fighting you. I'll sleep. I'll let you have it, all of it, the body, everything, just let me cross the road. Two minutes. Give me two minutes. Please.*

The voice was quiet for a moment. Gin felt it consider. He felt it actually think about the offer, the way you turn a coin over in your fingers.

*He does not owe me anything,* it said at last. *And I do not owe him anything. There is no debt between us. It is not my place to take what is not owed, or give what is not owed. That is not what I am.* A pause. *I am sorry. Truly. I can see that this is very hard for you.*

The young man in the road stopped moving his arms.

[break Watching]

It took four minutes.

Gin watched all of it. He could not close his eyes. He could not look away. The voice did not make him watch. It simply did not look away itself, and so he saw what it saw, every second. The young man's lips stopped moving. His hand opened on the wet road, slowly, like a flower. The blood stopped pulsing and only ran. The front wheel of the bicycle slowed, and slowed, and stopped. The bottle of tea reached the gutter and tipped in.

A taxi came down the road and stopped with a squeal. The driver got out and shouted and ran to the young man and knelt beside him in the wet and put his hands on him and shouted for someone to call an ambulance. A woman came out of an apartment building in her slippers with a phone at her ear. Lights came on in windows.

Nobody looked at the tall man standing on the far kerb with his umbrella.

When the ambulance came, nine minutes later, the young man had been dead for five.

Then Gin's body turned and walked away down the pavement. Unhurried. Holding the umbrella. Its footsteps steady and even on the wet stone.

Inside it, Gin was screaming.

He did not stop. He screamed the way he had not screamed since he was a child. Not words. Just sound, endless, from somewhere so far down inside that he had not known it was there. He screamed for the young man with the scar through his eyebrow. He screamed for the curry and the rice on the road, for somebody's dinner that would never come, for somebody waiting in an apartment somewhere, looking at their phone, wondering where their food was. He screamed for his own knee, which knew how to press, which had pressed twice before and saved two lives, and which had stood on a kerb nine metres away and done nothing.

Nobody heard him. Nobody could hear him. The street was full of people and lights and sirens and none of them could hear him at all.

*Hush,* said the voice. *Hush. Rest now. You are making yourself ill.*

He did not hush.

*I'll never forgive you,* he said. It came out ragged and broken, but it came out. *Never. Do you understand? Whatever you want. Whatever is owed. I will never forgive you for this.*

The body stopped walking.

It stood in the rain on an empty pavement under a street lamp, very still, for a long moment.

*Forgive,* said the voice slowly. As if it were trying out a word in a foreign language. *Forgive.* A pause. *That is a kind of debt, is it not? To forgive. You release a debt that is owed to you. You choose not to collect.* Another pause, longer. *I have never understood why anyone would do that.*

*Then you don't understand anything,* said Gin.

The voice did not answer. For the first time since it had come into him, it did not have an answer.

The body started walking again. But slower now. And Gin, sinking back down into the dark, felt something he had not felt before. A small hesitation in it. A catch. Like a man who has walked the same road a thousand times and suddenly, for no reason, is not sure if he has turned the right way.

He held on to that feeling as the dark came up over him. He held on to it with everything he had left.

*You don't understand anything,* he thought, falling. *But you heard me. I know you heard me.*

The last thing he felt was his left hand, in his coat pocket, closing very slowly into a fist.

He was not sure if he had done it, or the voice had.
