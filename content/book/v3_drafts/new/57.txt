#title He Touched

*The Font.*

Someone has touched the empty place.

The light feels it as a sleeper feels a hand on his shoulder. Not the weight. The warmth. For a long time nothing has come near that place. The twelve small lights go round in their great ring, and none of them ever turns toward the gap, because they are afraid of it, and they are right to be. The light holds the gap from underneath, as it holds everything, and does not think about it.

And now there is a hand on it. Small. Warm. Steady. With a little iron latch at the wrist.

The light turns.

It has not turned in a very long time. It did not know that it still could. It is like the stiffness of someone who has slept all night sitting up and wakes at the last station with no idea how he got there.

[break The little light]

The little light is standing at the edge of the gap.

Not in it. At the edge. With one hand on the hollow, as you put your hand on the frame of a door you are not sure you are allowed through. The light can feel it more clearly than it has felt anything. Small and bright and very tired, more tired than anything that young should be, with the grey thread of the dying one caught in it like smoke.

It is the right shape. The light knew that already. But it is one thing to know a key is the right shape, and another to hear it turn.

The gap hums. The whole ring hums. Twelve lights flicker across the white, all at once, as if someone has opened a window in a house full of candles. Somewhere in the city above, a cook in a small restaurant drops a bowl. A man in a boiler room stands up too fast and hits his head on a pipe. A young woman by a river in Kyoto puts her hand flat on the table and does not know why.

And under the gap, under all of it, the light shifts its weight.

[break The question]

The little one is going to ask.

The light can feel it coming. A question, standing at the front of the boy like a man at the edge of a platform. The light has had questions before. Not many. Once in a long while someone strong and lucky and desperate gets as far as the white and stands at the edge and calls into it. The light answers if it can. One question. It is a rule older than the light knows.

It waits for this one. It knows what it will be.

The light knows where the brother is. The light knows where everything is. It could tell him. It would be easy.

And if it told him, he would go. Tonight. Alone. With a latch on his wrist and his nose bleeding.

The light waits. It does not know what it will do.

The question does not come.

[break He stayed]

The boy does not ask. He does not turn round. He does not look at the light, though he knows it is there behind him. He stands at the edge of the hollow with his hand on it and stays. That is all. As someone waiting at a bus stop late at night stays beside a stranger who has fallen asleep on the bench, so that if the stranger wakes, someone else is awake too.

Nobody has done that.

The light does not know what to do with it. It is like being handed a cup of tea by someone who wants nothing back.

And with it, from nowhere, a smell. Sudden and very strong. Wet ink. Lamp oil. A paper lantern at a wooden gate, with one character on it, black on orange. A voice behind the gate, young and cheerful and impatient. *You are late again. The tea is cold. Again.*

The light holds on to the voice as hard as it can, and it goes anyway, like water through cupped hands.

[break Go home]

The boy is still there. Still touching the hollow. And the hollow is beginning to want him.

The light can feel that too. The gap has been empty a long time, and it is hungry the way a mould is hungry for whatever is poured into it. If he stays much longer it will close round him, and everything that runs through that place will run through him at once, and he is not ready, and it will burn him down to nothing.

So the light pushes.

Gently. As you move a child back from the edge of a pond. Not with a hand. It has no hands. With the whole of itself, for one moment, turned toward him.

The little one goes.

[break After]

The light holds.

There is warmth on the hollow where the boy touched it, the shape of a palm and five fingers, and the warmth does not fade. It stays there, small and steady, like a lamp left on in the upstairs window of a house.

The twelve lights are settling. The cook is sweeping up the bowl. The man in the boiler room is swearing at the pipe. The young woman in Kyoto has gone back to her coffee.

The grey one is still going out. The light could not stop that.

And somewhere far away in the city, in a body that is not its own, the stranger who came through last summer has stopped in the middle of a footbridge and turned its head toward the white. It felt it too.

The light goes back to the work.

But it does not turn back round. Above it the twelve settle, and the city goes on, and on a walkway in Jūjō a boy lies on a sofa with his foot off the end, and the light, holding everything, keeps that one small place at the edge of the white where a hand was laid, and does not let it cool.
