#title Remembering

*The Font.*

The dead are not coming home.

The light feels it before midnight. It always feels the last night of Bon, every year, as an old man feels rain in his knees. A warmth along the edges of the city. A thousand small doors opening onto a thousand small doorsteps, and on each doorstep a little fire, and the dead going back into the dark past the fires, slow and puzzled and content, like guests leaving a house where the lights are still on.

This year the warmth is wrong.

It is all in one place. North. By the river. Where the old lines go down into the water. A great false brightness, like a lamp held up in a window by someone who does not live there, and the dead turning toward it, one after another, like moths.

The light has felt that before.

It does not know how it knows. It has never felt anything before that it can remember. It is a light. It holds the ring of twelve and the empty place beside them, and it has no eyes and no memory and no road.

It has seen that before.

[break The lantern]

It remembers a gate.

Not as a picture. It has nothing to see pictures with. As a smell, a weight, a warmth, the way a sleeper remembers a room.

Wooden. Old. A roof of grey tiles over it, as gates had roofs then, and from the beam under the roof a paper lantern hanging, orange, with three characters on it in black. The light could not read them, the night the boy touched the empty place. It can read them now. It does not know when it learned again.

*逢魔時.*

The meeting hour.

Not the hour. The office. *The Office of the Meeting Hour.* Behind the gate, a long low building with paper walls and a gravel yard and a well, and inside, desks, low desks, and ledgers, and brushes, and men in plain dark coats who went out at dusk and came back after dark with mud on their sandals and something in their faces that they did not talk about at dinner.

The light remembers the coats.

It remembers the weight of one. On shoulders. Shoulders that it had once. Dark blue cotton, too heavy for summer, with a notebook in the inside pocket that bumped against its ribs when it walked. It remembers walking. A road along a river. Willows. The light going orange and gold and long, every shadow three times the length of the thing that cast it, and the feeling, every evening, at that hour, of the world going thin. Of doors opening. Of the other side leaning close, to see.

And it remembers not being afraid.

That was the strange thing. Everyone else in the office was afraid, a little, at that hour. They walked the road with salt in their sleeves and their hands on their charms. They were on the side of the living. They held the line.

The one in the blue coat did not hold the line. He walked down the middle of it.

[break The middle]

It remembers a man in a ledger. A case. Somebody's grandmother who would not leave a house. The others wanted to drive her out with salt and bells. The young man in the heavy blue coat with ink on the side of his first finger, sat down on the floor of the house beside the old woman's shadow and asked her what she was waiting for.

She was waiting for her son to come home from the sea.

He had drowned twenty years before.

The young man in the blue coat did not tell her so. He sat with her until dawn and listened, and wrote it all down, and in the morning he walked to the harbour and found the son's name in a temple register, and walked back, and sat down beside her again, and read it to her. And she got up, and thanked him, and went.

The others thought he was strange. Too kind to the dead. Too patient with things that should have been driven off. *You walk too close to the other side,* the head of the office told him once. *One day you'll walk right over.*

He did not walk right over. He walked the middle. The road between. With the living on one hand and the dead on the other, and himself in the centre, belonging to neither and listening to both. It was the only place he had ever felt at home.

The light holds very still, and feels the memory settle into it like warmth into a stone.

*The middle way.*

[break Obon]

And it remembers an Obon.

A long time ago. Before this city had this name. The fires on the doorsteps. The dead coming home. And somewhere, north, by the river, a false brightness, a lamp in the wrong window, and the dead turning toward it.

He had gone. Alone. In the blue coat, with the notebook. Because it was the meeting hour and he was the one who walked the middle and nobody else would go. And he had found her there. Not this face. Another face. A kind face, a woman's face, pouring tea for the dead at the edge of a river and telling them they could rest now, they did not need to go home, home was so far, and so cold, and here was so warm.

He had sat down on the far side of her teapot. He had refused the tea. He had listened to her for a whole night, as he listened to everything.

And then...

The light does not remember the rest. It reaches for it, and it is not there. A blank. A white. The white it lives in now. As if the night by the river were the last page of a book, and after it, nothing, only light, only holding, three hundred years of holding, and no road and no coat and no one waiting at home with tea gone cold.

*Kaname. You are late again.*

The young voice behind the gate. Cheerful and impatient. And a name in it. The light hears the name, and holds it, and turns it over. It is a good name. Somebody's. It does not feel like anybody the light knows.

*The tea is cold. Again.*

He never went back. The light understands that now, very slowly, like a word in a language it has not spoken for a very long time. He went to the river at Obon to stop a kind thing with a teapot, and he never came home, and someone waited.

[break The ring]

Around the light, in the white, the ring burns.

Eleven bright. Brighter than they have been for years. Something has happened to them. Someone stood among them this afternoon with a broken door in his chest and they all put their hands on it, and they have not quite let go.

One grey and empty and warm, a chair with somebody's warmth still in it.

And the empty place. Hollow. With the shape of a palm and five fingers on its edge, where the boy touched it, still warm, after three weeks.

And north, by the river, the false brightness, getting stronger.

And north, by the river, standing very straight at the edge of the false brightness, two in one body, both awake. The stranger who came through last summer to end the kind thing with the teapot. And the tired man it is wearing, who is the right shape for the empty place, and does not know it.

And beside them, in the weeds by a broken gate, a boy with a latch on his wrist and a key round his neck. The same shape. Smaller. Newer.

The light feels them all.

Then, very slowly, it does something it has not done in a very long time.

It turns.

It is not much. It is only turning. It is still holding, still carrying the empty place, still keeping the skin of everything tight. Nothing in the city changes. Nobody feels it but the twelve in their ring, which flicker, all at once, like candles when a door opens somewhere in the house.

But it has turned.

And for the first time since a night by a river, a long time ago, it is facing north.
