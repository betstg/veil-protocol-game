#title Remembering

*The Font.*

The dead are not coming home.

The Font feels it before midnight. It always feels Obon coming, every year, the way an old man feels rain in his knees. A warmth along the edges of the city. A thousand small doors opening onto a thousand small doorsteps, and on each doorstep a little fire, and the dead coming up out of the dark toward the fires, slow and puzzled and glad, the way people come up the road toward a house where the lights are on.

This year the warmth is wrong.

It is all in one place. North. By the river. Where the old lines go down into the water. A great false brightness, like a lamp held up in a window by someone who does not live there, and the dead turning toward it, one after another, the way moths turn.

The Font has seen that before.

It does not know how it knows. It has never seen anything. It is a light at the bottom of a column. It holds twelve lights in a ring and carries the dark thirteenth and it has no eyes and no memory and no road.

It has seen that before.

[break The lantern]

It remembers a gate.

Wooden. Old. A roof of grey tiles over it, the way gates had roofs then, and from the beam under the roof a paper lantern hanging, orange, with three characters on it in black. The Font could not read them, the night the boy touched the chair. It can read them now. It does not know when it learned again. It reads them the way you read your own name on a letter you did not know someone had sent you.

*逢魔時.*

The meeting hour.

Not the hour. The office. *The Office of the Meeting Hour.* Behind the gate, a long low building with paper walls and a gravel yard and a well, and inside, desks, low desks, and ledgers, and brushes, and men in plain dark coats who went out at dusk and came back after dark with mud on their sandals and something in their faces that they did not talk about at dinner.

The Font remembers the coats.

It remembers the weight of one. On its shoulders. Its own shoulders. Dark blue cotton, too heavy for summer, with a notebook in the inside pocket that bumped against its ribs when it walked. It remembers walking. A road along a river. Willows. The light going orange and gold and long, every shadow three times the length of the thing that cast it, and the feeling, every evening, at that hour, of the world going thin. Of doors opening. Of the other side leaning close, to see.

And it remembers that it was not afraid.

That was the strange thing. Everyone else in the office was afraid, a little, at that hour. They walked the road with salt in their sleeves and their hands on their charms. They were on the side of the living. They held the line.

The Font did not hold the line. It walked down the middle of it.

[break The middle]

It remembers a man in a ledger. A case. Somebody's grandmother who would not leave a house. The others wanted to drive her out with salt and bells. The Font, who was not the Font then, who was a young man in a heavy blue coat with ink on the side of his first finger, sat down on the floor of the house beside the old woman's shadow and asked her what she was waiting for.

She was waiting for her son to come home from the sea.

He had drowned twenty years before.

The young man in the blue coat did not tell her so. He sat with her until dawn and listened, and wrote it all down, and in the morning he walked to the harbour and found the son's name in a temple register, and walked back, and sat down beside her again, and read it to her. And she got up, and thanked him, and went.

The others thought he was strange. Too kind to the dead. Too patient with things that should have been driven off. *You walk too close to the other side,* the head of the office told him once. *One day you'll walk right over.*

He did not walk right over. He walked the middle. The road between. With the living on one hand and the dead on the other, and himself in the centre, belonging to neither and listening to both. It was the only place he had ever felt at home.

The Font remembers that now. It holds very still, at the bottom of the column, with its knees drawn up, and feels the memory settle into it the way warmth settles into a stone.

*The middle way.*

[break Obon]

And it remembers an Obon.

A long time ago. Before this city had this name. The fires on the doorsteps. The dead coming home. And somewhere, north, by the river, a false brightness, a lamp in the wrong window, and the dead turning toward it.

He had gone. Alone. In the blue coat, with the notebook. Because it was the meeting hour and he was the one who walked the middle and nobody else would go. And he had found her there. Not this face. Another face. A kind face, a woman's face, pouring tea for the dead at the edge of a river and telling them they could rest now, they did not need to go home, home was so far, and so cold, and here was so warm.

He had sat down on the far side of her teapot. He had refused the tea. He had listened to her for a whole night, the way he listened to everything.

And then...

The Font does not remember the rest. It reaches for it, and it is not there. A blank. A white. The white it lives in now. As if the night by the river were the last page of a book, and after it, nothing, only light, only holding, four hundred years of holding, and no road and no coat and no one waiting at home with tea gone cold.

*You are late again.*

The young voice behind the gate. Cheerful and impatient.

*The tea is cold. Again.*

He never went back. The Font understands that now, very slowly, the way you understand a word in a language you have not spoken for a very long time. He went to the river at Obon to stop a kind thing with a teapot, and he never came home, and someone waited.

[break The ring]

Above the Font, in the white, the ring of lights burns.

Eleven bright. Brighter than they have been for years. Something has happened to them. Someone stood among them a week ago with a broken door in his chest and they all put their hands on it, and they have not quite let go since.

One grey and empty and warm, a chair with somebody's warmth still in it.

And the thirteenth gap. Hollow. With the shape of a palm and five fingers on its edge, where the boy touched it, still warm, after six weeks.

And north, by the river, the false brightness, getting stronger.

And somewhere in the city, in a small yellow kitchen with a frosted window, two shapes in one body, both awake, both afraid, both the right shape for the hollow in the ring. The courteous old thing. The tired man it is wearing.

And on a sofa in Kita, with his foot over the end, in spite of being told, a boy asleep with a key in his fist.

The Font looks at them all.

Then, very slowly, it does something it has not done in four hundred years.

It puts one hand flat on the ground of the white, beside its knee. And it pushes. The way an old man pushes himself up out of a low chair.

It has knees. It has arms. It has hands, with ink on the side of the first finger.

It stands up.

It is not much. It is only standing. It is still at the bottom of the column, still holding, still carrying the dark thirteenth on its back like a sleeping child. Nothing in the city above changes. Nobody feels it but the twelve lights in their ring, which flicker, all at once, like candles when a door opens somewhere in the house.

But it is standing.

And for the first time since a night by a river, a long time ago, it is facing north.
