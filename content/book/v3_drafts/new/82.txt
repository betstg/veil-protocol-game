#title Kishin

She came up the grey beach at a walk.

A small neat woman in a pale grey cardigan buttoned to the throat, with her hair in a low bun and her feet bare on the grey sand, and a little river stone on a cord swinging at her neck. Behind her the low white table, and the cushions, and the teapot still steaming. In front of her, at the edge of the weeds, a tall man in a grey jacket with the sleeves pushed up, standing very straight, with two people in him.

She stopped three metres away.

*Well,* she said. Not out loud. In the air. In the weeds. In the empty frame where Rei's door had been. *Well. Look at you. The mender. Come all this way through the veil to find me, and put on a policeman, and learn to buy rice.* She tilted Rinka's head. *I've watched you all year. I couldn't believe it. You healed a child's arm on a bridge tonight. A child. Nobody asked you to. What were you going to do, mend the whole city?*

The body did not answer.

*You were the strongest of us,* she said. *On the other side. You could have mended anything. You could have mended the skin of the world if you'd wanted. You never wanted. You only ever wanted me gone.* She smiled. *And now you've gone soft in a borrowed coat, and you're going to lose to me on a beach, and the man inside you is going to watch.*

She lifted her hand.

[break Despair]

Rei felt it go into them.

Not a blow. Not fire. It was what she was. Grey and quiet and enormous, the river he had stood in that afternoon in the round room with eleven hands on his back. All of it. Every person in the city who had ever thought *what's the point*. Poured, all at once, into one body on the edge of a beach.

The body bent.

Rei felt the voice in it go under. Not far. A long way. The old thing that had come through to end her, that had been patient and cold and certain for longer than the city had been here, went under the grey like a stone into a pond. It had never felt despair. It had never had anything to feel it with. It had no idea what to do.

Gin did.

Rei felt his brother catch it. Inside. In the place under the stomach where Gin had felt things when he was nine and had nobody to tell. Gin caught the despair the way he had caught Rei falling off the kitchen chair when he was three, without thinking, with both hands, and held it.

*I know this one,* Gin said, inside, to the voice. Rei heard it through the empty frame, as clearly as if he were standing in the room. *I know this. It's not new. Everybody's got it. Get up. It's heavy. It's not the end. Get up.*

*I do not know how,* said the voice. Very small.

*Like the cup. On the bench. You hold it. It's the same weight. You get stronger.* Gin's voice cracked. *Come on. I've got the other side. Get up.*

The body straightened.

Slowly. One vertebra at a time. Like a man standing up from a very low chair with something very heavy on his shoulders. It stood on the edge of the weeds with the grey river still going into it and through it, and it did not go under again.

*Oh,* said Kishin. Interested. *Oh. He's carrying you.*

"We're carrying each other," said Gin.

Out loud. With his own mouth. Rei heard it. Gin's voice, rough and dry and a little too fast.

[break What Gin said]

Kishin's face went very still.

"You think he's gone soft," said Gin. The body's jaw set, as Gin's jaw set over a bad report. "He has. I watched him do it. He sat on an old man's step and drank tea for ten months and listened. He mended a boy's arm on a bridge tonight and the boy said thank you and he didn't know what to say back. He grieved an old man who was nothing to him. He told me his name over three cups." The body's hands closed into fists. "He's not a coat and I'm not a coat. There are two of us in here, and we decided. On a beach. Ten minutes ago."

*You,* said Kishin. Very quietly. *You're awake. He let you.*

"He moved over," said Gin. "Like on a bench. You wouldn't know. You've never sat next to anybody in your life. You only ever sat across a table from them and poured."

And the voice spoke, very quietly, in Gin's mouth, in the polite careful voice that set each word down like a cup.

"You broke things," it said. "On the other side. For as long as there has been another side. You broke them and you kept the pieces. That is all you have ever been. A thing made of pieces that belong to other people." The body took a step. Onto the grey sand. "I mend. That is all I have ever been. I came through to end you, and I did not know how, because ending is not a thing I know how to do." Another step. "Tonight I found out that I do not need to end you. I only need to do what I am. I am going to put the pieces back."

[break Mended]

He put his hands on her face.

Both of them. Gin's long hands. Flat, on either side of Rinka's face, as she had put her hand on Tanizaki's cheek on the step. Gently. And Rei felt them do it together, Gin and the voice. One pair of hands, two people. Gin's clairsentience, finding every piece. The mender's gift, putting each one back where it had come from.

Rei never forgot what it looked like.

It looked like somebody mending a broken bowl. Every piece. Every chip. Every crack. Found, and turned the right way up, and set back in its place.

He felt it go out of her. Not into the body. Not into the river. *Back.* A grief she had drunk a hundred years ago went back to a house in Fukagawa that was not there any more, and settled into the ground under a car park, and was quiet. A lonely boy she had sat with long ago got his loneliness back, and with it, somehow, the memory of the friend he had made the week after, which she had taken too. Mika's longing went back to Mika, sitting in the weeds beside an old dog. Kaneda's tiredness went back to Kaneda, on a walkway in Tabata, with a glass of barley tea. The woman in the cardigan got back the warmth of a hand on her knee, and with it the knowledge that it had not been real, and under that, smaller, the knowledge that a boy holding out his hand on a walkway had been.

Thousands of them. Tens of thousands. Going out of her into the dark like birds released from a net.

And as each one went, there was less of her.

That was the thing. That was what she had never known, in all that time, because she had never once given anything back. She was made of what she had taken. Nothing else. And as the mender found every piece and put it back where it belonged, there was less and less of her left to hold the shape.

*Stop,* said Kishin.

Her voice was very small now. Not the bottom of a lake. A puddle.

*Please.*

The hands did not move.

"That word," said the voice quietly, "you have to mean." It looked at her out of Gin's eyes. "I learned that this year. It took me a year. You do not have a year."

The last piece went.

It was very small. A cup of tea. Offered, on a step, by an old man in a faded blue jinbei, at the end of his life, to the thing that was killing him. *You came all this way. Have some tea.* She had not taken it. She had walked away. But for one second she had wanted to. Rei had seen it. Something small and quick, very far down, like a hand reaching out by old habit for a thing it had not been offered in a very long time.

The mender found that too. And put it back on the step.

And there was nothing left of Kishin at all.

[break Rinka]

The woman on the beach did not fall.

She stood with Gin's hands on either side of her face and blinked. Her eyes were brown. Small and ordinary and wet. She looked up at the tall man in front of her as if she had never seen him before, and she had not.

"Where," she said. Her own voice. Thin and hoarse, like a voice that has not been used for years. "Where is she. Where did she go."

The hands came away from her face, very carefully.

"She is gone," said the voice. "There is nothing left of her."

Fukai Rinka looked round the grey beach. At the low white table. At the teapot. At the cushions. Her face crumpled.

"She was the only one who ever..." she said, and could not finish it, and sat down on the grey sand beside the table with her knees drawn up and put her face on them and cried. Not quietly. Like somebody whose god has died.

The little river stone on its cord had slid off her neck and lay in the sand. Grey, with a white band through it. Ordinary. Just a stone.

It cracked. Very quietly. Along the white band. And lay there in two pieces.

Gin's body knelt down on the grey sand beside her. It did not touch her. It knelt there, a little way off, and stayed. Rei could not tell, from where he stood, which of them was doing it. He thought perhaps it was both.

[break The sea]

Out on the grey sea, the shapes in the fog went very still.

Rei felt them look. At the beach. At the woman crying on the sand. At the empty table with nobody to call them to it any more. At the tall man kneeling, who turned his head and looked out at them across the grey water. Calmly. Politely. Like a doctor looking up from a patient at somebody who has come into the room without knocking.

*Next,* said that look. *Who's next.*

The shapes turned.

One by one. Slowly. Without any hurry. Like clouds when the wind changes. They turned away from the beach, and went back down into the fog, and the fog took them, and the grey sea was empty.

Inoue Misaki lowered her hand. The bubble of nothing in front of her went out like a soap bubble popping. She looked at her palm with mild interest. On her shoulders, the ginger cat yawned.

"Huh," she said. "That's that, then."

Beside her, Kurosawa took his hands out of his pockets for the first time.

And at the edge of the beach, where the old rails ran down into the water, the grey began, very slowly, to go out of the sea.

Not all of it. Not yet. The night was still thin. But Rei could see the real river now, black and ordinary, coming up through the grey like a street through morning fog. The lights of the city on it. A long way off, on the other bank, somebody's last sending fire still glowing red in a dish on a balcony.

He looked down at his own chest. At the empty frame where his door had been. Everything was still coming in.

And through it, very faint, from very far away, from somewhere beyond the white and the grey and the river and the city, something he had felt on the back of his neck twice before and had never once turned to look at.

The light.

It had turned. He could feel it. The way you can feel, with your eyes shut, that someone in the room has turned to face you.

It was facing north.

It was facing him.
