#title Kishin

*Yushiro.*

She said it the way you say the name of a dog that has run off and come back. Fondly. Without any doubt at all that it will come.

*Sit.*

Rei felt it go through the body beside him like a hook through a fish.

It was not a sound. It was not a voice. It was older than voices. It was the oldest thing Rei had ever felt, older than the river, older than the bell, older than the white light at the bottom of the column. A claim. A debt called in. *You are mine. You have always been mine. Come.*

Gin's body took a step forward.

Rei felt both of them in it. He felt Yushiro try to stop. He felt the effort of it, enormous, like a man trying to stop a train by standing in front of it. He felt Gin, beside the voice, inside, throwing his whole weight against the leg, the way he had thrown himself against the inside of his own skull on a bench outside a tobacconist's. And the leg moved anyway. The way a leg moves when somebody you have obeyed your whole life says *come here* and you are already walking before you have decided anything.

A second step. Toward the river. Toward the grey beach and the white table and the ring of thirteen cushions with a gap in it.

*Good,* said Kishin. *Good. Come and sit down. You've been away so long.*

[break The claim]

Rei did not move his feet. "*Yushiro.*"

He said it.

Not loud. Not the way Mori said *no*. Not the way Kishin said *sit*. He said it the way he had said it on Tanizaki's step, testing the weight of it. Like a cup set down on a table. Placed. Clear. With everything that was coming in through the empty frame in his chest behind it.

His hand came up, open, toward the body's back. "Yushiro. *Don't.*"

The body stopped.

Half a step. One foot lifted. Hanging there, in the air, above the weeds.

*Oh,* said Kishin. Softly. Interested. *Oh. You gave it to him. Your name. You gave it away.*

The body did not answer. It stood with one foot lifted, between them, the boy behind it and the woman in front, and it shook. All over. The way a rope shakes when two people are pulling it from both ends.

*That was very foolish,* said Kishin. *You know what you are. You know what a name is. Whoever holds it can call you. And now two of us hold it.* She tilted her head. *Well. Let's see whose call is older.*

She said it again.

*Sit.*

The body lurched. The lifted foot came down. Forward.

"*Yushiro.*" Rei took a step after it. "Don't. You don't owe her. Listen to me. You don't owe her anything."

*He owes me everything,* said Kishin. *Shall I tell you what?* She looked past the body at Rei, with great kindness. *Before there was a river here, before there was a city, before there were people to have names, there was a hunger. A small one. A hunger for things to be owed. To be counted. To be paid. It had no shape. It had no name. It drifted. It was nothing.* She smiled. *And I found it. And I gave it a name. Yushiro. And with a name, it had a shape. And with a shape, it could take. And it has been taking ever since, and every single thing it ever took, it took with the name I gave it.* Her voice was very gentle. *That is the debt. Its name. Its whole self. Everything it is. It owes me for being anything at all.*

The body stood still.

Rei felt Yushiro inside it. Felt him hear it. Felt him know it was true.

*Sit,* said Kishin. *You can't refuse me. You never could. I made you.*

[break What Gin said]

And Gin spoke.

Out loud. With his own mouth. Rei heard it. Gin's voice, rough and dry and a little too fast, coming out of the body that had been standing still with one foot in the weeds.

The body's jaw set, the way Gin's jaw set over a bad report. "No. That's not how names work."

Kishin's face went very still.

"You gave him a name," said Gin. "Fine. You gave it to make him owe you. You gave it with a hook in it." The body's hands, both of them, closed into fists. "He gave it to my brother on an old man's step without one. Nobody asked him. Nobody was owed. He just gave it." Gin breathed. Hard. "A thing that's given can't be a debt. That's the whole point of giving. You'd know that if you'd ever once given anybody anything."

*You,* said Kishin. Very quietly. *You're awake.*

"I've been awake for a year," said Gin. "He let me. Every day. He sat at a table and drank tea and I told him what kind of thing he was, and he listened. Every day. He learned." The body's head lifted. "He grieved an old man he owed nothing. He said please. He said sorry and meant it. He took a girl out of your white house and she gave him a packet of tissues and he didn't know which way up to hold them." Gin's voice cracked. "He's not yours. He's not anybody's. He's ours. Me and my brother. Because we asked."

The body stood in the weeds with its fists closed and two people in it.

And Yushiro spoke, very quietly, in Gin's mouth, in the polite careful voice that set each word down like a cup.

The body's hands opened. "I have paid you."

[break Paid]

Kishin did not move.

"You gave me a name," said Yushiro. "I have owed it to you since before the river. I know. I have never forgotten it for one day." The body took a step. Not toward the gap in the ring. Toward her. "I gave the same name away, a week ago, to a boy on a step. Freely. Without a hook. And when I gave it away, I found that I did not have it any more in the way I had it before. It was not mine to owe. It was mine to give. And I gave it." Another step. "You cannot call in what has been given away. That is the only rule older than you."

*That isn't a rule.*

"It is now," said Yushiro. "I am Duty. I keep the accounts. I have kept them since before this city had a name. And I am telling you, as the one who keeps them, that the account is closed." The body stopped at the edge of the grey beach. "I owe you nothing."

The hook went out of him.

Rei felt it. He felt it come out, the way a splinter comes out, all at once, with a little sharp tug and then nothing. The body straightened. It stood on the edge of the grey sand with its fists unclenched and its shoulders down, as if something very heavy that it had been carrying for a very long time had been lifted off its back.

And Kishin, for the first time, stepped back.

[break The account]

Yushiro lifted his head. "But you owe a great deal."

He walked onto the grey beach.

The grey sand did not hold him back. It did not pull him down. He walked across it in Gin's body with Gin awake beside him, and Rei felt them both, and felt them walk in step, the way two people walk in step when they have been walking together long enough that they no longer have to think about it.

Kishin did not step back again. She stood beside her low white table with her hands at her sides and her kind borrowed face perfectly composed.

*Do I,* she said.

"You have taken," said Yushiro, "and never paid. Not once. I have watched you do it for longer than I can count. Every grief you ever drank. Every lonely child you ever sat with. Every man who came to your door at the end of his strength and found you kind. Every woman who let you put your hand on her knee. You took all of it, and you have never paid for one cup." He stopped in front of her. Close. "You took a clairsentient named Fukai Rinka, who held grief for other people for her whole life, and you put the lights out in her one by one and wore what was left. You took an old man on a step who offered you tea." His voice did not change. "You took the despair of a man who held it for this city for years so nobody else would have to, and you tried to take the last of him in his own chair."

*Everybody takes,* said Kishin.

"Yes," said Yushiro. "And everybody pays. Tonight, you pay."

He put his hands on her face.

Both of them. Gin's long hands. Flat, on either side of her face, the way she had put her hand on Tanizaki's cheek on the step. Gently. And Rei felt them do it together, Gin and the voice. One pair of hands, two people.

And Yushiro did the thing he had been made to do.

He kept the accounts.

[break Returned]

Rei never forgot what it looked like.

It looked like a ledger being read aloud. Every line. Every entry. Every single thing she had ever taken, called out by name and handed back.

He felt it go out of her. Not into Yushiro. Not into the river. *Back.* To where it came from. A grief she had drunk in 1923 went back to a house in Fukagawa that was not there any more, and settled in the ground under a car park, and was quiet. A lonely boy she had sat with in 1965 got his loneliness back, and with it, somehow, the memory of the friend he had made the week after, which she had taken too. Mika's longing went back to Mika, sitting in the weeds beside an old dog. Kaneda's tiredness went back to Kaneda, at the top of a hill in Kita, lighting fires. The woman in the cardigan got back the warmth of a hand on her knee, and with it, the knowledge that it had not been real, and under that, smaller, the knowledge that the boy who held out his hand on the walkway had been.

Thousands of entries. Tens of thousands. Going out of her into the dark like birds released from a net.

And as each one went, she got smaller.

That was the thing. That was what she had never known, in all that time, because she had never once paid for anything. She was made of what she had taken. Nothing else. Not one thing in her was hers. And as Yushiro read out the account and gave it all back, line by line, there was less and less of her left to hold the shape.

Her face changed. The kind face. Fukai Rinka's face. It did not crack or snarl or burn. It simply began to look like what it was. A woman's face, tired, with a few grey hairs left in on purpose, and nobody behind it. Nobody at all. A house with all the lights out.

*Stop,* said Kishin.

Her voice was very small now. Not the bottom of a lake. A puddle.

*Please.*

Yushiro's hands did not move.

"That word," he said quietly, "is not owed. You cannot call it in. You have to mean it." He looked at her. Gin's dark attentive eyes and the old careful thing behind them, both at once. "I learned that from a boy. It took me a year. You do not have a year."

The last line of the account went out.

It was very small. A cup of tea. Offered, on a step, by an old man in a faded blue jinbei, at the end of his life, to the thing that was killing him. *You came all this way. Have some tea.* She had not taken it. She had walked away. But she had wanted to, for one second. Rei had seen it. Something small and quick, very far down, like a hand reaching out by old habit for a thing it had not been offered in a very long time.

That went back to the step, too.

And there was nothing left.

[break The stone]

Fukai Rinka's body stood on the grey beach for a moment longer, with Gin's hands on either side of its face.

Then it folded. Slowly. Gently. The way a coat folds when you take it off a hook. Gin's hands went down with it and laid it on the grey sand beside the low white table, carefully, the way you lay down somebody who has fallen asleep on a train.

The little river stone on its cord slid off her neck and lay in the sand.

Grey, with a white band through it. Ordinary. Just a stone.

It cracked. Very quietly. Along the white band. And lay there in two pieces.

Out on the grey sea, ten vast shapes in the fog went very still.

Rei felt them look. At the beach. At the empty dress on the sand. At the ring of thirteen cushions round the white table with nobody to call them to it any more. At the gap.

At the body standing by the table with two people in it, which turned its head and looked out at them across the grey water. Calmly. Politely. The way a clerk looks up from a ledger at somebody who has come into the office without an appointment.

*Next,* said that look. *Who's next.*

The shapes turned.

One by one. Slowly. Without any hurry. The way clouds turn when the wind changes. They turned away from the beach, and went back down into the fog, and the fog took them, and the grey sea was empty.

Inoue Misaki lowered her hand. The bubble of nothing in front of her went out like a soap bubble popping. She looked at her palm with mild interest.

She flexed her fingers. "Huh. That's new."

Beside her, Kurosawa took his hands out of his pockets for the first time.

On the grey beach, Gin's body knelt down in the sand beside the folded dress and the cracked stone, and stayed there, on its knees, with its head bowed. Rei could not tell, from where he stood, which of them was doing it. He thought perhaps it was both.

And at the edge of the beach, where the old rails ran down into the water, the grey began, very slowly, to go out of the sea.

Not all of it. Not yet. It was still the meeting hour, still held open by something, still thin. But Rei could see the real river now, black and ordinary, coming up through the grey the way the street comes up through the fog in the morning. The lights of the city on it. The lanterns. A long way off, on the other bank, somebody's Obon fire on a doorstep.

He looked down at his own chest. At the empty frame where his door had been. Everything was still coming in.

And through it, very faint, from very far away, from somewhere beyond the white and the grey and the river and the city, a long low hum.

The column.

Something at the bottom of it was standing up. Something with knees and arms and ink on the side of its first finger. Something that had been holding the light at the centre of everything for three hundred years, alone, and the dark thirteenth chair on top of it for seven.

And it was facing north.

It was looking at him.
