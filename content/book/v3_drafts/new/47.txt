#title Teleport

The message came at eleven at night on the twenty-sixth of April, from a number Rei did not know. It was one line.

*Shin-Ōkubo. Hyakuninchō 2, the alley behind the Korean church. Now. Bring iron. Inoue.*

He was there in nineteen minutes. He did not stop to think. He was learning, slowly, that the people at the round table did not send messages like that unless they meant them.

Shin-Ōkubo is the Korean neighbourhood of Tokyo, just north of Shinjuku. In the day it is packed with teenage girls buying K-pop posters and cheese hot dogs and face masks from Seoul. At night the main street stays bright and loud with Korean barbecue restaurants and karaoke and music, but the side streets go dark very fast, narrow and twisting, full of old apartment buildings and small churches and shuttered grocers with Korean signs.

The alley behind the church was very dark. And it was full of people.

[break The hollow ones]

There were twelve of them. Rei counted later. At the time he only saw a crowd. Ordinary people. A woman in an office suit. A teenage boy in a school blazer. An old man in slippers. A young mother with a baby carrier on her chest, empty. A delivery driver still in his helmet. They stood in a loose half circle in the alley, quite still, very close together, facing the wall at the end.

Against the wall, with his back pressed to the bricks, was Park Junho. The thin young holder of Longing, with the tired eyes, from the round table. His phone was in his hand, its screen lit. His face was white.

And all twelve of the people facing him had the same eyes.

Huge. Black. Pupils swollen to the edges. Wet and shining in the light from Park's phone. And all twelve pairs of eyes were moving together. At exactly the same moment. The same direction. Like one animal with twenty-four eyes.

Rei felt his wrist go so cold it burned. He opened the door in his chest two fingers. Three.

They were empty.

That was the worst thing. Not possessed, like Mrs. Tachibana, with something driving. Not shared, like the man with the cats. Empty. Hollowed out, like Fujimoto Kenji's flat, all the furniture gone. People who had sat in a white room and drunk tea and been listened to, so kindly, so patiently, until there was nothing left in them but a thin grey thread, running back west across the city to a gentle woman pouring tea. They were not wearing anything. They were being worn, from a long way away, like gloves on the end of very long fingers.

"*Longing,*" said all twelve of them at once.

Twelve voices, from twelve mouths. A man's deep voice, a woman's high one, a boy's voice not yet broken, the old man's thin croak. All saying the same word, at the same moment, in the same flat tone, like a choir that had been taught one note.

"*Longing. So much longing. Let us in. We'll fill it. We'll fill everything.*"

They took a step forward together. Park pressed back against the wall.

"Uedera." A voice beside Rei's ear. "Good. You came. Stay behind me."

Inoue Satoru was standing next to him. He had not been there a second ago. He was tall and lean and his jacket with many pockets was torn at the shoulder, and there was blood on his knuckles, and he was breathing hard. He was looking at the twelve hollow people with an expression of total concentration.

"They're people," said Rei. "Inoue-san, they're just people. Ordinary..."

"I know what they are." Inoue did not look at him. "That's why I'm not killing them. That's why this is taking so long." He flexed his bloody hand. "They came for Park an hour ago. Out of nowhere. Kishin's pets. It's reaching for another chair." His mouth tightened. "It's greedy. I told Mori it'd be greedy."

"What do I do?"

"Tell me where they're going to be." Inoue looked at him for one second. His eyes were dark and fierce and very tired. "Mori says you read rooms. Read this one. Tell me where they're going to move before they move." He turned back to the alley. "I'll do the rest."

And he was gone.

[break How a teleporter fights]

Rei had seen Itsuki open a door from Higashi-Jūjō to the Sumida. He had seen Sōun open a door to the sea. He had thought he knew what teleportation was.

He did not.

Inoue Satoru did not open doors. He simply stopped being in one place and started being in another, with nothing in between. One moment he was beside Rei. The next he was behind the teenage boy in the school blazer, three metres away, and his hand was already coming down on the back of the boy's neck, flat and hard, exactly where the skull met the spine. The boy dropped like a puppet with its strings cut. Before he hit the ground, Inoue was gone. He was behind the delivery driver. The driver's helmet went flying. The driver fell. Inoue was gone. He was behind the woman in the office suit, catching her as she dropped, laying her gently on the wet ground, gone.

It took less time than it takes to tell it. Three people down in two seconds.

The other nine turned. Together. All nine heads swinging round at the same moment, all eighteen black eyes searching the alley. Hunting. Hungry.

And Rei saw where they were going to go.

It was like the rivers in Kyoto. Not the past. Not what they were. Where they were *leaning*. With the door in his chest open three fingers, he could feel the shape of the next second in the alley, the way you feel the shape of a wave just before it breaks. The old man in slippers was going to go left, toward the bins. The young mother was going to go for Park. Two of the others were going to go for Rei.

"Left!" he shouted. "The old man, left, by the bins! The mother's going for Park! Two coming for me!"

Inoue was there. By the bins. Before the old man got there. He did not strike the old man. He caught him, by both arms, gently, like catching a falling child, and laid him down. Then he was in front of Park, between Park and the young mother with her empty baby carrier, and the mother walked straight into his outstretched hand and he touched her forehead, once, lightly, with two fingers, and she folded at the knees and sat down on the ground and was still.

The two coming for Rei were a man in a grey suit and a girl of about Yua's age in a school uniform. They came fast, running, with their black eyes wide and their arms out, and their mouths open, saying *longing longing longing* in their two voices.

Rei had the iron. He did not throw it.

He did not know what made him do it. He opened the door in his chest one more finger's width, and looked at the girl in the school uniform. At her river. Not the grey thread pulling her. Underneath. What had been her, before the white room.

*Wonder.* Bright and small and faded, like a firework seen through fog. A girl who had once loved stars. Who had wanted to know how everything worked. Who had been so lonely, at school, being different, that a kind woman in a grey coat had found her on a bench in Kabukichō and given her a card with a white flower on it.

"Your name's Saki," he said. He did not know how he knew. "You like the stars. You wanted to be an astronaut."

The girl stopped running.

She stood in the alley, a metre from him, with her arms still out. Her black eyes were fixed on him. And for one second, something moved in them. Behind the black. Very small. Very far down. Like someone at the bottom of a well looking up and seeing, for a moment, a circle of sky.

"*...stars...*" said the girl. In her own voice. A real girl's voice, thin and frightened.

Inoue appeared behind her, and touched her neck, and caught her as she fell.

The man in the grey suit hit Rei like a car.

[break Object insertion]

They went down together on the wet ground. The man was heavy and strong and his hands were round Rei's throat at once, squeezing, and his face was ten centimetres from Rei's face, and his eyes were black and huge and drinking him in. Rei could not breathe. He could see the grey thread coming out of the man's chest, pulling west, and something coming down it the other way. Something cold and soft and patient. Looking at him through the man's eyes.

"*Rei,*" said the man, in a soft woman's voice. "*Hello again. You came to save poor Park? So kind. Just like...*"

Inoue appeared above them.

He did not grab the man. He did not strike him. He put his bloody hand out flat, palm down, a few centimetres above the man's head, and closed his eyes.

And the man's mouth was suddenly full of iron.

Rei saw it happen. One moment the man's mouth was open, speaking in Kishin's soft voice. The next, it was packed. Full of black iron filings, from Rei's own jar, which Rei felt suddenly go light in his jacket pocket. Inoue had moved the filings. Not by throwing them. By teleporting them. From the jar, in Rei's pocket, to the inside of the man's mouth, with nothing in between.

The man screamed through the iron. A high thin shriek, Kishin's shriek, coming out of a man's throat. His hands came off Rei's neck. He rolled away, clawing at his mouth, spitting black filings onto the wet ground. And the grey thread, the one running out of his chest toward the west, went slack. Snapped. Whipped away into the dark like a cut fishing line.

The man lay on his back on the ground, coughing up iron, gasping. His eyes were brown. Ordinary. Terrified. He was looking up at the sky and crying.

"Where am I," he was saying. "Where am I, where am I, what is this, where..."

[break The count]

Twelve people on the ground in the alley behind the church. Inoue went from one to the next, kneeling, two fingers on each neck. Rei sat against the wall with his hands on his bruised throat, next to Park, who was shaking so hard his teeth chattered.

"Eleven," said Inoue at last. He was kneeling by the old man in slippers. He had not moved for a long time. "Eleven breathing."

Rei looked at him.

"The old one," said Inoue quietly. "His heart. It was already going. The thread was holding him up. When I cut him off it..." He did not finish. He sat back on his heels on the wet ground and looked at the old man's face, at the slippers, at the thin ankles in grey socks. "Eighty, maybe. Somebody's grandfather. Went to a nice lady's house for tea because he was lonely. And then he came here to help eat a stranger, and died in an alley."

Nobody said anything. Far away, on the main street, someone was singing karaoke, a Korean love song, very badly.

Inoue Satoru stood up. He looked down at his bloody knuckles. At the old man. At the eleven people starting to stir on the wet ground, groaning, frightened, not knowing where they were. Then he looked at Rei.

"Every chair," he said. "It's going for every chair it can reach. Park tonight. Who next?" He wiped his hand on his torn jacket. "I'm going to Mori. Now. Stay with Park. The Division'll be here in ten minutes. Don't be here when they come." He paused. "You did well. The girl. The stars. I've never seen anybody do that." He frowned. "Don't do it again. You opened too far. I felt it from across the alley."

And he was gone.

[break The stairs]

Rei did not go home. He took Park three streets away, up a narrow staircase above a Korean grocer that smelled of garlic and sesame oil, to a door that a small, thin, wary girl with short black hair opened before he knocked.

Choi Hana looked at him. At Park, grey and shaking. At the marks on Rei's throat.

"Inoue called," she said. "Get in."

She made them tea, Korean barley tea, in a tiny kitchen with a window onto the alley. She gave Park a blanket and a bowl of rice porridge and made him eat it. She did not ask any questions. When Park was asleep on her sofa, with the blanket over his head, she came and sat on the stairs outside her door, where Rei was sitting, and lit a cigarette.

They sat on the stairs for a while. The smell of garlic came up from the grocer. Far away, the karaoke had stopped.

"You did the thing," said Hana. "With the girl. Inoue told me. You said her name."

"I saw it. Underneath."

"Yeah." Hana smoked. Her left sleeve had ridden up her arm a little, as it always did when she lifted the cigarette, and the edge of the thick pale scar showed on the inside of her forearm. She did not pull it down this time. She looked at it. "I could do that once. Before." She was quiet for a moment. "This. You want to know about this. Everyone does. They look at it and they wonder."

"You don't have to..."

"I know I don't have to. Shut up." She turned her arm in the light from the door, so that the whole scar showed, thick and ridged, from wrist to elbow. "I was fourteen. There was a man. A teacher. He was very nice. He gave me tea. He said he understood about the things I could see. And one night..."

Her phone rang.

It was loud in the quiet stairwell. A cheerful little tune. Hana looked at the screen, and her face changed. All the hardness went out of it.

"It's my grandmother," she said. "In Busan. She only calls if something's wrong." She stood up, already answering, already speaking fast in Korean, already going down the stairs two at a time with the phone at her ear and the cigarette still burning in her other hand.

At the bottom of the stairs she stopped. She looked back up at Rei for one second, over the phone.

"Later," she said, in Japanese. "I'll tell you later."

Then she was gone, out into the dark, into the alley, still talking.

Rei sat alone on the stairs above the grocer for a long time, with the smell of garlic and the sound of Park breathing behind the door.

*There was a man. A teacher. He was very nice. He gave me tea.*

He thought of a white room. A low table. A gentle woman pouring tea.

He did not think it was the same story. He hoped very much that it was not.
