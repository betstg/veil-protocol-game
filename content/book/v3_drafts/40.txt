#title Tendo
#part Book Three. The Thirteen

They set the trap on a Monday.

Shimizu planned it. She sat in her office with the blinds down and Rei and Kato on the other side of her desk and Kuma asleep on her feet, and she did not say anything for nearly ten minutes. The small line between her eyebrows came and went. Then she took out four blank cards and wrote on each one.

"Four operations," she said. "None of them real. Four different places. Four different times." She laid the cards on the desk. "Each one goes to one person. Only one. Aoi gets this one. The two officers from the third floor get these. And Tendo gets this."

She pushed the last card a little apart from the others.

"If someone from the white house turns up at one of these places," she said, "we'll know which card they read."

"And if nobody turns up?" said Kato.

"Then I'm wrong," said Shimizu. "And I've insulted four colleagues for nothing. And I'll apologise to all of them." She looked at the cards. "I'm not wrong."

[break Thursday]

On Thursday evening, at the place on Tendo's card, a quiet park in Asagaya with a pond and a broken fountain, the woman in the grey coat sat down on a bench at exactly the time written on it.

Rei watched her through Gorō's eyes, from a branch of a tree across the pond, with the hand mirror cold in his fingers and blood starting to run from his nose. She sat for twenty minutes. She looked round the park, slowly, carefully. She looked at the bushes and the paths and the empty fountain. She looked, once, straight up at the tree where Gorō was sitting, and smiled at him, a soft kind teacher's smile.

Then she stood up, and left a white chrysanthemum on the bench, and walked away.

Gorō screamed and flew.

At the other three places, nobody came.

[break The accurate record]

Moriya did it in his office on Friday morning, with the door shut.

Rei was there because Moriya asked for him. Shimizu was there. Kato was there, standing by the filing cabinets with his hand in his pocket. Kuma was lying across the doorway like a draught excluder, so that nobody could leave without stepping over him.

Tendo Akira sat in the chair in front of Moriya's desk with one ankle on his knee and listened to the whole thing without interrupting. The cards. The park. The chrysanthemum on the bench. The flat in Nakano. He listened with polite interest, like a man hearing a story about someone he had met once at a party.

When Moriya finished, Tendo smiled.

"That's very good," he said. "Shimizu-san, that's very good. Four cards. I should have thought of that." He looked at Rei. "Was it you? Did you see it? You did, didn't you. In the office. I saw you look." He shook his head admiringly. "Nobody's ever seen it before. Not in nine years. Not even Moriya."

"See what?" said Moriya.

"The contract," said Tendo cheerfully. "The thing in my river. Go on, Uedera. Tell him."

Everyone looked at Rei.

"Something's lying across him," said Rei slowly. "Where his feeling should be. Underneath. Something grey and still. Like a log in a stream. It hums." He looked at Kato. "That's why you can't hear him."

"Bingo," said Tendo. He uncrossed his legs and leaned forward with his elbows on his knees, as if they were all friends having a drink. "I was fifteen. My father wanted me to be the best. That's his whole thing. He's on every committee and every board and every council in this country, and he wanted his son to be the strongest psychokinetic the Division had ever seen." He shrugged. "So he found something for me. A small thing. From the far side. Very old, very quiet, very obliging. And it signed a contract with me. Not a Vessel Program contract. A private one. It lives in me, across my river, very still. It makes my fire hotter. It makes me impossible to read. And in return..." He smiled. "Well. In return, I let it see."

"See what?"

"Everything I see." Tendo spread his hands. "It's curious. That's all it is. Curious. It's been looking out of my eyes for thirteen years. Every room I walk into. Every person I meet." His pupils went wide, for one second, black and shining, and came back. "It's had a very interesting year. It's never seen anything like you, anomaly."

[break Upstairs]

"Who did you tell?" said Moriya. His voice was very quiet. "About Nakano. About the park."

"Oh, not the white house. Not directly. I've never met Kanna-sensei." Tendo looked almost offended. "I told upstairs."

The room went still.

"The ninth floor," said Tendo. "Protocol. They found me when I was nineteen. My contract makes me very interesting to them. They study things like me. People with things in them." He looked at Kato, and at Rei. "People who read wrong on every instrument in the building." He smiled. "They promised me something. That one day they'd tell me what I am. What the thing in me really is. What it's done to me. Nobody else will ever tell me that. My father won't. The Division won't. Upstairs will." He sat back. "So I tell them things. What the team does. Where it goes. What it finds. Especially about you, Uedera." He shrugged again. "What they do with it after that isn't my business."

"They gave it to the Circle," said Shimizu flatly.

"Did they?" Tendo raised his eyebrows. "Probably. Upstairs likes the Circle. The Circle makes interesting things. Hollow people. Vessels. Ready-made. Protocol watches. Protocol takes notes. Protocol has, I think, a little arrangement." He looked at the window. "Hagiwara-san manages the public side. Very elegantly. She has dinner with my father once a month."

Kato made a sound. A small, sharp, sick sound.

Tendo looked at him with something almost like sympathy.

"Sorry, Kato," he said. "You were the obvious answer. It's not personal. It's just that if anyone was going to be looked at, it was always going to be the telepath." He turned to Rei. "And you just *didn't check*. That was the bit I didn't see coming. I've been thinking about it all week. You looked at him and you just *knew*." He shook his head. "I'd love to know what that's like. Being trusted like that." He paused. "I bet it's the most interesting thing in the world."

[break Fire]

"You're suspended," said Moriya. "As of now. Pending full investigation." He opened a drawer and took out a form. "Your lanyard and your Capturer, please. You'll be escorted from the building. Everything you've said will go in the accurate record."

"No, it won't," said Tendo pleasantly.

He snapped his fingers.

Every file in Moriya's office caught fire at once.

Not with a roar. With a soft, eager *whump*, like a gas ring lighting. Every grey filing cabinet along the wall, every drawer, every folder, burst into tall white flame from the inside at the same moment. The heat hit Rei like a slap. The plant on the windowsill blackened and curled. The cold cup of tea on Moriya's desk boiled and cracked.

Moriya did not move. He sat behind his desk with the form in his hand and watched his life's work burn. Ten years of the accurate record. Every name. Every report. Every thing that someone in the building had done that they should not have done. Rei saw his face in the white light. It did not change at all. Only his hand, holding the form, went slowly into a fist.

"Kuma!" said Shimizu.

The dog was already moving. He came off the floor in the doorway and went straight for Tendo, low and fast and silent, the way he had been trained. Tendo looked at him and the dog stopped. Mid-leap. Hung in the air. And then was thrown sideways across the room into the burning cabinets with a yelp of pain that Rei would hear for a long time.

Tendo looked at the dog lying against the burning cabinets, and his face went white. "No," he said. "No, no, I didn't mean. Kuma. I'm sorry. I'm so sorry." His hand was shaking.

He stood up. He brushed off his beautiful suit. He took the lanyard from inside his jacket and dropped it on Moriya's burning desk. Then he stepped over the place where the dog had been and walked to the door.

Rei stood in his way.

He did not decide to. He was simply there, in the doorway, with the heat of the fire on his back and his left hand on his wrist, on the latch.

Tendo stopped. He looked at Rei, interested.

"Are you going to open it?" he said softly. "The door? I'd love to see. They'd love to see, upstairs. They'd give a great deal to see." His pupils went wide. Black. Shining. "Go on. Open it. Show us what's behind it."

Rei looked at him. At the black eyes. At the thing behind them, curious and cold and humming, looking out of a man who had sold it his whole life for the promise of an answer.

He did not lift the latch.

"No," he said.

Tendo looked at him for a long moment. Something moved across his face. Disappointment. And under it, very faint, something that might once have been envy.

"I'm sorry," he said. "I liked this team. I liked it more than anything I've ever had." His eyes were wet. "I just wanted somebody to tell me what I am."

Then he put his hand flat on Rei's chest, quite gently, and pushed, and Rei flew backward down the corridor and hit the far wall, and slid down it, and the world went grey for a while.

[break After]

When it came back, the fire alarm was screaming and the corridor was full of smoke and people running with extinguishers. Kato was kneeling beside him. Shimizu was kneeling beside Kuma in the doorway of the burned office, with her hands on his side, and her face was perfectly calm and her hands were shaking.

Tendo Akira was gone.

Nobody had stopped him. He had walked down the corridor and into the lift and down to the lobby and out through the front doors of the Division building, past the security desk, past the guards, in his beautiful suit, and nobody had stopped him.

The next morning there was a memo on the board outside the trainee room.

*Field Officer Tendo Akira has been transferred to a special assignment, effective immediately. His duties will be redistributed. Thank you for your understanding.*

There was no signature. There was no department. At the bottom, very small, where a department name should be, there was only a number.

*9.*

Aoi stood in front of the board on her crutches for a long time. She had liked him. He had made her laugh in the car with his terrible singing. He had carried her bag up the stairs at the temple. He had driven her home every night for three weeks when her leg was bad and never once complained.

"He carried my bag," she said. "Every night. He carried my bag."

Kato put a hand on her shoulder. She did not shake it off.

In his office on the fifth floor, among the black wet ashes of ten years of files, Moriya Tatsuki sat at his burned desk with a new notebook, and began, very slowly, in small neat handwriting, to write it all down again from memory.

The plant on the windowsill was dead. In the afternoon, someone brought him a new one. Nobody knew who.

Rei knew. He had seen Okada carry it up the stairs.

Kuma lived. His back leg was broken in two places and he would limp for the rest of his life. He came back to the second floor six weeks later with a cast and a new red and white silk cord, and lay down across Rei's feet under his desk, and did not move from there for a week.

[break Two new names]

On the first Monday of June, Shimizu called the team into her office. All of them. Rei, Kato, Aoi on her crutches, and Kuma in his cast on his blanket in the corner.

There were two other people in the room.

One was Moriya Tatsuki. He was standing by the window in his slept-in suit, with his lanyard tucked away, holding a cup of tea. He did not look like a man who had just watched ten years of his work burn. He looked exactly the same as always. Stocky. Tired. Calm. Only his eyes were different. They moved round the room slowly, the way they always did, but now there was something at the back of them, hard and still, like a nail.

"Moriya-san has asked for a transfer to field," said Shimizu. "For six months. The Director has signed it."

Kato stared at him. "You *asked*? For *field*? You hate field. You told me field was where people went to make mistakes you'd have to write down."

"It is," said Moriya. He drank his tea. "I've decided I'd like to see some of them happen. Before they get to my desk." He looked at Rei. "And I'm tired of writing down what other people tell me. I'd like to see what's on the ninth floor with my own eyes. That's harder from the fifth."

He was a psychometabolist. Level five. He read bodies, living and dead, and could keep a body going long past the point where it should stop. Rei thought of Aoi's leg and the bell and Kato's knee pressing for eighteen minutes, and was very glad.

The other person in the room was standing very straight by the door, with his hands folded in front of him.

Rei knew him. He had seen him once, in the cold garden of the Tsukishiro house, in formal black, looking at the tattoo on Rei's wrist with a face like a closed file.

"Fujiwara-Kage Sena," said Shimizu. "Auditor. The Director has assigned him to this team for six months. After what happened with Tendo, the eighth floor wants an Auditor in the room." Her voice was very flat. "He will come on every operation. He will write a report on every operation. He will read every report we write."

Sena bowed. A small, exact, correct bow. "I look forward to working with all of you," he said. His voice was measured and quiet, every word set down carefully. "I'll try not to be in the way."

"You'll be in the way," said Kato.

"Probably," said Sena. He did not smile. "That's what an Auditor is for." He looked at Rei. "Uedera. We met in November. You were pouring tea."

"I remember."

"You pour very well. For a trainee." Sena's eyes went, for one second, to Rei's left wrist. To the edge of the black mark under the cuff. Then back to his face. "I've read your assessment. Adept. Clairsentience four." A pause. "I've also read the instrument logs from the Box in February. And from the Kawaguchi library, the reading room, on the twenty-first of April. There's a sensor in the ceiling there. Nobody remembers it." He looked at Rei steadily. "They don't say Adept."

The room went very quiet.

"Sena," said Moriya, from the window. Quietly.

"I haven't filed anything," said Sena. "I'm only saying. So that we understand each other." He folded his hands again. "My family voted yes on your brother's file, Uedera. I want you to know I didn't agree with it. I also want you to know I'm not here to be your friend. I'm here because one person on this team already lied to all of you for four months, and nobody saw it until it was too late." He looked round at all of them. "I'm here so that doesn't happen twice."

Kato was looking at him with an expression Rei had not seen before. Not a joke. Something careful.

"He means it," Kato said at last, to the room. "All of it. Every word." He sounded surprised. "He's thinking about whether he'll be any use. And whether we'll hate him. And he's decided he doesn't care if we do, as long as nobody else on the team gets hurt." He turned his king over in his pocket. "That's very annoying. I wanted to hate him."

Sena looked at Kato for a long moment. "You're the telepath."

"I'm the telepath."

"Good," said Sena. "Then you'll know when I'm lying. That will save us both time."

Rei looked at Kato. Kato looked at Rei. Neither of them said anything.

"Six," said Shimizu. "We're six now. One of us went to the ninth floor. One of us is on crutches. One of us has a cast. One of us," she looked at Sena, "reads everything we write. And one of us," she looked at Kuma, "is a dog." She opened a file. "It will have to do."
