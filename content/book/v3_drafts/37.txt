#title Watanabe Jun
#part Book Three. The Thirteen

Watanabe Jun spent his days in a public library in Kawaguchi, just across the river from Kita, in the reading room on the third floor, at the table by the window.

"He doesn't live anywhere, really," Chen Lian had told Rei. "He has a room. He doesn't go to it much. He sits where it's quiet and people leave him alone." She had looked sad. "Despair's like that. It doesn't need a home. It needs a chair in a corner."

[img scene_rei_watanabe_library.png | The reading room in Kawaguchi]

The reading room was big and bright and silent, with long tables and green-shaded lamps and old people reading newspapers on wooden poles. A student was asleep on a pile of textbooks. A man in a suit was doing a crossword. At the table by the window, alone, Watanabe Jun sat with a book open in front of him that he was not reading.

He looked worse than at the meeting. Three weeks worse. His face was thinner. His skin had a grey, papery look, like a leaf at the end of autumn. The grey streaks in his hair had spread. He sat very still, with his hands flat on the table on either side of the book, and looked out of the window at the river.

Rei sat down opposite him. Watanabe did not look up.

*Hello, Uedera,* he said. Not aloud. Inside Rei's head, the way Sōun had spoken on the first day of training. A quiet, soft, slow voice, like someone speaking in a library. *Sorry. It's a library. And I'm a telepath. It's easier.*

*Hello,* thought Rei, as clearly as he could.

Watanabe smiled very faintly. *You think loudly. Like Kato. Do you know Kato?*

*He's my partner.*

*Good. He needs someone who isn't afraid of him.* Watanabe turned a page of the book he was not reading. *You came to see how long I've got.*

[break What despair is]

Rei did not answer that. He opened the door in his chest a finger's width, and then, carefully, a little more. Two fingers. Three. The latch let him. He was learning how.

And he saw.

Watanabe Jun was being eaten.

It was not like Mrs. Tachibana, where the thing was inside, driving. Nothing was inside Watanabe. He was entirely himself. But through him, all the time, despair was flowing. The despair of the whole city, all of it, every person in Tokyo who woke up and thought *what's the point*, every person on a station platform who looked at the tracks too long, every person who had stopped hoping. It flowed into Watanabe the way the Kamo flowed under the bridge in Kyoto. That was his job. To hold it. To let it flow through him, steady, so that it did not flood.

And something was drinking it.

Rei saw the grey thread clearly now. It came out of Watanabe's chest, just below the heart, thin and grey and taut like a fishing line, and went out through the window and away across the river, west. It was pulling. Steadily. And it was not only pulling the current. It was pulling him. Every hour, a little more of Watanabe Jun went down that thread with the despair he was holding. His colour. His warmth. His memories, maybe. Rei could see places in him that had gone hollow, like the inside of a tree that is still standing but will fall in the next storm.

The tips of his fingers, flat on the table, had gone grey. Not pale. Grey. The grey of ash.

*Two months,* said Watanabe. *Maybe three. Before the summer, anyway.*

*Can't anyone stop it?*

*I've tried.* Watanabe looked at the river. *If I fight it, if I close up, the current spills. All of it. The whole city's despair, with nobody holding it. Do you know what that would look like?* He did not wait. *It would look like a lot of people standing at the edge of a lot of platforms on the same morning.* He turned a page. *So I don't fight it. I hold it, and it drinks, and I hold it some more.*

Rei felt sick.

*People think despair is sadness,* said Watanabe gently. *It isn't. Sadness still cares. Sadness cries because it wanted something. Despair is when you stop wanting. When you think nothing will ever change, so why move.* He looked down at his grey fingertips. *It's the most dangerous of the thirteen. Rage at least gets up in the morning. Despair stays in bed and lets the house burn.* He smiled faintly. *And somebody has to hold it. So that the rest of you can get up.*

[break The other end]

*Who's drinking you?* thought Rei.

Watanabe was quiet for a long time.

*A demon,* he said at last. *A demon of despair. The oldest I've ever felt. It's been in the city for a long time. Hiding. It found a woman a few years ago. A good woman. A clairsentient. Kind. It settled into her the way mould settles into a wall that was always a little damp.* His voice was very soft. *It doesn't drive her. It lives in her. It wears her kindness. People go to her for comfort, and she gives them tea and listens, and they go home lighter. And they don't understand why, for weeks afterward, they can't get out of bed.*

*Kanna-sensei,* thought Rei.

Watanabe looked at him for the first time.

*You've seen her.*

*Through a window. A white room.*

*Then she's seen you.* Watanabe looked back at the window. *Be careful. It has a name. It told me its name, one night in February, very politely, through the thread. That's how sure it is.* He paused. *Kishin.*

The word went into Rei like cold water.

*It wants my chair,* said Watanabe. *Not the current. The chair. It wants to sit in the seat of Despair. A demon, in a holder's seat. That has never happened. Not once. Not in all the history the Archive has.* He folded his grey hands. *If it happens, the current will run the other way. Instead of a holder keeping despair steady on this side, a demon will be pouring it through, from the other side. Into the city. On purpose.*

*What happens then?*

*Then the veil starts to come apart,* said Watanabe simply. *And the things on the other side start coming through. Not one at a time. All at once.* He looked at the river. *And it won't stop at one chair. Something like that never wants only one chair.*

[break The voice]

The thread pulled.

Rei felt it. A long slow tug, like a fish taking a line. Watanabe's face went grey to the lips. His eyes closed. His hands, flat on the table, spread wider, gripping the wood. Across the reading room, the old men turned their newspapers and the student slept on and nobody looked.

And then Watanabe's eyes opened again.

The pupils were huge. Black. Wet. Shining. And they were not looking at the river. They were looking at Rei. Taking him in. All of him. Every breath. Every heartbeat. With a deep, soft, patient hunger, like a cat watching a bird on a branch it knows it does not have to hurry to catch.

Watanabe's mouth smiled. Not his smile. A teacher's smile. Gentle. Kind. Warm.

"*Hello, Rei,*" it said aloud.

The voice was a woman's voice. Soft and low and patient. It came out of Watanabe's thin grey face like a radio picking up the wrong station. A man in the corner looked up from his crossword, frowned, and looked down again.

"*You came all this way to see poor Jun,*" said the voice. "*That's very kind. You're a kind boy. Just like your brother.*" The smile widened, gently. "*He came to see me too, you know. In July. He sat in my white room and I gave him tea. He was so tired. So very tired. Carrying so much, all on his own.*" A pause. "*I told him he could put it down. I told him there was no point carrying it. Nothing he did would change anything. He listened, Rei. He listened for a long time.*"

"Where is he?" Rei's voice came out cracked and too loud. The student woke up. Someone said *shh*.

"*Oh, he left,*" said the voice softly. "*Before I could finish. Something came and took him. Something old, that I know very well. That owes me.*" The black eyes did not blink. "*Or that I owe. It depends how you count.*" It laughed, very gently. "*But I kept his cup. On the table. In the white room. Waiting.*"

The smile went very wide. Too wide, for a woman's smile on a man's face.

"*His tea is getting cold, Rei. Come and see me. Come and drink it for him.*"

And then it was gone. Watanabe's pupils shrank back to two small dots, and his face was his own again, grey and tired and kind, and he was gasping, his hands shaking on the table.

*I'm sorry,* he said, inside Rei's head. *I'm sorry. It does that. Sometimes. It looks out.*

Rei could not answer. He was holding the edge of the table so hard his knuckles hurt.

[break What Watanabe asked]

They sat for a long time in the quiet reading room, until Watanabe's hands stopped shaking.

*Uedera,* he said at last. *When I go. Before summer.*

*Don't.*

*When I go,* said Watanabe gently, *the current will look for someone. Someone with the shape. If there's someone near enough, it'll find them. If there isn't...* He did not finish. *It'll be drinking me when I go. Kishin. It'll be right there, at the end of the thread. And if nobody else is near enough, the current will go down the thread into it.* He looked at the river. *And it will have my chair.*

Rei understood.

*You want me to find someone. A new holder. Before you die.*

*I want you to be there,* said Watanabe. *When it happens. You and Mori and anyone else who can stand it. Between me and the thread. So that when the current comes out of me, the first thing it finds is not Kishin.* He smiled faintly. *I can't ask the others. They'll try to save me. They'll waste the time trying to save me. You won't.*

*Why won't I?*

*Because you're Duty,* said Watanabe. *You'll do what's owed. Even if it's watching.* He closed his book. He had not read a page. *That's what I'm asking. Don't save me. Stand between me and the thread.*

Rei looked at the grey fingertips on the table. At the thin papery face. At the kind tired eyes.

He thought of Gin on the twelfth of August, going out to look at something because someone had to.

*Okay,* he thought. *I promise.*

Watanabe looked at him sharply.

*Don't promise,* he said. *Not about this. Promises are for demons. They're heavy. They'll hold you.*

*I know,* thought Rei. *I'm making it anyway.*

Watanabe Jun looked at him for a long time across the library table, with the river going by outside the window and the thin grey thread pulling, pulling, from his chest.

Then he did something Rei had not seen him do. He laughed. Very quietly. A small, surprised, real laugh, like someone finding a coin in an old coat.

*Duty,* he said. *Stone all the way down.* He shook his head. *Your brother would hate this. He'd be so proud.*
