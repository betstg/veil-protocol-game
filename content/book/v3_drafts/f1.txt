#title A Small Light
#part Book One. The Archive

*The Font.*

It does not sleep the way you sleep. It has no bed and no eyes to close. But it has been still for so long that stillness is the same as sleep, and so it sleeps.

It is very large. It is not a thing that has a size. It is under the city, and under the river, and under the old red gate, and under the thirteen bright points that it holds up the way a hand holds up a lantern. It feeds them. It has always fed them. Twelve points of light, scattered across the city and beyond it, each one a living person holding one feeling open and steady. Grief in Kyoto. Rage somewhere hard to find. Love. Fear. Wonder. And the others. Twelve. It feeds them from the centre, and they keep the thin skin between the living and the dead pulled tight, like the skin of a drum.

There should be thirteen.

The thirteenth point has been dark for six years. The one called Duty. It is the heaviest of them all. When it went dark, the Font took it up itself, because someone had to, and there was no one else. It has carried it ever since. It is very tired. It does not remember a time when it was not tired. It does not remember very much at all.

It does not remember having a name.

[break Something came]

Tonight something came through.

Something small. Something that should not have been able to come so far. A little light, steady and stubborn, walking through the white toward it, with a cold thing held against its chest. And wrapped round the little light, clinging to it, a dirty hungry shape full of wanting, that had looked out at the city through a hundred rats.

The Font did not mean to burn the dirty shape. It simply did. The shape came too close to the centre, and the centre is the centre, and things that come too close to it burn. The Font barely noticed.

It noticed the little light.

The little light stood at the edge of everything, very small and very far away, and asked it a question. The Font had been asked questions before. Not often. Once every few years, someone pushes through the white, half drowned, and asks something. *Will I live. Does she love me. Where is it hidden.* The Font answers. It is one of the rules. It does not remember who made the rules. It answers, and then it pushes them back, gently, because the living cannot stay here. It does not want them to burn.

This little light asked about a brother.

The Font looked for the brother. It was not hard. The brother was very near. Very near the dark place, the place where Duty should be. Something was wearing him. Something old and courteous and patient, with a debt in its heart. The Font knew that thing. Long ago, it had known that thing very well.

*Yes,* said the Font. *He is alive.*

It put a hand on the little light's chest and pushed it home.

[break A hand]

That was the strange part.

A hand.

The Font had not had a hand for a very long time. It did not have one now. But when it pushed the little light back across the white, it pushed it with a hand. It felt the shape of it. Five fingers. A palm. The warmth of a chest under the palm, a heart beating, slow and frightened and brave.

It was a man's hand. A tired man's hand. There was ink on the side of the first finger, as if the hand had written a great deal, a long time ago, in a different kind of light.

The Font did not know whose hand it was.

It lay very still for a long time afterward, in the white, under the city, holding up its twelve lights and carrying the dark thirteenth, and thought about the hand.

Then, slowly, it went back to sleep.

But somewhere very far down, so far that even the Font could not reach it, something had turned over in its sleep. Like a person in a bed in the early morning who hears, from a long way off, a voice they used to know, calling a name that might once have been theirs.

The little light was walking toward it. The Font could feel it now. Very far away. Very small. Walking.

It did not know why, but it did not mind.
