#title The White House
#part Book Three. The Thirteen

*The Healer.*

It stood across the street from the white house for three hours, in the rain, under a clear plastic umbrella, and did not go in.

It was a quiet street on the west side of the city. Old wooden houses with small gardens. Bicycles under plastic covers. A vending machine humming at the corner. And in the middle of them, the white house. Clean and square and modern, with a white wall and a white gate and a hedge clipped so straight it looked like a ruler. White blinds in every window. Light behind the blinds, soft and warm.

The Healer knew what was inside. It had known for four hundred years.

It could feel the old thing in there. Sitting in a white room, in a kind woman's body, pouring tea. Patient. Calm. Very, very full. It had been eating for a long time. Lonely people. Sad people. People who sat on benches in Kabukichō and heard voices. It had been eating them slowly, one cup of tea at a time, until they were hollow, and then it kept them, the way a farmer keeps a field. Hollow people are useful. You can wear them from far away.

And it had been eating the man who held Despair. The Healer could feel the thread, grey and taut, going out of the white house and away east across the city. Pulling.

*Kishin.*

The Healer did not say the name aloud. It did not need to. They had known each other's names for four centuries.

[break The debt]

The body was cold. The Healer had forgotten, standing still so long, that bodies get cold. It shifted its feet. It moved the umbrella to the other hand. Inside, very deep, the man stirred, the way he did more and more often now. Not fighting. Watching. Since the night of the two cups, he had stopped fighting. He watched instead. The Healer had not decided yet whether that was better or worse.

*You could go in,* said the man, very faintly. *Now. While it's sitting there. You've been waiting four hundred years.*

*It is too strong,* said the Healer. *In there, in its own house, with its herd around it, it is much too strong. I would lose. And then your body would be in its house. With it.* A pause. *I do not wish that.*

*Since when do you care what happens to my body?*

The Healer did not answer that.

It watched the white house. The blinds. The warm light.

*It knows about your brother,* it said at last.

It felt the man go very still.

*It looked out of the Despair holder's eyes in a library in Kawaguchi. It saw him. It spoke to him.* The Healer's voice was very quiet. *It told him to come and drink your tea.*

*No.*

*It will want him. Not to eat. To empty. Your brother is the shape of the dark chair. More than you. More than anyone in four hundred years. If the old thing sits in the chair of Despair, and your brother is hollow in its white room, then there is no one to sit in the chair of Duty. Ever. And the Font, carrying both, will break.* A pause. *And the far side will pour into the near. All of it. At once.*

The man did not answer. The Healer felt something come up from the bottom of the body. Not fear. Something harder. Something that felt, from the inside, very much like the thing the Healer itself was made of.

*Then stop it,* said the man.

[break The hollow one]

A woman came out of the white house.

She was about fifty, in a cheap raincoat, with a plastic bag of shopping. She walked out of the white gate and down the street toward the station, under the rain, without an umbrella. She walked very straight. Her eyes, when she passed under the street lamp, were huge and black, and they moved, slowly, taking in the street, the houses, the bicycles, the vending machine.

They stopped on the Healer.

She stopped walking. She stood in the rain, ten metres away, and looked at it with the old thing's eyes. And the Healer felt the old thing look out of her. Recognise it. Through the borrowed face.

The woman smiled. Kishin's smile. Soft and warm and kind.

"*Oh,*" said the woman, in a gentle voice that was not hers. "*It's you. After all this time. And wearing such a nice coat.*"

The Healer said nothing.

"*Come in,*" said the woman softly. "*Come in and have tea. We can talk about the old days. About what's owed. I'll even pour you a cup.*" The smile widened. "*I'll pour him one too. The man inside you. I kept his cup. On the table. He sat in my white room in July and drank half of it before you took him away from me. I've been keeping the other half.*"

Something happened in the Healer then.

It did not decide it. It did not think. It was a demon, and demons do not think. They want, and they move. And in that second, standing in the rain, it wanted, more than it had wanted anything in four hundred years.

It crossed the ten metres in one step.

It put its hand, Gin's hand, flat on the woman's chest, over the grey thread that ran out of her, back toward the white house.

And it closed its fist.

The thread snapped. The Healer felt it go, like a rope cut under tension, whipping back toward the white house into the dark. The woman's eyes went small and brown and ordinary. Her face went slack. She swayed. The Healer caught her by the elbow before she fell.

She looked up at it. A tall man with long black hair, holding an umbrella over her, in the rain. Her face was full of confusion and fear.

"Where," she said. "Where am I. I was having tea. I was having tea with a lady..."

"You are on a street," said the Healer. "It is raining. Go home."

It put the umbrella in her hand. It closed her fingers round the handle. It turned her by the shoulders and pointed her toward the station, very gently, the way you point a child.

"Go home," it said again. "Do not come back here."

The woman walked away down the wet street, holding the clear umbrella, looking back over her shoulder twice.

[break What it did]

The Healer stood in the rain without the umbrella and let it fall on Gin's face. On his long black hair. On his coat.

In the white house, behind the blinds, the warm light had gone out.

*Why did you do that?* said the man, very quietly.

*I do not know.* The Healer looked at the dark house. *There was no debt. Nothing was owed. She was nothing to me.* It looked down at its hand. At Gin's hand. Still closed in a fist. *I wanted to. So I did.*

*That's not a reason.*

*It is the only reason a thing like me has ever had.* The Healer opened the hand slowly. *Until now, it always wanted to take.*

The rain ran down its face. Down the man's face. Into the corners of his mouth.

*It knows I am here now,* said the Healer. *It knows I am close. It will move. It will hurry.* It turned away from the white house and began to walk, in the rain, without an umbrella. *I cannot fight it alone. Not in its house. Not with its herd.*

*Then don't fight it alone,* said the man.

The Healer kept walking.

*Ask,* said the man. *You said it didn't occur to you. It's occurring to you now. I can feel it.*

The Healer did not answer for a long time. It walked to the station. It touched the card to the gate, correctly, the first time, and the gate opened, and it went through. It stood on the platform among the late-night people, wet through, and looked at the trains.

*I have never asked anyone for anything,* it said at last.

*I know,* said the man. *It's not as hard as it looks. You just have to find the right door and knock.*

A train came in. Silver, with a sky-blue stripe down its side.

The Healer looked at the stripe for a long time. Then it got on.
