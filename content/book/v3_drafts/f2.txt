#title The Light Comes Closer
#part Book Two. The Families

*The Font.*

The little light is closer now.

The Font can feel it the way you feel the sun move across a room while you sleep. Warmer on one side. It has not come through the white again since the night by the river. But it is nearer. It walks in the city above, small and steady, and every day it is a little brighter.

It has things round it now. Other lights. Small ones. One that opens doors and has a cold sleeping thing curled in its back, locked in behind twenty-four little gates. One that hears everything and is lonely, and is a little less lonely than before. One very old, very patient, in a garden gone to seed, that the Font has felt from far away for forty years. One with a dog. One with three notebooks. A crow.

The Font does not know why it notices them. It has never noticed the lights round any one person before. It holds up twelve great lights across the city. It carries the dark thirteenth. It does not look at small ones.

It looks at these.

[break The chair]

There is a chair in the dark.

That is the only way the Font can think of it. The thirteenth place, the one called Duty, the one that went dark six years ago. It is not really a chair. It is a shape in the veil, a hollow, the size and shape of a person who should be there and is not. The Font has been sitting in it. Holding it. Carrying its weight on top of everything else, the way a tired man carries a sleeping child on his shoulders for miles because there is no one else to carry it.

The little light is the right shape for the chair.

The Font understands this suddenly, without wanting to, the way you understand a word in a dream. The little light is exactly the right shape. The same shape as the courteous old thing that is wearing the brother. The same shape as the brother. Two brothers, and a demon, and a chair. All the same shape.

Duty.

It does not know what this means. It does not want to know. It is too tired to know things.

[break A name]

But something else is happening, deep down, under the Font, in a place it did not know it had.

It is remembering.

Not much. Not clearly. Flashes, like lights seen from a train at night. A room with paper walls and a low desk. A brush in a hand, and ink, and a page of very small careful writing. An evening, a long time ago, orange and gold, at the hour when the light goes strange and the day turns into night. People called that hour something. A special name. The meeting hour. The hour when demons walk.

*Ōma-ga-toki.*

The Font does not know where the word comes from. It turns it over and over, like a stone in a stream.

And with the word, for one moment, a feeling. Not a memory. A feeling. Of walking down a road at that orange hour, in a long coat, with a notebook in the pocket and a job to do, and of being tired, and of being glad to be tired, because the work was good, and someone was waiting at home with tea.

Then it is gone.

The Font lies very still in the white for a long time after that.

It has twelve lights to hold. It has the dark thirteenth to carry. It has no name, and no hands, and no road, and nobody waiting with tea. It has not had any of those things for four hundred years. It is the Font. It is the light at the centre. That is all it is.

But somewhere, up in the city, the small bright light is walking toward it with a crow and a dog and a boy who paints doors, and the Font finds, to its surprise, that it is waiting.

It has not waited for anything in a very long time.

It does not mind.
