Systems & Progression

Working spec · v0.1 · the simulation under the book

reactive social-simclock-drivengated pathsagent NPCs

Seven interlocking systems turn the interactive book into a living world: a clock, deadlines, a dependency graph of paths, relationships & romance, reputation & gossip, NPC agents, and channels. Design assumes the full vision; the build fakes the costly parts with rules first.

AThe Clock — Day + Hour

The campaign runs on Day N · time-of-day. Time is the real pressure (and, per the GM notes, it is felt, not announced).

  • Blocks. Each day = Morning · Afternoon · Evening · Night. Actions and travel spend blocks.
  • Travel costs time, and it shifts. Walking vs train differ; and both differ by time of day — morning/evening rush = frequent but crowded trains; midday = slower; night = sparse trains, taxi or wait. Each place's from-Ōji cost (already in homes-distances) gets a time-of-day modifier.
  • Actions cost blocks. A meeting, a scene-read, a training session, a stakeout — each consumes time, so every day forces choices about what you don't do.
  • The world advances on the clock even when you rest — NPCs move on their routines, the possession deepens.
  • It is a real 2015/2016 calendar. The clock is anchored to a fixed real date — the day Gin was abducted — and the campaign opens three days later, with Rei in the apartment. We use a real date because opening hours vary by day of the week, so the engine must know it's a Tuesday vs. a Sunday. (Open decision: the book says only "Tokyo, 2015" — we pick the canonical abduction date.)

CThe Dependency Graph — the paths

You can't take a path until you've earned its prerequisites. Multiple OR-routes reach the same gate — that's the replayability and the "all the paths."

The routes below are examples, not a closed list. The world should be able to open a path we never tabled — a chance encounter, a case lead, a renegade contact, a favour called in. Treat the graph as the known doors, and leave room for the engine (and the player's choices) to cut new ones. "Befriend a Tower family," for instance, doesn't have to mean the front door: it can mean going through a black-sheep son who lives apart from the house.

DRelationships & Romance

Every significant NPC holds a stance toward you (hostile → wary → neutral → warm → loyal) and a trust value that gates access, information, protection — or betrayal. Romance is a layer on top, with its own states:

  • Public vs secret. A romance carries a known? flag. Secret affairs can be exposed by gossip (system E) → jealousy, leverage, broken trust elsewhere.
  • What it causes. Romance ≠ just flavor: it changes access, loyalty under pressure, who protects you, who turns on you, and which endings open. Rejecting someone has costs too.
  • Gated. Some NPCs only become romanceable past a trust threshold or after you've visited their place (the avatar-unlock).

EReputation & Gossip

  • Contextual reputation. You hold separate standings with factions (CVD, Grid, Towers, the street) and with individuals. Being trusted by the Grid can cost you with the CVD.
  • Propagation. NPCs talk. If you're known to associate with a bad-reputation person, the next person you meet may greet you accordingly — your social record arrives before you do.
  • Seeded by veil-protocol-relationships.html — who already knows whom and how they're regarded.

FNPC Agents — the living world

Each significant NPC is modelled as an agent: their sheet, their goals, their schedule (straight out of npc-routines), their relationships to you and to each other, and what they currently know. They act off-screen — move on their routine, meet, talk, share or withhold, and send messages, some of which you never see, that still change the world.

⚠ THE EXPENSIVE PART — BUILD IT TIERED

Live AI agents conversing in the background is the most magical and the most costly/unpredictable idea here. So we tier it:

  • Tier 1 (build first): scripted simulation — scheduled movements, rule-based gossip, flag changes, off-screen "messages" resolved as state updates. No live AI. Feels alive, costs nothing, never breaks canon.
  • Tier 2: lightweight AI for the gossip/flavor text only, on top of the scripted state.
  • Tier 3 (far): true live multi-agent chatter. Powerful, but only once the rest is solid and the cost is justified.

You already built the engine's data

npc-routines = the schedules (clock + agents) · homes-distances = the travel-time engine (system A) · relationships = the seed social graph (D & E). Formalizing these systems is mostly wiring data you already have, not inventing from zero.

HPlaces — Stations, Weekday Hours, Access

Travel runs through stations

  • You don't teleport to a place — you go to its nearest station, then walk (or a short hop) the last stretch. Every place carries nearestStation + walkFromStation.
  • Each station holds a list of the places around it — arriving at a station shows you what's within reach. Ōji is the home hub; spoke→spoke trips route through it (times add), exactly as the engine prototype already does.
  • Art needed: an image per station used, plus a train-scene (the travelling-between moment). Logged in the Game Book pending list.

Opening hours are per weekday + hour

  • A place isn't simply "open 9–17" — it's open by day of week and hour (the shrine on a festival Sunday, the bar closed Mondays, the police desk thin on weekends). This is why the clock is a real calendar (system A).
  • Data shape: hours: { mon:[9,17], ..., sun:null }. Arrive outside the window → a dark door, time still spent.

Access is gated — you can't go everywhere

  • Rank-gated rooms. CVD rooms check your rank/clearance — the vaults, the containment floor, the masters' level are closed until you've earned them. (Maps onto the CVD room set already in the book.)
  • Private space. You can't walk into anyone's home — entry needs an invitation / trust threshold / relationship. Same for Grid safehouses (Grid trust), Tower property (family standing).
  • Data shape: each place/room carries an access rule — public, rank≥X, member:CVD/Grid/Tower, or trust:npc≥N — checked before any entry.

IThe Investigation Layer

Rei is a clairsentient reading crime scenes — so the game is, mechanically, a detective game. Six surfaces (drawn from Sir Brante's consequence, Disco Elysium's reactivity, and modern detective UIs) carry it. All sit inside the oil-noir / 2015-archive skin.

  • Evidence Board. A corkboard of photos, documents and entities with string connections (person ↔ clue ↔ location). The player draws links; correct ones unlock deductions. (Build: React Flow.)
  • Journal. A persistent, auto-logged diary of discoveries, plus space for the player's own handwritten-style notes.
  • Dossier. A file per person: portrait, known facts, suspected secrets, and the full interview-transcript history. The avatar fills in once you've been to their place.
  • AI Interrogation. A per-NPC chat where the AI plays the character strictly in role — revealing only what that person knows and would choose to share, gated by trust/flags, returning to the spine at defined exits. (This is the system §F free-text talk, focused on interrogation.)
  • Case File cover. A folder metaphor that organizes everything per case — evidence, persons, notes, map pins — so multiple investigations stay separate.
  • The Map. See below.

The Map — real Tokyo, our own distances

Question on the table: a Google-Maps-style real render of Tokyo with place pins and foot/train distances. Feasible — two ways, and a clear recommendation:

  • Recommended — stylized diegetic map. A hand-styled Tokyo (oil-noir / archive paper) with pins for known places, locations unlocking as the investigation progresses, and our own authored foot/train times (already in homes-distances, Ōji as hub). Works offline, matches the book's identity, no external dependency. The detective references you sent all use stylized maps, not live Google Maps.
  • Possible — real map tiles. Leaflet + OpenStreetMap with true lat/long pins. More "real," but needs to be online, clashes with the painterly skin, and true door-to-door foot/train timing would need a routing API. Since we already track travel times ourselves, the real engine doesn't need live routing — so this is a look-and-feel choice, not a mechanical need.
  • Either way: the map is a view on the same location/station data the engine already holds — pins and routes are drawn from it, so we can swap the skin later without touching the engine.

JChoices, the GM-AI, and finding people NEW

Abilities are locked until trained

  • A path you haven't unlocked shows on the wheel as locked — not buyable, not grindable. It opens only through a training method: a recognition event, or a willing teacher of that path (Suppression training, a master, a Tower tutor). You can see the locked door; you cannot pick it.
  • Once open, it grows by use (per the progression spec). So the gate is training; the growth is doing.

Choices are real choices

  • Two or more, almost always. A single "continue" is narration, not a choice — reserve it for pure connective beats. Decision beats always branch.
  • Plus a free-text option. Every meaningful beat also offers "do something else — type it." The player writes what they want to do, and the GM-AI responds in-world.
  • The book never goes off the rails. The GM-AI's job is not to invent a new story — it's to take whatever the player typed, honour it in the fiction, and steer them toward one of the authored next nodes. Freedom of expression, bounded destinations. (Needs the AI runtime; until then the free-text option gives a written acknowledgement and nudges back to the listed choices.)

NPCs are agents, hidden on the map

  • Every character is an AI agent with a schedule (system F) and a place on the map — but hidden. You do not see them as pins. You find a person by going where they are. Travel to a place at a time they're scheduled there, and they're there; the avatar unlocks (the "face on arrival" hook).
  • Asking for a number. When you talk to someone, one of the choices (when it fits) is to ask for their phone. Say yes and they're added to your Contacts — now reachable by SMS/call without travelling. Relationship-gated: a wary contact won't give it.

Combat & encounters

You rarely kill a bodiless thing — you survive it, banish it, or take it in. A round: you act — Strike (Body + Combat + d6) · Power (path×3 + d6, costs Stability + thins the Veil) · Exorcise (Resolve + Symbol Work + d6 vs the entity's DC, once worn down — and it gives a clue) · Absorb (when it's low → its power, +Veil +Madness, the villain road) · Flee (always). Then it acts — draining Stability, overflowing into Health when your nerve breaks, raising Madness. You lose by Health → 0 (down) or Madness → 100 (possession).

  • Two dials: Type (spirit / demon / idea — what it wants) × Grade (E → top — how hard it hits). A spirit can be top-grade; a demon is a spirit that fed and climbed.
  • Met at the grade of its place, not the player. The world is open; the soft gate is what lives there. Go somewhere ahead of the story and the entities are simply stronger — flee, and the story remembers what you saw.
  • Zone tiers I–V: Ordinary ground → the edges → the works → the inner doors → the thin places. Each place carries a tier; the engine draws an entity whose tier fits (bumped up when the local veil is thin).
  • Survival by rank: Postulate/Adept handle E; Arbiter up to B; Vanguard takes A; only Singularity/Guardian contest top-grade. Full table in the Warden's book.

The database veil-data/

Single source of truth, separate from presentation. JSON node-tables linked into a network: characters · places · factions · thirteen · ranks · entities. Each node has an id and links (member_of, lives_at, knows ±100, rides, …). The game loads it at startup; combat already reads enemies from entities.json. Scope discipline

Design the whole simulation (this doc). Build it as thin vertical slices, faking the expensive layers with rules until they earn their cost. Order I'd suggest: clock (+ real calendar) → station travel → weekday hours → access gates → goals/deadlines → dependency gates → relationships → reputation → scripted agents → channels → (later) live AI.
